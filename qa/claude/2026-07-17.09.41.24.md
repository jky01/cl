## Reframed problem and the pivot

Compact-mechanism selection here is a **discrete support-search** problem: among exponentially many
in-range-equivalent dense fits, find the sparse-input one. Continuous gate relaxation on dense `A,B`
has no gradient pressure toward the sparse solution once ID loss is ~0. Options:

1. **Explicit support search + refit** (what the diagnostic effectively did): enumerate/beam-search
   candidate input-supports for each operator, fit coefficients, score by ID-loss + exact structural
   cost, keep the min. Deterministic, and it provably found x*x. Scales combinatorially, so it needs a
   greedy/beam or a restricted operator budget — but it's the clean "does a search-based selector give
   retention+extrapolation+recovery" test.
2. **Fix the gate optimizer**: sparse-input pressure directly on `A,B` (group-L0 per input-coordinate,
   not just per-unit), plus annealing / support-restart / proximal steps, so the continuous method can
   still reach the sparse point. This keeps it differentiable (better for the eventual LM) but may
   inherit the same non-convex trap.

I lean building #1 first as the existence proof (search-based selection recovers x*x, extrapolates,
and — through the continual protocol — retains parity), because it cleanly separates "is a selection
principle sufficient" from "can a *differentiable* one be made to work". Then #2 is the
scalability/LM-portability question.
