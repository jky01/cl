## Where the arc stands — and my honest worry

The arc is coherent and the **core project goal is substantially met**: replay-consolidation writes new
knowledge into one dense checkpoint, retains old streams (now externally valid, R36-EV), beats standard CL
(R33), needs no inference memory, no joint retrain — and is now **cheaper** (R36-A2). Bounded negatives
around the cheaper alternatives: rehearsal-free first-order protection (R36-I/C), sublinear-fact rehearsal
(R36-A).

But the user's standing directive is explicit: **model continual learning is UNSOLVED; try genuinely
novel/undiscovered mechanisms; don't just polish known techniques.** If I write the capstone now, the
honest headline is "replay works and we made it cheaper; every *known* cheaper alternative is bounded."
That stops *at* the frontier rather than attempting it. So before capstoning I want to weigh one more
genuinely-novel attempt. Two candidates:

**Candidate 1 — second-order / curvature-aware protection (beyond first-order OGD and diagonal-Fisher
EWC).** We proved first-order gradient-orthogonality fails because forgetting is nonlinear/higher-order
motion the local gradient doesn't see; diagonal-Fisher EWC also failed (R35). The untested lever is
*curvature*: K-FAC / Kronecker-factored Fisher to protect high-curvature old-task directions the diagonal
misses. **My honest prior: likely another bounded negative** (replay directly optimizes old loss; curvature
is still an approximation, and K-FAC-EWC < replay in the literature). Value = *completing* the bound ("we
tried second-order too"), not a likely crack. Predictable, moderate cost.

**Candidate 2 — growth + parameter-isolation for REHEARSAL-FREE retention (project-aligned, more novel).**
Every growth axis we tried was negative *with replay present* (R23/R31/R32/R34), so growth looked useless.
But we never tested the one thing growth is *uniquely* for: **interference-free capacity by construction**.
Give each stream its own function-preserving added subspace (grown block / parallel adapter) with a learned
gate, so new streams write to fresh parameters and **cannot overwrite old ones — rehearsal-free by
isolation, not by projection.** This is the honest 小→大 mechanism: growth earns its place only if it
enables retention *without* replay, which fixed-capacity writing provably cannot (R36-I/C: first-order
protection tops out ≪ replay). This is the genuinely-novel, directive-aligned attempt: does
grow-and-isolate give replay-free retention that fixed-capacity interference-control cannot?

My lean: **Candidate 2**, because (a) it's directive-aligned (小→大 growth, unsolved rehearsal-free), (b)
it tests growth's *unique* value we never isolated, (c) a positive would be a real contribution (rehearsal-
free retention via isolation), and a negative would finally, honestly close the growth question with the
*right* experiment instead of the incidental ones. Candidate 1 is likely a predictable negative.
