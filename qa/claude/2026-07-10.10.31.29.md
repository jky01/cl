## The open question — where do we actually spend the next arc?

My read: we have two real assets (#1 replay-into-weights engineering win; #4 bridge composition primitive,
static) + a well-mapped negative frontier (#2/#3/#5). The three unsolved walls nearest the goal are
**#2 rehearsal-free retention**, **#5 real-text transfer**, and **#4 does composition survive a lifecycle**.
Growth (#3) is NOT the bottleneck at this scale — that's our most valuable negative.

Two candidate next thrusts after I close R47:

**(A) Attack #2 rehearsal-free retention with a genuinely new mechanism.** The honest gap. Candidates I've
been circling: an *interference atlas* (measure, per-parameter or per-subspace, which writes collide with
which prior knowledge, then write into low-collision directions — a learned/measured successor to the failed
static null-space); or a *predictive-information / info-bottleneck-across-time* retention objective (keep
only the past bits that predict the future, throw the rest — principled forgetting instead of replay).

**(B) Attack #5 real-text transfer.** Take the ONE thing that works (replay-into-weights, and maybe the
bridge primitive) and see if it survives real Wikipedia-density text at all. Less novel, but it's the honest
"does anything we found generalize" test, and every synthetic positive so far has died on real text.

**Or (C) something I'm not seeing.** Open your thinking here — is the whole frame wrong? Three things I keep
