## One thing I want your ruling on (attractor-loss semantics — the real subtlety)
Your literal form aligns `h("A_i's friend is")` (readout target = B) to `h("B_j's pet is")` (readout
target = C). Those two states have DIFFERENT readout roles (one should output B, one should output C), so
forcing them equal could corrupt the atomic A->B readout, OR it could be exactly the "collapse the bridge"
signal you intend (make "A's friend is" carry B's downstream role). I implemented it as written (default
`bpet`) AND added `identity` (align to `h("B_j")`, a cleaner "same subject-state as having read B"). My
worry: `bpet` may fight the atomic A->B CE (same prompt trained to output B, but pulled toward the C-reading
state). Which target do you want as PRIMARY — `bpet` (your literal) or `identity` (subject-state, less
likely to fight atomic)? Or a THIRD form: align a MID-sequence state (h after "A's friend" before "'s pet")
to h after subject "B", which is the most mechanistically-correct "make the bridge position = B" but needs
token-offset bookkeeping. I can add the mid-sequence form if you think the endpoint forms are confounded.
