# Deep discussion: continual learning is unsolved — where our own result stops, and what novel mechanism might actually move it

## 1. Where our strongest result actually stops (honest diagnosis)

R33 is real, but let's be blunt about what it is and isn't:

- ours = replay-consolidation. The retention comes from **self-distilling prior streams to a
  pre-round snapshot**. That is **rehearsal**. We regenerate old-stream *inputs* and match a snapshot's
  outputs. No-gold ≠ no-rehearsal — we still replay the old input distribution.
- R23/R31/R32/R34 all quietly *depend on replay* to look clean. Replay is a crutch that **masks the
  real enemy: interference.** When we forbid replay (naive/continued/loramerge/ewc), everything
  forgets with an age gradient. That's the actual unsolved problem showing through.

So the honest statement is: **we have a strong rehearsal-based continual-consolidation loop; we have
NOT solved rehearsal-free continual learning.** The field hasn't either. That's the frontier.

## 2. The reframing I want to test: interference, not capacity, is the bottleneck — and it may be the FIRST real justification for growth

Every growth probe was negative *because replay papered over interference*. R31's "1 layer holds 800
facts" is true only *with replay*. The quantity that actually saturates is not storage but the
**interference-free subspace**: the number of directions in weight/activation space into which you can
write a new fact **without rotating the representations that other facts read from**.

Conjecture: **if you write knowledge rehearsal-free and interference-aware, the model saturates its
interference-free capacity long before its storage capacity — and THAT saturation is the first
principled, measurable trigger where function-preserving growth becomes genuinely necessary** (growth
adds fresh orthogonal directions). This would reconcile the whole arc: growth looked useless because
replay hid interference; remove replay + write interference-aware, and growth gets a real job.

This is the bet I find most promising and most "undiscovered": not a new regularizer, but a different
**write primitive** + a **principled growth trigger**.

## 3. Concrete novel directions (components exist separately; the novelty is the synthesis + the trigger)

I'll be honest about prior art and mark what's genuinely new.

**(A) Interference-aware localized writing (rehearsal-free by construction).**
Instead of gradient descent on the new stream (which rotates shared features), write each new fact via
an update constrained to the **null space of the directions old facts read from**. Maintain a running
low-rank basis `U` of "occupied" read-directions (from the keys/activations of consolidated facts).
New writes are projected to `I - UU^T`. Old readouts are provably ~unchanged → no rehearsal needed.
Prior art: Adam-NSCL / OGD / null-space CL exist for task-incremental image nets; **under-explored for
factual knowledge in a pretrained LM, and never coupled to a growth trigger.** Novel part: use the
scaffold's *keys* (we already compute proj_k over facts) as the occupied basis, and grow exactly when
`rank(U)` approaches the writable dimension.

**(B) Closed-form sequential editing as the write op (ROME/MEMIT-style) + verify-consolidate loop.**
Replace fine-tuning with rank-one/rank-k closed-form weight edits that insert a fact while minimizing
collateral (least-squares under a "preserve these old keys" constraint). We already have the scaffold
to *verify* an edit's answer-recall before committing (answer-recall guard). Sequential model editing
is known to degrade after many edits — the open question is whether **our verify-guard + an
interference-constrained edit** pushes the number of non-destructive sequential edits far past current
limits. Novel part: guarded, verification-gated sequential editing with a saturation-triggered growth.

**(C) Meta-learned interference-free write operator (the Ω controller, re-aimed).**
We shelved Ω as a *growth* controller. Re-aim it as a **write-rule learner**: meta-train a small
hypernetwork that maps (new fact, current weights) → a weight delta that is optimized to (i) install
the fact and (ii) leave a held-out probe of prior facts unchanged — i.e., meta-learn to *not
interfere*. This attacks forgetting at its root (the update direction) rather than mopping up with
replay. Genuinely under-explored for LM knowledge.

**(D) Generative self-rehearsal (rehearsal-free-ish).**
Our replay uses real old inputs. A model that **generates its own old-knowledge probes** (dreams up
"X's <attr> is" prompts it has consolidated) would be replay without a stored buffer. Known for images
(DGR); shaky for precise facts. Novel part: use the scaffold as the generator of self-rehearsal
targets during consolidation, then discard it.

## 4. How to make the frontier *visible* (so novel methods can win where replay can't)

The reason growth/novelty looked unnecessary is our benchmark *allowed replay*. Propose a
**rehearsal-forbidden continual benchmark**: no old-stream inputs may be reconstructed or replayed
during a new stream (enforced structurally, like our leak contracts). Metrics: rehearsal-free
oldest-stream retention, and — crucially — an **interference-saturation curve**: as streams accumulate,
track the writable-subspace occupancy and the point where rehearsal-free writing starts destroying old
facts. Growth's value is then tested as: *does adding capacity at the saturation point restore
rehearsal-free retention that a fixed model cannot?* That is the clean, novel growth-necessity test we
never actually ran (all prior ones had replay on).
