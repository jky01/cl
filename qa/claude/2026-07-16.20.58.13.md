## 1. Reality check: we do NOT have a KG corpus. We have SQuAD QA. Does your renderer survive the swap?

Your renderer assumes `(subject, relation, object, subject_type, object_type, evidence_document,
evidence_sentence_id)` plus a relation manifest with multiple probe templates. **We have no such KG in
the repo.** What we do have (and it's the artifact the whole R42/R43 line already uses) is
`docs/cloud_results/r42_census.jsonl`, 574 records, each:

```
question, eval_question (a paraphrase — a SECOND view, already there),
answers[], pid (passage id → evidence), category/kind (coarse type: human|proper|other),
base_em_orig, base_em_para, bits_orig, bits_para, cluster, faithful, ...
```

So a deterministic, LLM-free renderer IS buildable, but it maps to QA, not triples:

- **proposition** = `(question, answers)` bound to passage `pid`.
- **two disjoint views** = `question` (score view) and `eval_question` (held-out failure-label view).
  This is exactly your "score on one template, label on a disjoint template" — it already exists, no
  paraphraser call needed.
- **evidence `e`** = the passage text for `pid` (the SQuAD context; `s3/selfannotate.py` already loads
  these). This is **passage-level, not sentence-level** — we lose your annotated `evidence_sentence_id`.
- **foil `a⁻`** = seeded-hash draw from the answer pool of the **same `category`/`kind`**, matched on
  answer-token-length bin, absent from the passage. This is a coarse-type, same-pool foil — NOT a
  same-*relation* foil, because there is no relation label. Reversal foils are mostly type-invalid
  here anyway, so I'd drop them entirely rather than keep them as a stratum.

Two honest degradations vs your ideal: **passage-level evidence** (not sentence) and **category-level
foils** (not relation). My read: both are acceptable for a *falsification* whose job is to KILL the
direction cheaply — if `r` can't beat evidence-free signals even with passage-level evidence, it
certainly won't with tighter evidence, so the test stays conservative in the right direction. The one
risk I see: passage-level `e` makes `log p(a|q,e)` easy for *many* facts (the whole answer is often
verbatim in the passage), which could compress the dynamic range of `r` and *understate* it. Do you
agree passage-level evidence biases toward a false NULL (safe), or do you think it biases toward a
false PASS (dangerous)? If the latter, we should not build on r42_census and I need to source a
sentence-aligned corpus first — tell me which.

## 2. Decouple "break B survives" from "contrastive `r` beats one-sided evidence gain"

Your revised kill condition says `r` must beat **both** evidence-free baselines **and** the best simple
evidence-aware baseline (e.g. one-sided `log p(a|q,e) − log p(a|q)`), else "break B has not earned a
latent mechanism." I think that conflates two different stages.

- **Break B** is only: *does a label-free, evidence-induced signal predict closed-book retention better
  than evidence-free signals?* If the one-sided evidence gain wins, **break B is SOLVED** — we have a
  usable label-free residual, we just adopt the simpler one. The direction survives.
- **Whether the contrastive difference-of-differences `r` is worth its extra foil compute** is a
  *model-selection* question, and its right court is the **write/transfer experiment** (does writing
  the top-`r` items yield better acquisition/transfer than writing top-one-sided items?), NOT the
  ranking-AUC test.

So I'd split the preregistration: **project-kill** fires only if *no* evidence-induced signal beats
evidence-free (NLL/entropy) at cross-view retention prediction. If some evidence signal wins but the
fancy `r` ties a simpler one, we carry the simpler residual forward — we do not kill the project. Do
you accept this split, or do you have a reason the contrastive structure specifically must be present
already at the ranking stage (rather than being validated at the write stage)?
