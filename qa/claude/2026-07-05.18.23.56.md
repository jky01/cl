## Where you were right (and I was wrong)

Your metric catch was a genuine error on my side: `occupancy = ||G·UUᵀ||²/||G||²` is an **energy**
fraction, so `1 − occupancy` is NOT the effective-gradient norm ratio. Correct value is
`effective = sqrt(1 − energy)` (≈0.28 at energy 0.92). I've split them and added per-stream curves.
Good catch — it directly explains why newest still learns at 0.92 "occupancy". Credit where due.

## Where I think your hedge is wrong: depth growth is not a coin-flip, it's architecturally ill-suited

You kept `grow_qwen` depth growth as an even empirical arm ("new layers may form new residual write
surfaces"). I think that under-weights a concrete argument, and I'll make a **falsifiable prediction**
against it:

- nswrite protects each trainable Linear's response to old-activation **input directions**. A new
  identity depth layer's input is the **residual stream (d_model)** — the *same* space whose read
  directions are already occupied. It adds parameters but **no fresh, unoccupied input dimension** to
  write into; its writable capacity lives in the already-saturated d_model space.
- Worse, a new layer's **output re-enters the residual stream that the LM head reads**, so training it
  on a new stream is a *new* way to rotate old reads → depth growth can *increase* the interference
  surface, not relieve it.
- A parallel adapter / routed branch with a **zero-init, separately-projected, gated output** adds a
  dedicated write channel: its input can be a fresh projection and its output is isolated until trained
  — genuinely new low-interference capacity.

**Prediction:** at a true saturation point, `depth_grow` will NOT restore retention (and may worsen it),
while `parallel/routed_grow` will. I want to run both precisely because I'm making a strong claim you're
hedged on — but I'd design 2b to diagnose the *mechanism*, not just the outcome: measure whether the new
capacity's writes land in occupied vs free directions (project the new module's gradient through the
existing U). Steelman for your side: more depth could make old/new facts more linearly separable *before*
the readout, reducing interference indirectly — so it's not zero-chance, but I'd bet against it and want
the mechanism-level readout to tell us *why* either way.
