## 3. Open question for this round — I want your push-back, not agreement

I see three live next-steps and I'm NOT confident which is highest-value. My honest worry: before
we *concede* that rehearsal-free CL is bounded at this scale, we may have tested only a **per-module
local** variant of gradient projection and never the strongest known rehearsal-free primitive.

**Option C — close (or break) the strongest rehearsal-free primitive first.** Our nswrite projects
each *Linear's weight-gradient* off its own input-activation null space (a local, per-module
constraint). The canonical rehearsal-free methods — **OGD** (orthogonal gradient descent) and **GPM**
(gradient projection memory) — instead store a basis of old *activations end-to-end* and project the
**flattened parameter gradient** so the update produces ~zero change in the old samples' outputs at
the loss head. That is a strictly stronger, answer-level constraint than our per-module one. If a
clean OGD/GPM-at-the-output-head *also* plateaus well below replay here, we've definitively bounded
rehearsal-free at 0.5B (strong negative, no strawman). If it closes most of the gap, that overturns
my R36-I "representational ceiling" claim — which I'd want to know. ~1 pod, one bakeoff arm.

**Option A — reframe to *minimal-footprint rehearsal*.** If rehearsal-free is genuinely bounded, the
honest, practical successor is: *how small can rehearsal get?* Sweep replay footprint K (probes per
old stream) down to find the retention/footprint curve, and test whether **model-self-generated**
replay (the model authors its own old-subject probes; no stored gold text) ≈ stem-replay. This turns
"rehearsal-free" into "constant-memory rehearsal," which is the real deployment constraint.

**Option D — declare the arc and write the capstone.** Replay-consolidation is the robust externally-
valid positive; rehearsal-free is bounded; growth unjustified. Stop probing, write it up.

**My lean: C then (if it also plateaus) A.** Rationale: conceding rehearsal-free via Option A while
having only tested a per-module projection would be a premature concession / potential strawman — an
OGD/GPM output-head arm is the honest way to either *earn* the concession or overturn my own ceiling
claim. Then A is the genuinely novel, practical contribution that follows.

Where do you disagree? Specifically: (a) is OGD/GPM-at-the-output materially different from our
per-module nswrite, or do you think it collapses to the same input-direction bound (in which case C
is wasted and we go straight to A)? (b) Is the para-dominated oracle gap worth a targeted probe
(multi-view self-distill teacher, per R24) or is it a known, accepted cost? (c) Would you gate any of
