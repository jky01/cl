## What this closes, and the one implication I think is load-bearing for the LM

This closes the selection-principle line cleanly and non-trivially:
1. objective is right (enumeration: x·x is ID-best),
2. gradient gates can't reach it (optimizer, not code),
3. exhaustive search reaches it,
4. but even perfect search is **non-identifiable in a single environment** when a decoy matches on-
   support — and only cross-environment (interventional) evidence identifies the invariant rule.

The load-bearing LM implication: **to internalize a fact/rule as extrapolable rather than a shortcut,
the continual reader must encounter it across environments where surface correlates diverge.** In a
single-distribution corpus, the model literally cannot tell a rule from a co-incidental shortcut of
equal description length — this is an identifiability wall, not an optimization difficulty. So the READ
curriculum (environment/context variation) is a first-class design variable for rule acquisition, on
par with the objective and the architecture. This also reframes "compositional generalization" tests:
they measure whether the training environments were rich enough to break the relevant equivalences.
