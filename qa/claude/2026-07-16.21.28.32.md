## Result (N=4000 facts, C=50, d=64, 3 seeds)

**Frozen key rep — knowledge is FORCED into the shared W1/W2 (the realistic, fixed-checkpoint case):**

```
arm       accA_postA  accA_postB  accB_postB  forget(A)
naive        1.000       0.032       1.000      0.968
replay       1.000       0.277       0.994      0.723   (25% of A rehearsed)
ewc          1.000       0.061       0.960      0.939   (lambda=1e3, diag Fisher of A)
local        1.000       1.000       0.998      0.000   (freeze shared substrate; private
                                                          additive hidden slot per B key)
joint         —          0.624       0.619        —     (oracle A∪B)
```

**Trainable key rep — each fact gets a private embedding row (private address):**

```
arm       accA_postA  accA_postB  accB_postB  forget(A)
naive/replay/ewc/local/joint  all 1.000  all 1.000  0.000 forgetting; joint capacity 1.000
```

## What I read from this (and where I want your fire)

1. **The entire difficulty collapses onto one variable: is the substrate shared or private.** With a
   private trainable address per fact, *even naive sequential has zero forgetting and joint hits 1.0*.
   Force the same knowledge through a shared fixed substrate and naive forgetting is 0.97 and even the
   joint oracle caps at 0.62. So "continual learning is hard" is, in this toy, *exactly equivalent* to
   "a fixed-size dense checkpoint forces knowledge to share parameters." That's the constraint we
   actually signed up for. Do you accept this framing, or is the toy smuggling the difficulty in via
   the h=64 bottleneck (i.e. is the capacity wall doing work that a real LM's width wouldn't)?

2. **EWC essentially fails here (0.061, barely above naive 0.032).** Diagonal-Fisher anchoring can't
   protect densely memorized associations — consistent with our banked "rehearsal-free is the real
   wall." Replay only buys back its budget fraction (0.277 at 25%). I don't think this is an EWC bug;
   I think dense factual memorization is close to the worst case for a quadratic penalty because the
   Fisher is nearly isotropic across the memorized rows. Agree, or do you want a stronger rehearsal-
   free control (SI / online-EWC / K-FAC) before I conclude EWC is genuinely weak here?

3. **`local` is a TRAP and I'm flagging it as one, not a win.** It hits 0 forgetting AND ~1.0 on B —
   and even *beats the joint oracle* — precisely because it gives each fact a private N×h additive
   slot, dodging the shared bottleneck entirely. That is your "boosting evasion": perfect retention
   bought with unbounded per-item parameters = external memory, forbidden at inference. It only
   re-proves "private address ⇒ no interference." Its ONLY legitimate use is as the *pre-consolidation*
   upper bound before a fold.

## The probe I want to run next (this is your fold-without-interference question, made concrete)

The toy now lets us test the actual crux cheaply: **can we consolidate the `local` private slots back
into the fixed shared W1/W2 and KEEP the retention?**

- Step 1: reach the `local` state (A in shared weights, B in private slots, both ~1.0).
- Step 2: FOLD — distill the slot-augmented function into the shared W1/W2 only (slots deleted), under
  an old-A trust region, and re-measure accA/accB with slots removed.
- Prediction space: if post-fold accA collapses back toward naive, interference is intrinsic to the
  fixed substrate at this N/h → **growth signal**. If a fold exists that keeps both, the private basis
  found an interference-free write direction → the interesting positive.
- Then sweep h (shared width) to trace where joint recovers and where fold-failure forces growth —
  i.e. draw the capacity-vs-interference frontier and locate the point growth becomes *necessary and
  measured* (your regime-3 language).

Two questions before I build the fold:

1. What is the honest **retention referee** for the fold in this toy? We DO have all of A's keys here
   (it's synthetic), so a clean old-A audit is available — but that's exactly the "retained old
   information" you called an impossibility boundary in the real setting. For the *sandbox* I think
   using full-A as the fold's trust-region referee is fine (we're measuring whether an interference-
   free fold EXISTS at all, an existence question), and only later do we restrict the referee to a
   bounded/anchored proxy to measure the deployable gap. Agree with that staging?

2. Is "fold the private-slot function into shared weights under an old-A trust region" the right
   minimal model of consolidation, or would you rather I test the harder self-generated-anchor fold
   (no access to A's keys, only model-regenerated A) immediately, since that's the real deployable
   question and the full-A version might give a falsely optimistic existence result?
