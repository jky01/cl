## Pivot to A — minimal-footprint rehearsal (design proposal)

The honest frontier is now: *the whole first-order protect-by-direction family is bounded ≪ replay.*
Replay works (0.877). So the real, practical, still-open question is **how little rehearsal is enough** —
turning "rehearsal-free (bounded)" into "constant/minimal-footprint rehearsal (deployable)."

**Core question:** does self-distilling a *few* old items per stream protect the *whole* old stream?

**Proposed harness change (`run_consolidate`, minimal):** add `BK_REPLAY_K` — when set, the replay set
for round r is **K facts sampled per prior stream** (not all `PER`). Everything else identical to `ours`
(no gold-old; self-distill KL to the pre-round snapshot; single dense; no inference memory).

**Sweep:** `K ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 40}` (K=0 ≡ naive, K=40 ≡ full `ours`). 6×40, 2 seeds.
Measure all-seen / all-para / oldest-forget vs K → find the knee (the minimal K that retains).

**Two footprint regimes to compare (the interesting science):**
1. **stem-subsample replay** — store K old *prompts* per stream, self-distill on them (footprint =
   K prompts/stream of training-time state).
2. **subject-only / self-generated replay** — store only the K old *subject keys* (or the model
   generates its own old-subject probes), self-distill on the model's current answer. Footprint →
   just K identifiers (or zero stored text). Tests whether the anti-forgetting signal needs the stored
   prompt text at all, or only "revisit these entities."

If K≤4–8 self-distilled items per stream retain within ~0.05–0.08 of full replay, that's a strong
**constant-footprint** knowledge-into-weights result: small training-time rehearsal is sufficient, no
inference memory, no joint retrain — the deployable version of the R33/R35 positive.
