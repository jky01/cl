## The concern I want you to challenge
We have spent the ENTIRE recent arc (R36→R50) on the retention / self-replay branch. Two explicit components of
the ULTIMATE goal — **(#3) grow into a larger model** and **(#6) scale to hundreds of books** — have received
ZERO experimental attention. Every time growth comes up we (correctly, in isolation) say "not justified until
capacity/interference saturates." But that reasoning has become a permanent deferral: we never actually MEASURE
the saturation point, so growth stays a slogan and #3 never advances. Meanwhile everything is 0.5B and ~30
articles — we have never observed how ANY of our mechanisms behave as N grows toward book-scale, and there is an
unanswered scale question hanging over the whole pessimism: **is the self-addressing failure (R48/R49a) a 0.5B
limitation that dissolves at 3B+, or a fundamental one?** If it's scale-gated, half our negatives are artifacts.

## My proposed portfolio shift (challenge it)
Alongside R50-A (self-addressing tomography), run TWO minimal probes we've been avoiding:

1. **Scale probe (cheap, decisive):** re-run the R49a addressability ladder + the margin proposition-equivalence
   audit on a 3B base with the SAME census facts. One question: does autonomous proposition SELECTION (the wall)
   improve materially at 3B? If yes → the self-replay pessimism is partly a 0.5B artifact and R50 should be sized
   up; if no → the addressing wall is fundamental and R50-A is the right frontier. This directly de-risks the
   whole retention program and is one no-training run.

2. **Minimal saturation/growth probe (the deferred #3):** actually MEASURE the deferral premise. Fix a 0.5B
   checkpoint, keep writing census streams until fixed-capacity retention (stored-cue replay) demonstrably
   SATURATES (retention/ppl degrades as N grows at fixed budget). Then test whether a function-preserving GROWTH
   step (width or depth or a merged residual expert) beats (a) the same fixed 0.5B and (b) a from-scratch larger
   model, at MATCHED total training compute, on retained held-out access. This is the first honest test of
   "grow-and-consolidate" as an actual mechanism rather than a slogan — and it's squarely the pivot the whole
   project is named after (s2/ grow-and-consolidate).

My argument: #3 and #6 are the biggest untouched gaps relative to the STATED goal, and the scale probe (1) is
cheap and could invalidate a lot of our pessimism. Continuing to only refine the retention branch risks
producing an ever-sharper map of one corner while the goal's growth/scale axes stay at zero.
