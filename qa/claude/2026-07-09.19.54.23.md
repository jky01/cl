## Implementation summary
- `load_audit`: reads frozen `r44_audit.jsonl`, per relation splits DISJOINT target (T=8 held-out
  matchable) + prime pools. Consumes the frozen artifact (no resampling after seeing results).
- `select_primes(pid, arm, N)`: schema_commit = N same-relation primes; item_k_matched = N from OTHER
  relations; schema_shuffle_same_kind = N from same-KIND other relations. All confound-matched to the
  target set via `_match` (nearest on ans_ntok, bpt_para, subj_bits).
- `consolidate` = PRIME write: QA-CE on primes (original Q->A) + neutral base-anchor KL, steps =
  `PRIME_STEPS * N` (scales so all N primes commit; prime compute is not the controlled variable).
- `bind_progressive`: BIND the T targets with QA-CE, cumulative gradient steps, measure held-out EM at
  each budget in `B_SCHED`. Eval: `tgt_manual` (MANUAL paraphrase = primary, per your pre-flight fix),
  `tgt_3b` (subset with rag_em_3b=1 = secondary robustness), `tgt_orig` (sanity).
- Prime retention measured pre AND post bind (`prime_para_pre/post`) + `prime_committed` (orig EM after
  prime, verifies the write happened before BIND — your "measure it twice").
- Floor: N=0 run once, labeled `floor` (arm-independent). Cells: relation × arm × N × seed; B swept
  progressively inside each cell. Dumps per seed. Slope summary = mean tgt_manual EM vs N per arm/bucket.

## Questions
1. **Bind budget calibration (the real risk you flagged).** `B_SCHED` default {1,2,4} gradient steps is
   probably too FEW at lr 1e-5 to move EM off the floor (each step = 1 pass over 8 targets). I'll run a
   PILOT: 1 person + 1 org relation, 1 seed, N∈{0,16}, WIDE schedule B∈{2,5,10,20,40}, to find the steep
   part, then set B to 3 points bracketing the 50% point. Agree that's the right calibration, and is
   "steps over the 8-target batch" the right budget unit (vs your "answer-target-token updates" — with
   fixed T=8 and fixed answers, steps ∝ token-updates, so I report both)?
2. **PRIME_STEPS scaling with N.** I scale prime steps ∝ N so all primes commit regardless of density.
   But that means higher-N cells pay more PRIME compute. Since the CONTROLLED variable is BIND budget
   (matched across arms/N), is scaling prime compute with N acceptable, or do you want prime budget also
   fixed (risking that high-N primes don't all commit)? I think commit-verified primes matter more than
   equal prime compute — `prime_committed` audits it.
3. **Interference confound in schema_commit primes.** All N schema_commit primes share the SAME relation
   output pathway as the target (e.g. all "who wrote X" -> a person). Could a schema_commit GAIN actually
   be the model settling into "output a person name" (answer-KIND prior) rather than relation schema? The
   `schema_shuffle_same_kind` control is meant to catch exactly this (same answer-kind, different
   relation) — if shuffle ALSO gains, it's answer-kind not relation-schema. Is that control sufficient,
   or do you want an additional answer-kind-only baseline?
4. **Cell count / cost.** 6 rel × 3 arm × 4 N (minus N=0 collapse) × 2 seed ≈ 132 prime-then-bind cells;
   each = up to PRIME_STEPS·16 + ~40 bind steps + evals. At ~30-60s/cell that's ~1.5-2.5h. Acceptable, or
   trim N to {0,8,16} or drop a person relation for the first screen?
5. **Slope test.** Headline = is (tgt_manual EM vs N) slope steeper for schema_commit than item_k_matched,
   in BOTH buckets, both seeds, with shuffle not passing. I'll fit per-relation slopes + relation-block
   bootstrap offline. Good, or do you want the mixed-effects `arm:N` fit run inline?
