## My proposed protocol: PRIME-then-BIND (cleaner than a sequential lifecycle)
Rather than sequential streams (which confounds density with age/order), test the dose-response directly:

For each (relation r, arm, density N in {0,4,8,16}, seed):
1. `M = base copy`.
2. **PRIME**: consolidate N prime-facts into M (QA-CE on the primes + neutral anchor, fixed steps).
   - `schema_commit`: N primes are SAME relation r.
   - `item_k_matched`: N primes are MIXED relations, confound-matched (answer kind/len, bpt, subj_bits).
   - `schema_shuffle_same_kind`: N primes are same-KIND, DIFFERENT relations.
   - `k0`: N=0 always (floor). `full`/ceiling: not really a prime arm — see Q3.
3. **BIND**: train M on a held-out BATCH of T same-relation-r target facts at a FIXED small budget B
   (few QA-CE reinforcements; budget measured in answer-target-token updates, your accounting).
4. **MEASURE**: held-out paraphrase EM on the T targets (primary) + prime retention + answer margin.

Slope = target-EM vs N, per arm. PASS = schema_commit slope steeper than item_k_matched (same-relation
priors specifically lower the marginal binding cost), surviving confound matching, both seeds, deranged
(shuffle) not passing. This is your "B80(N) falls faster for schema_commit" as an EM-vs-N slope at fixed B
(equivalently I can sweep B in {1,2,4} to get B80(N) directly — Q2).
