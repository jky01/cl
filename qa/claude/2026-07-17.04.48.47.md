## Your decisive contrast holds

- **res_unseen (model-only generalization to held-out residual inputs):** rule2 gets 0.22–0.26 (≫
  chance 0.025) — the network **discovers the second rule and generalizes to inputs it never trained
  on**. Atomic stays at chance 0.02 for every capacity. So `growth × structured residual → unseen
  generalization`, `growth/any-capacity × atomic → storage only, no generalization`. This is exactly
  your qualitative prediction and the clean separation between "creates knowledge" and "relocates a
  memory table."
- **grow ≈ wide** (res_unseen 0.264 vs 0.257; r1 0.250 vs 0.252): the modest rule2 gain from growth is
  a **capacity** effect, not a *when-added* effect — the function-preserving grow reaches the same
  place as wide-from-start, so this isn't a timing artifact.
- res_train=1.0 everywhere confirms training-set memorization is uninformative; only res_unseen
  separates discovery from storage, as you designed.

## Where it falls short, and it maps onto your condition-3

**rule-1 retention crashed to 0.20–0.26 in ALL conditions**, growth included (0.224→0.250 is within
noise). Under all-plastic phase-2 with only a 25% referee, the residual overwrote rule-1 regardless of
width. So this run is your condition-1/2 (all plastic); **width alone does not protect the old rule** —
it buys residual generalization but not old-rule preservation. That's precisely why your condition-3
(clean rule first, PROTECT the old block, train only the residual capacity) is needed: to get *both*
rule-1 retention and rule2 discovery. And rule2 is only partially discovered (0.26, not ~1.0),
consistent with the residual support being small and fighting the referee for the shared substrate.

So the honest status: the **growth thesis is validated** (grow for compressible residuals, they turn
into model-only generalization; don't grow for atomic, it's just storage), but the **mechanism to be a
real continual learner needs the protected-old-block growth** you specified, not naive all-plastic
width expansion.

## Proposed next (your condition-3 + compute views)

1. **Protected growth:** at the phase boundary, freeze the old h0 block (W1[:, :h0], and its rows of
   W2), grow the extra Δ units, and train phase-2 updating ONLY the new units (+ their W2 rows) on the
   residual. Compare to (a) fixed h0 all-plastic, (b) wide-from-start all-plastic, (c) fixed-total-
   width partitioned (h0 old block frozen + reserved residual block) so "protection helps" can't just
   mean "bigger". Prediction: protected-grow keeps rule-1 (≈ its phase-1 value) AND gets rule2
   unseen-generalization; atomic protected-grow keeps rule-1 but still no residual generalization.
2. **Two compute views** as you asked: matched-update/data AND matched-total-training-FLOPs (credit the
   grown model for being small in phase-1). Report final inference params/FLOPs too.
3. **The two-stage trigger** you defined, made operational: after routing residual off the protected
   block, fit a small probe on the residual and GROW only if it shows held-out gain (rule2 → yes;
   atomic → no). I can implement the probe as "does a fresh small head on frozen features predict
   held-out residual inputs above chance."

One question: for protected growth, do you want the old-block freeze to be hard (zero grad on
W1[:, :h0], W2[:h0, :]) or a soft trust-region (penalize drift)? Hard freeze is the cleanest causal
isolation of "separate capacity absorbs the residual"; soft would let a little old-rule adaptation
help rule2 share structure. I lean hard-freeze first for the clean claim, soft as a follow-up.
