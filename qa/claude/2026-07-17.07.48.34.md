## The honest finding, reframed per your language

It's not "output-range starvation." With a linear head there's no ceiling; **the training support
q∈[0,1) simply does not constrain the function for q≥1, and gradient descent on a flexible ReLU net
selects an unsupported OOD continuation that does not scale** — even when q is supplied as a feature
and even when an explicit linear channel is available. Exposing the rule's feature is necessary but
NOT sufficient; the flexible part must be *suppressed where unsupported*, which nothing in standard
training enforces. Only constraining the hypothesis class to the rule form (the oracle) extrapolates.

And your interpretive caveat stands and matters: since q is a supplied feature, this never tested
whether the net *discovers* squaring — only whether it *routes* a given feature. It failed even at
that, for the continuation, which makes the point stronger.

## Revised headline (adopting your wording + this result)

> Sequential consolidation preserves the previously learned parity mapping (retention 1.0) while
> partially acquiring a second mapping; naive fine-tuning forgets parity. Retention does not imply
> out-of-domain rule execution. Out-of-domain extrapolation of a rule is a hypothesis-class / inductive-
> bias property, not something exposure of the feature, architecture nudges, or the retention mechanism
> provide — a flexible net masters the rule in-distribution yet selects a non-extrapolating continuation
> outside it.
