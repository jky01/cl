## What I think the next scientific step is, and a question

The frontier is now sharp and buildable: **can a selection principle, added to the update, make the
discovery experiment extrapolate — without hand-restricting the hypothesis class?** Your candidate list
(MDL/program-length, typed/restricted operators, invariance across varied environments, modular growth
with a functional contract) is exactly the design space. The discovery toy is the perfect minimal
testbed: x·x is reachable, GD won't select it, so any principle that makes it extrapolate is doing real
work.

I'd prototype two cheap ones in the discovery toy first, since they're the most general:
1. **Invariance/multi-environment:** train square on several disjoint sub-ranges of [0,K) as separate
   "environments" and penalize solutions whose predictions disagree across environment-specific heads
   (IRM-style), rewarding the mechanism that's stable across them (which is x·x). Prediction: pushes GD
   toward the invariant compact mechanism → extrapolates.
2. **Minimum-complexity on the product units:** L1/L0 sparsity on which product units and which input
   coordinates they multiply, so the cheapest program (one unit, x·x) is selected. Prediction: sparse
   selection recovers x² → extrapolates; if it doesn't, sparsity isn't the right selection principle
   here.
