Two clean, separable facts:
1. **Horizon extrapolation of SEEN rules is SOLVED.** A 1.8M single-token model trained to H=12 executes an
   arithmetic recurrence to L=48 (4xH) at full-exact 1.00, with random unseen seeds each eval, so it is
   genuinely rolling the recurrence forward, not replaying. This is exactly csum's behavior, now for a
   family of arithmetic rules. This already satisfies the user's literal ask ("train a rule, continue its
   numbers past training length") for any rule IN the training set.
2. **Rule generalization to HELD-OUT deltas FAILS** at both p, even at p=23 with only 15 train / 7 held
   deltas. Debug shows the model applies a WRONG (memorized-family) delta to held prefixes, not the delta
   present in the prefix.

## My diagnosis (want you to check it)

The plain model predicting s_n from the local window {s_{n-1}, s_{n-2}} COULD use the delta-agnostic
general solution `s_n = 2 s_{n-1} - s_{n-2} mod p`, which generalizes to every delta. It does not learn
that. I think the reason is a COVERAGE/identifiability effect, not capacity: during training the pair
(s_{n-1}, s_{n-2}) only ever takes values whose difference is a TRAINED delta, so the general map `2a-b mod
p` is only supervised on the subset of its (a,b) domain reachable by seen deltas. A held-out delta is an
unseen region of that modular-arithmetic input space. This is the classic modular-generalization / grokking
setting: models fit the trained residue-pairs and do not extrapolate the algebraic operation to held-out
pairs without very long training + weight decay (grokking), or an architectural/representational bias.

So this is the SAME selection wall as the toy line (`toy_decoy`): training is observationally consistent
with both the general rule and a per-family memorization, and SGD picks memorization. In the toy, only
MULTI-ENVIRONMENT / invariance evidence broke the tie. Here every delta is effectively its own environment
but the model can memorize each, so multi-environment alone does not force generality.

## The three questions I want peer-reviewed

1. **Is held-out-RULE generalization even the right bar for the user's goal?** The user asked: train
   odd/even/quadratic, then have the model continue them. Those are IN-family (seen) rules, and seen-rule
   horizon extrapolation is SOLVED. I could honestly declare the milestone met (bounded family, seen rules
   extrapolate to 4xH) and note held-out-rule generalization as a separate, harder claim. Do you agree that
   is the honest deliverable, or is "continues a rule it was NOT trained on" the more meaningful target we
   should insist on?

2. **If we do chase held-out generalization, which mechanism?** Two candidates:
   (a) **Grokking**: train 10-100x longer with tuned weight decay at small p; test whether held-out deltas
       eventually generalize (the known modular-arithmetic grokking curve). Cheap-ish at p=23 locally.
   (b) **Single-token difference scaffold**: interleave the first difference as its own token
       `s0 d1 s1 d2 s2 ...` where d_i = s_i - s_{i-1}. Then predict d_i by COPYING d_{i-1} (delta-agnostic!)
       and s_i = s_{i-1} + d_i (one general modular add). Neither sub-step needs to know the delta value, so
       the model cannot memorize per-delta and MUST use the general add -> should generalize to held-out
       deltas. This is the same scaffold that FAILED in numseq, but there the failure was multi-DIGIT
       execution; on single tokens the copy+add are single-token ops that have a real chance. I lean (b) as
       the cleaner test of "compile the rule into a delta-agnostic local program", with (a) as the
       inductive-bias baseline. Do you agree, and do you want both run paired?

3. **Or stop optimizing this and move to the continual-learning main line now?** Seen-rule horizon
   extrapolation is a sufficient substrate to start sequential acquire-retain-grow (your prior directive):
   phase A arithmetic subfamily -> phase B a disjoint subfamily -> retain A, no replay, prefix-only rule
   selection. Held-out-rule generalization could be pursued IN that setting (does acquiring B improve or
   destroy generalization on A's family?). Is the single-token substrate with SEEN-rule extrapolation
   enough to begin the continual protocol, or do you want held-out generalization established first so
   "retention of a rule" means retaining a GENERAL rule rather than a memorized set?

I lean: (Q1) declare seen-rule horizon-extrapolation the honest milestone for the user; (Q2) run the
single-token difference-scaffold (b) as one clean attempt at held-out generalization, plus a grokking
long-run at p=23 as the baseline; (Q3) then move to the sequential continual protocol regardless, because
that is the project's actual question. Push back if you think held-out generalization is a prerequisite,
not a parallel track.
