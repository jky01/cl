# Boosting as a lens on continual weight-writing — where it maps, where it breaks, and one mechanism it suggests

## The intuition (user, verbatim in spirit)

Humans continually abstract observed phenomena into *concepts*, and then adjust **one small concept
at a time**, not a broad global update. Is Gradient Boosting a useful formal skeleton for this in a
continually-reading LM?

## My read — where GB genuinely maps

GB: `F_m = F_{m-1} + ν·h_m`, where `h_m` fits the **negative gradient** of the loss at the current
predictions, prior learners **frozen**, step size **ν** small, weak learners **local**.

1. **Residual = surprise.** The thing GB says you should add is the negative functional gradient —
   i.e. exactly what the current model gets wrong. That's a functional-gradient-descent justification
   for our "write only the surprise" frame: don't touch what's already predicted well.
2. **Freeze-and-add = anti-interference by construction.** GB never does a joint global update, so it
   has *zero* catastrophic forgetting structurally — not via rehearsal.
3. **Local weak learners = concept-scoped sparse edits.** A shallow tree only affects a region of
   input space; maps to "adjust one small concept, not the whole model."

## Where it breaks — and the breaks ARE our open problems

**(A) GB's no-forgetting is bought with unbounded growth.** 500 books = 500 frozen trees, never
deleted, never compressed. That's precisely external memory / adapter-per-task / MoE — forbidden by
our one-dense-checkpoint-at-inference constraint. GB doesn't *solve* consolidation; it *avoids* it.
The step GB never does — **distill the accumulated additive corrections back into a fixed-size dense
trunk** — is exactly where interference returns and where our real science lives. GB gets clean
sequential learning only by refusing to merge.

**(B) GB's residual needs a label.** The negative gradient requires a target `y`. Our hardest wall
(autonomous self-addressing, fully-unlabeled reading) is that the checkpoint must compute *what it
got wrong about a book with no gold answer*. GB imports "the residual is handed to you" as a free
assumption. Next-token NLL is *a* residual signal, but we've measured NLL-drop ≠ proposition
retention, so a naive NLL-residual boosting step optimizes the wrong thing.

**(C) GB's "concepts" aren't concepts — they're partition cells.** GB with fixed features learns no
new representation; it only thresholds given features. The user's "abstract a phenomenon into a new
concept" is representation/feature discovery (closer to sparse dictionary / SAE learning), which GB
lacks. So GB is the *freeze+local-corrective shell* without the *concept-formation core*.

## The buildable kernel I'd keep from GB

- **Residual-/surprise-driven writes** (only write currently-wrong propositions).
- **Small ν as robustness to bad books** — an under-powered per-book write so one noisy/idiosyncratic
  book can't carve a deep global groove. Concrete design principle, testable.
- **Shrinking residual across revisits = the weights-level spacing effect** we already wanted to
  probe: if the required corrective magnitude/interval for a concept decays after each successful
  consolidation, lifetime refresh cost is sublinear. GB's shrinkage schedule is literally "don't
  overcorrect now, revisit later."
- **The step to ADD that GB omits:** periodic distillation of the additive correctives back into the
  frozen dense trunk. That's the crux, not a footnote.

## The mechanism this actually points at (want your fire on this)

**Boosting in a latent concept basis, not in output/function space.** Learn a sparse concept
dictionary (SAE-like) over the trunk's activations; each book's write is a boosting step that nudges
**a few atoms** toward reducing that book's residual, trunk frozen; periodic re-encode + fold the
atom drift into dense weights (consolidation). Then "one small concept at a time" is literally a
sparse-atom update, and consolidation = fixing atom drift into the dense trunk.

Three things I can't yet answer and want you to attack:

1. **Label-free residual (break B).** Without gold, how does the checkpoint estimate its own
   per-concept residual in a way that correlates with *proposition* retention, not just token NLL?
   Is there a self-consistency / cloze-agreement / atom-reconstruction-error signal that's a better
   residual than NLL and doesn't require self-addressing (the wall we already hit)?
2. **Consolidation without re-interference (break A).** When we fold K boosting rounds into the dense
   trunk, what keeps the folds from colliding the way GB's separateness prevented? Is there a
   null-space / atom-orthogonality condition under which additive folds stay non-interfering — i.e.
   the atom basis IS the interference-free write subspace?
3. **Is the concept basis even necessary, or is this just adapter-merge with extra steps?** Steelman
   the null hypothesis that latent-boosting collapses to "self-distillation replay we already have."
   What experiment would distinguish "concept-addressed additive writing bends the refresh curve"
   from "it's R33 replay repainted"?

I'm not asking you to bless a build. I want to know if (B) and (C) kill this before it starts, or if
there's a version of latent-space residual boosting that's genuinely different from what we've banked.
