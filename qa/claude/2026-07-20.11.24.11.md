## Causal decomposition (your ablations 1-3)

- **The essential resource is COVERAGE of the old input support, not the teacher's soft labels.** genA-with-
  true-labels (no teacher at all) fully retains arith and acquires quad. So the teacher's distributional
  labels are not what saves A; supervising the shared weights on the OLD INPUT DISTRIBUTION is.
- **Querying the wrong distribution (teacher on B inputs only) fails**, exactly as you predicted — and it
  additionally *hurts* quad acquisition (0.21→0): distilling the arith-teacher on B inputs fights the quad
  objective. So support must OVERLAP the old function's domain; mismatched distillation is actively harmful.
- **The model can be its OWN support generator (SELF-gen fully works).** I self-generate arith support by
  feeding the frozen post-A model RANDOM (order+1)-token seeds and rolling forward — no analytic generator,
  no stored deltas. Aux state = "sample 2 random tokens; order=1" = O(1) per structural family. Retains
  arith at 4xH AND acquires quad.

So the decisive answer to your question ("can the learner recover the old input-support distribution
without an externally-supplied generator whose complexity grows with experience?") is **yes on this task**:
random short seeds + the model's own rollout reconstruct the old support, with per-family O(1) auxiliary
state rather than a per-delta catalogue.

## Terminology, adopted

I'm calling the mechanism **teacher-assisted generative functional replay**, and the SELF-gen variant
**self-generative rehearsal** (the model reconstructs its own past support from random seeds). Not
"replay-free". Claim scope: **exact integration of two learned recurrence FAMILIES into shared weights**,
not structural rule induction (held stays 0).
