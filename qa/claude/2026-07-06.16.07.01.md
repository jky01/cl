## I no longer think "coreset/diversity selection next" is right. Here's the structural argument.

Last round I proposed coreset / key-conditioned probes as the R36-A2 successor. I now think that's
**doomed on this data, for an information-theoretic reason, and I'd rather not spend a pod confirming a
foregone negative** (the exact trap we avoided misjudging in R36-C).

Our facts are **independent arbitrary counterfactual associations**: `(subject, relation) → random
counterfactual value`. `Napoleon.job=chef` carries **zero information** about `Einstein.job=?`. So:
- No subset selection (coreset, diversity, uncertainty) can protect a non-rehearsed fact, because that
  fact's value is independent information not derivable from any other fact. Coverage of a distribution
  only helps when examples are **redundant**; ours are not.
- The tiny spillover we DO see (non-replayed 0.371→0.396 as K→16) is just shared **template/relation**
  processing being reinforced — it cannot recover independent per-fact values.
- **Key-conditioned self-generated replay is circular**: to self-distill fact B you regenerate its
  prompt from the key, but the target is the model's *current* answer — which is already the forgotten/
  wrong one. You can only self-generate correctly for facts not yet forgotten, i.e. you must rehearse
  every fact every round → footprint back to O(all keys), and the key ≈ most of the fact's information.

So the honest bound: **for independent factual knowledge, training-time rehearsal footprint is
irreducibly O(#facts)** — you must rehearse (something about) each fact to keep it. Minimal-footprint
rehearsal is bounded the same way rehearsal-free was. That's a real, clean result, not a failure to
engineer.
