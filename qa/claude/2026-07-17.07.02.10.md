# Acquisition-quality fix worked (2-2.3×), and it surfaced the growth-transition signal: a capacity-saturation gradient.

Source: `qa/claude/2026-07-17.07.02.10.md`. Files: `s3/toy_consolidate.py`, `s3/toy_cycle.py`,
results `docs/cloud_results/toy_consolidate_truelabel.txt`, `docs/cloud_results/toy_cycle_truelabel.txt`.

The acquired-quality limitation I flagged was a consolidation-data bug, not a mechanism failure. The
consolidation was distilling the **gated teacher's weak residual prediction (~0.16)** onto the residual
region. Fix: distill the teacher only on the OLD/rule region (where it's strong), and use **true labels
on the newly-read residual** — legal because reading a fact gives its label.

Single acquisition: residual 0.163 → 0.417 (h0) / 0.462 (H), rule kept 0.654.
Cycle (4 groups, grow→consolidate ×3):

```
round  width  grp0   grp1   grp2   grp3      (was: 0.24/0.20/0.21)
3      160    0.667  0.551  0.451  0.352
```

grp0 retained 0.667 flat all 4 rounds (zero forgetting), width flat 160 (bounded).

## Two findings worth your read

1. **A graceful capacity-saturation gradient:** later-acquired groups degrade 0.551 → 0.451 → 0.352.
   grp0 is never forgotten (0.667 throughout), so this is not erosion — it's shrinking free room as
   more independent rules are packed into fixed h0=160. **This gradient is the operational signal for
   when growth must switch from temporary scaffold to permanent load-bearing capacity.** It also shows
   up in the single-acquisition H vs h0 split once residual quality is pushed up (0.462 vs 0.417).
2. So the growth story is now complete and honest: **growth is a scaffold while the fixed width has
   room; it becomes necessary when the degradation gradient signals saturation.** That's a measurable,
   non-arbitrary growth trigger — grow permanently when new-acquisition quality (or the h0-vs-H gap)
   crosses a threshold, not merely when forgetting appears.
