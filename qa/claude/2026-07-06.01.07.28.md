## Drift result (matched-newest, confound rejected, then honest tempering)

## Mechanistic hypothesis: AdamW breaks the null-space projection (this may be the real limiter)

Here's the bug I think we've been sitting on. `run_nswrite` projects the **gradient**:
`m.weight.grad ← G − (G U)Uᵀ`, then calls `opt.step()` with **AdamW**. But AdamW's update is
`m̂ / (√v̂ + eps)` — the momentum `m̂` of projected gradients stays ⊥U (linear combination), but the
**elementwise** division by `√v̂` does NOT preserve the column space. So the *realized parameter update*
is NOT ⊥U — Adam rotates it partly back into the old-input directions we're trying to protect. Over
1000s of steps this leaks exactly the drift we see. Plain SGD wouldn't do this (update = −lr·projected_G
stays ⊥U); AdamV's preconditioner is the culprit.

If this is right, we've been measuring "gradient occupancy" (which looks well-protected, energy 0.9+)
while the *actual weight change* is far less orthogonal to U than we thought — consistent with "94%
gradient removed yet still forgets."

## The cheap fix (test before building closed-form editing)

Enforce the constraint on the **accumulated** ΔW, not the per-step gradient. At each stream, save
`W_start`; after each `opt.step()`, re-project: `ΔW ← (W − W_start)(I − UUᵀ)`, set `W ← W_start + ΔW`.
This makes the realized `ΔW` exactly ⊥U on the input side regardless of optimizer — i.e. it *guarantees*
`ΔW·x ≈ 0` for old x∈span(U), which is precisely your functional-editing constraint `‖ΔW X_old‖²≤ε` in
its ε=0, hard form, but implemented as a cheap re-projection instead of a closed-form solve. Two variants
to test (I'll add both behind a flag):
- `reproject`: re-project accumulated ΔW each step (immune to Adam rotation).
- `sgd`: just swap AdamW→SGD+momentum for the projected update (cheapest possible test of the hypothesis).

Prediction: if AdamW-rotation is the limiter, `reproject`/`sgd` should retain markedly better than current
nswrite at BOTH 12 and 24 streams, at matched newest — and, importantly, might make the 24-stream benefit
NOT attenuate (because the accumulated ΔW is truly constrained, so cumulative drift is bounded by U's
completeness, not by Adam leakage).

## Where this sits vs functional editing

`reproject` IS a hard functional constraint (`ΔW X_old = 0`), just enforced by projection rather than a
least-squares solve. If it works, it's most of the value of your closed-form editing at a fraction of the
code, and keeps one dense model / no memory / rehearsal-free. If `reproject` still attenuates at 24
streams, then the limiter is U's *incompleteness* (the input basis doesn't capture all old-read
directions), and THAT is when the closed-form solve (which can store old outputs Y_old directly, not just
input directions) earns its complexity.
