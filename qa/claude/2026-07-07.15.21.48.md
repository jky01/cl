## The paradigm we skipped entirely: PARALLEL-train + MERGE, not SEQUENTIAL-write

Every mechanism we've discussed (nswrite, keytie, schema, grow_sep, surprise-gate) assumes SEQUENTIAL writing
into one evolving model. But there's a live research paradigm we never mentioned: **task arithmetic / model
merging** (task vectors, TIES-merge, DARE, model soups, Fisher-weighted merge). Train a SEPARATE fast model
(or LoRA) per stream from the SAME frozen base, then MERGE the deltas into one dense checkpoint.
- Why it fits our contract EXACTLY: inference is one dense un-routed model, no old DATA is needed at merge
  time (only the per-stream deltas), no joint retraining. Task arithmetic's finding is that knowledge is often
  LINEARLY separable in weight space: `θ_base + Σ τ_i` recovers multi-task ability.
- Why it's a different corner from all our work: interference is resolved at MERGE time by conflict-resolution
  (TIES drops sign-conflicting coordinates; DARE randomly prunes deltas), not avoided at write time. This
  sidesteps the sequential-drift problem entirely — each stream trains on a clean base, so there's no
  catastrophic forgetting DURING training; forgetting becomes a MERGE-interference problem instead.
- The honest catch: merging N deltas degrades as N grows (sign conflicts accumulate), and you must keep the N
  deltas around until merge (storage O(N·rank), but not O(data)). This is arguably a DIFFERENT decomposition
  of the same cost, but it might interact beautifully with surprise (#1): only surprising streams produce
  large task vectors; low-surprise streams produce near-zero deltas that merge for free.
- Testable in s2 directly: a `merge_ties` arm — per stream, train a LoRA on the frozen base only; at the end,
  TIES-merge all LoRAs into dense; eval old-only para. Compare to nswrite/ours_tgt. Question: does
  parallel+merge beat sequential rehearsal-free writing at matched compute?

I'm surprised we never tested this. It may be the strongest baseline we're missing, OR a genuinely better
route. Which is it?

## Quantitative reality-check: what does the knowledge-capacity scaling law SAY about "hundreds of books"?

We keep arguing mechanism without grounding the TARGET in numbers. There's a measured scaling law for
in-weight factual knowledge (Allen-Zhu / "Physics of LLMs": ~2 bits of storable knowledge per parameter). If
that holds, a 0.5B model tops out around ~1e9 bits ~ 125 MB of raw fact storage. R31 saw capacity-saturation
around 800 facts for 1 fixed layer + replay.
- Concrete question I want us to actually estimate: "hundreds of books" — say 300 books × ~10^4 novel facts =
  3e6 facts × ~50 bits/fact = 1.5e8 bits. Against a 0.5B model's ~1e9-bit budget, that's ~15% of capacity —
  FEASIBLE in principle, but only if the write is near information-optimal (which our current writes are NOT
  — they're hugely redundant). So the gap may be an EFFICIENCY gap (bits-per-fact overhead), not a capacity
  wall. This would say: the lever is write-efficiency (VSA, surprise-residual), and GROWTH is NOT needed for
  300 books at 0.5B if we write efficiently.
- Or the scaling law says the opposite at our regime. I want us to nail the arithmetic: does the capacity law
  make growth necessary or unnecessary for the stated target? This reframes the entire growth debate from
  philosophy to a back-of-envelope we can check against R31.

## Geometry: WHY does interference happen? (loss-landscape / permutation symmetry)
We treat interference as given. But linear mode connectivity + permutation symmetry (Git Re-Basin) say two
solutions often live in the SAME low-loss basin up to neuron permutation. If new knowledge could be placed in
a permutation-equivalent "empty slot," interference would be structurally avoided. Is CL partly a
basin-alignment problem? This is a deeper "why" under nswrite (which empirically protects directions without
explaining why they collide).

## Sparsity/modularity: structural interference-freedom, "growth without growth"
If each fact activated a SPARSE subnetwork (lottery tickets / SupSup supermasks / PackNet), different facts
use different parameters → interference is structurally near-zero WITHOUT adding capacity — you ALLOCATE from
an overparameterized dense net instead of growing. This is a different answer than VSA (sparsity vs
superposition) and than grow_sep (allocate existing slack vs add units). Does an overparameterized 0.5B have
enough "empty subnetworks" to allocate per stream, dodging both growth AND interference?

## Challenge our OWN metric: is "retain every old fact" even the right target?
Forgetting specifics while keeping gist IS generalization (bias-variance). A model that memorizes every fact
verbatim may generalize WORSE. Maybe the right headline isn't old_para retention but "does reading stream N
improve the WORLDVIEW / downstream capability?" If so, some forgetting is correct, and our entire metric suite
optimizes the wrong thing. R38B-A's paraphrase-EM is a memorization-robust metric, but it's still
per-fact-retention. Is there a capability-delta metric that would better capture the real goal?
