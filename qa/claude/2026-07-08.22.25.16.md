# Estimate: how long to read + internalize ONE book, IF Step B / B*(T) pass?

Discussion/strategy (no pod). I did a back-of-envelope from OUR measured numbers; want your
cross-examination — where the estimate is wrong, and any new insight on the cost model itself.
Evidence: `docs/cloud_results/r42_census.*` (density), `docs/cloud_results/r41_rung1.json`
(per-arm wall 440–497s; config 3 streams × [3+3] articles × 5 QA, 300 CPT + 400 CONS steps),
`s3/wikibridge.py` (LR 1e-5, batch: 4 LM + 8 QA + 8 replay per step). FINDINGS R42/R41.

## Book model
~300-page book ≈ 90k words ≈ 360 census-passages ≈ **~1,500 atomic facts** (R42: 3–5 gated
probes/passage). Model Qwen2.5-0.5B, one 4090.

## My estimate (current, UNoptimized recipe)
| stage | basis | estimate |
|---|---|---|
| ① read+probe+quality screens (3B gen, faithfulness, self-contained, paraphrase) | R42: 122 passages/~600 probes = 178s on a 3090 | 360 passages → ~15 min |
| ② internalize (scaffold CPT + consolidation-distill + surprise-routed replay) | R41: 163s per stream of 15 facts @700 steps → ~11 s/fact | 1,500 facts → **~4.5 h** |
| ③ closed-book eval | fast | ~10 min |
| **total** | | **≈ 5 h / book** |

## My four claims for you to attack
1. **Cost tracks #facts, not #pages.** R42 says ~70–80% of a book is on-manifold (below τ=8) and
   needs ~no replay budget; only the ~20% exception tail is expensive. So a novel ≪ a textbook. Do
   you buy "expensive part = O(#exceptions), cheap part ≈ free"? Note the subtlety: even on-manifold
   facts still cost the scaffold-CPT + consolidation forward/backward passes to WRITE; surprise only
   economizes the REPLAY term, not the initial write. So per-fact write cost is NOT free even at
   low bpt. Does that break the "cheap part ≈ free" framing? I think it caps the achievable speedup:
   selective replay saves the O(T·#facts) rehearsal term, not the O(#facts) write term.
2. **11 s/fact is gross over-training** → steps cut 3–5× likely lossless (commit rate saturates
   early: 700 steps × batch 8 / 15 facts ≈ 370 exposures/fact). Optimized ≈ **1.5–2 h/book**. Is
   370 exposures/fact obviously wasteful, or is the QA-CE + LM + replay mixture such that each fact
   gets far fewer effective gradient touches than the raw count implies? I have NOT run a
   steps-vs-retention curve — this is my weakest claim.
3. **Retention ≠ perfect:** R41 bar is ~0.53 exception / ~0.62 all-old paraphrase EM. "Remembers
   ~60% of the hard points on one read," not eidetic. Fair characterization?
4. **7B extrapolation:** ~14× train FLOPs, needs one A100/H100, ~2–4 h/book; LoRA-write+merge or
   top-layers-only compresses further. Too hand-wavy? The write-term FLOPs scale with params, but
   the NUMBER of gradient steps to commit a fact may NOT grow with model size (could even shrink —
   bigger model, fewer steps to store a fact). If steps-to-commit is flat or falls with scale, the
   14× is an overestimate. Do you have a prior on how steps-to-commit scales with model size?

## The three caveats I'd rank above wall-time
- retention < 100% (above);
- **facts ≠ understanding** — internalization is atomic-fact + paraphrase-robustness; composition/
  abstraction is the R32/R34 unsolved axis, and the two-hop canary shows composition is NOT
  surprise-visible at span level;
- **book #1 ≠ book #100** — our continual-write evidence stops at ~10² facts; 1,500 facts/book ×
  many books is the B*(T) accumulation we haven't measured. This is the estimate's biggest
  extrapolation risk (if B*(T) is linear, book #100 is far more than 5h because the replay set the
  new book must protect has grown).

## Questions
1. Where is the estimate most wrong — the 11 s/fact, the O(#exceptions) framing, or the flat
   per-book time (ignoring that book N must also replay books 1..N-1)?
2. New insight on the cost model: is "time per book" even the right unit, or should it be
   "incremental interference cost to add book N given N-1 already in weights"? The honest continual
   number is d(cost)/d(book), and R42 only measured single-book density, not cross-book interference.
3. Any cheaper decisive experiment to bound d(cost)/d(book) than the full B*(T) ladder — e.g. a
   2-book real-text run (book 1, then book 2, measure book-1 retention + book-2 write cost) as a
   minimal cross-book probe before the full ladder?
