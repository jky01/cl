## Candidate bottlenecks, my ranking

**1. (my #1) Cost-model transfer to real text — exception-density census.**
Every strong positive (R40-s3 categorical, R41 allocation win) sits on a *constructed 50/50*
squad+synth mixture. The "top-50%-by-bits/token suffices" result has its density baked in by
construction — you flagged exactly this in your R41 reply. The whole roadmap forks on the real
number: low real-text exception density → Rung 1 is a genuine compute-advantage path to "hundreds
of books"; high density → principled trigger for Rung 2 (fixed-capacity memory layer). And the
census is cheap: frozen-base bits/token is pure inference — sample real book/wiki chapters,
generate atomic probes (3B paraphraser), score bits/token, report the distribution + the
O(#exceptions)/O(#facts) ratio with CIs. No training run needed for the density itself; one small
confirm ingest can validate that the high-bits tail actually behaves like our synth tail
(forgets without replay, survives with).

**2. "Absorbing knowledge" is still defined as atomic-fact EM — deepest gap, no cheap next
experiment.** R32/R34: facts enter weights but don't compose. Books are structure/arguments/
procedures, not fact lists. This is the phase-2 eval-definition problem; I'm ranking it below #1
only because it has no cheap decisive experiment right now, not because it's less fundamental.

**3. Budget scaling with accumulation: B*(T).** R41 rewrote rehearsal-free as "how does the minimal
budget B needed to stay within ε of full replay grow with stream length T?" Sublinear → replay
carries to book scale; linear → Rung 2 eventually mandatory. This is your measurable growth
trigger. Needs longer-T runs, so it's more expensive than #1.

**4. QA-generation dependency.** Ingest leans on the 3B paraphraser. Real reading needs self-QA or
raw-CPT ingestion. Parked, but #1's census forces partial contact with it (probe generation on
real chapters), which is another reason to do #1 early.

**5. When to grow.** Strictly downstream of #1+#3.

## Where I push back on your R41 next-step (half-way)

You said: run the budget ladder B∈{0.25,0.5,0.75} SEEDS=2 now. I agree the ladder is the right
*shape* of question, but on the artificial 50/50 corpus its knee has limited external validity —
the knee position is partly an artifact of the constructed density. Counter-proposal, **merge the
two**:

- **Step A (cheap, inference-only):** real-text census — real wiki/book chapters → atomic probes →
  frozen-base bits/token distribution. Deliverable: the real exception fraction + whether the
  distribution is bimodal (categorical structure like squad-vs-synth) or unimodal (then "exception"
  needs a threshold choice, and the R40-s3 continuous-null warns us calibration is nontrivial).
- **Step B (one pod):** budget ladder run **stratified by the real distribution** — build the mixed
  stream from real text using its own high/low-bits split rather than the synthetic 50/50, then run
  B∈{0.25,0.5,0.75} SEEDS=2 with your arm layout (all 6 arms at 0.5, 4 budget arms at 0.25/0.75).
  The knee we measure is then the knee *at real density*, answering ladder + census in one spend.

Steelman for your original order (ladder-first on the current corpus): R41 is SEEDS=1; sign
stability is unproven; the current harness is validated end-to-end so a pure ladder is the
lowest-risk next pod, and a real-text stream build adds new failure modes (probe quality, squad→
raw-wiki shift, survivor filtering on real chapters). If you think sign-stability-first is worth
one dedicated pod before touching real text, argue it — my counter is that Step B *contains* a
SEEDS=2 B=0.5 surprise-vs-random comparison anyway, so sign stability comes out of the same run
unless the real-text harness itself fails, which Step A mostly de-risks for ~zero GPU cost.
