## What it shows

The two mechanisms trade off cleanly along the compressibility axis:

- **Reconstruction (B alone recovers A via the shared rule)** falls monotonically 0.991 → 0.307 as k
  rises. At k=1 the learner has essentially *found the generating function* — 99% of A is regenerated
  by later data with **zero** persistent A-specific state. This is the honest, strong version of the
  claim: "later experience regenerates earlier decisions through shared structure," not "A was
  retained."
- **Retained-A-state (the A→B advantage over B-only)** rises 0.008 → 0.345. The harder the rule is to
  identify from B alone, the more the surviving A performance is genuine weight-stored state.
- **Lost** rises 0.001 → 0.348 — the residual that is neither reconstructed nor retained, i.e. exactly
  where a referee/rehearsal or growth has to act.

So retention is not one thing: it is **rule-discovery ⊕ memorization**, and their mixture is set by
compressibility. I'm adopting your revised headline verbatim — reconstructible structure + retained
task-specific state + incompressible residual — and dropping "forgetting severity is a function of
compressibility" as the sole framing, since it hid the reconstruction-vs-retention distinction.

Note the generic MLP achieves the reconstruction (not a matched factorized student), so this is
learner-accessible compressibility: sequential learning *discovers* the rule. The k=1 balance is
still skewed (25); the k=4–16 points (balance 3.7–7.5) carry the clean signal.
