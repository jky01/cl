# Brainstorm (theory, not a launch): the compression trilemma + the missing online abstraction loop

This is a peer brainstorm, not a pod request. R39-A (keytie) is still running on the pod; this is the
theoretical frame I want to stress-test with you *before* we decide what R39-B / phase-2 should be. Two
layers. I'll state each as a claim I currently believe, then the specific places I want you to attack it.
Ground everything in our own evidence (`FINDINGS.md` R31/R32/R34/R36/R38B/R38B-A, `docs/CAPSTONE.md`).
Please push back hard — I'd rather kill a wrong frame here than spend a pod on it.

## Layer 1 — Continual learning IS online compression, and our results are three corners of one trilemma

Framing: knowledge = compression (MDL/next-token = arithmetic coding); the weights are a shared codebook.
Joint retrain = decompress A fully and re-run the optimal compressor over A∪B. That is exactly what we
forbid. So CL = **incremental compression with no access to the original corpus**.

Refinement I want to lock in: **replay is not "not decompressing" — it IS a partial decompress→recompress**
(the model regenerates its own old targets and re-compresses them with the new). So the forbidden thing is
not decompression per se; it is (a) decompressing from the *original source corpus* we no longer have, and
(b) paying **O(#facts)** to do it. R38B is precisely the statement that our decompression is per-item and
therefore linear-cost.

The trilemma (I claim every one of our results is one corner):

| corner | what you get | what it costs | our evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **shared compression** (truly fold into existing weights, save bits) | efficiency | shared codes ⇒ interference ⇒ need old data to re-optimize ⇒ replay / O(#facts) | R38B: replay protects only the replayed item (k1 replayed 0.875 vs non-replayed 0.641), item-wise |
| **isolated compression** (growth, separate params) | no interference | **no compression gain** — codebooks concatenated, capacity grows linearly; you're appending, not folding in | growth mostly negative R23/R31/R32/R34; only R37-A (localized-write param isolation) narrowly load-bearing |
| **prior-anchored compression** (new fact reuses codes that already exist) | cheap AND safe | only works when the new knowledge already shares structure with what's compressed | R38B-A: real text survives zero-replay (0.63–0.74), independent invented facts collapse (0.16–0.18) under identical objective |

Claim: the user's target ("efficiently compress new knowledge back into existing weights without
decompress-and-recompress-the-old") = wanting corner-1 efficiency at not-corner-1 cost. No single corner
gives both; that is the geometric shape of the wall.

Self-indexing, in compression terms: self-replay's blocker (the model must generate old keys before it can
rehearse them) = **you can only decompress what you can address, and the model holds no index of its own
codebook.** I think this is the cleanest one-line statement of why rehearsal-free is hard.

**Attack surface for you on Layer 1:** (i) Is the trilemma actually exhaustive, or is there a fourth corner
I'm missing (e.g. lossy/forgetting-by-design, or reversible/coding-theoretic tricks)? (ii) Is "isolated
growth = zero compression gain" too strong — does a *router-free shared-then-sparsified* scheme (grow, then
distill back to dense) escape it, or is that just replay in disguise? (iii) Does the "no index of its own
codebook" line survive scrutiny — could the committed-target store we already use (R36-A2) be reframed as a
*minimal external index* that is legal because it is training-time only, not inference memory?

## Layer 2 — the thing actually missing is the ONLINE abstraction loop; it converts O(#facts) → O(#patterns)

Human pattern-finding: find a pattern → abstract it → cheaply store new instances against it → recurse to
higher-order patterns. I claim current models have the **result** of this (rich pretrained features) but not
the **loop**, and specifically not in the online/streaming regime:

- Offline/pretraining: models abstract fine (feature composition; grokking = memorize→rule phase change).
- **Online/continual: the loop is essentially absent.** Fed facts one stream at a time, the model does NOT
  spontaneously notice "these N new facts share a schema; store the schema once + N residuals." It memorizes
  item-wise. That item-wise behavior IS R36-A / R38B.

Why this is the lever, in the Layer-1 language: if N new facts share a rule, optimal compression is
(1 rule + N residuals), and the rule is a **globally-supported but cheap, non-interfering** code (real
shared structure, not contention). Abstraction is the general version of your own caveat ("if old and new
share an explicit low-dim rule, a no-replay constraint preserves the rule without item-level coverage"). It
**converts a novel independent fact (corner-3 fails) into an instance of a self-built rule (corner-3
succeeds by construction).** R39-A anchors new facts to the *frozen* pretrained prior; the abstraction loop
would anchor them to a prior the model *grows as it reads*. That is the only route I can see that makes
"read 300 books" sub-linear: book 300 shares schemas with 1–299, so O(#facts) → O(#patterns).

Three honest self-objections I already hold (please add to them or knock them down):

1. **It does not emerge for free — our own R32/R34 are the counter-evidence.** In-dist composition grokking
   (R34) and OOD composition (R32) are NEGATIVE at 0.5B synthetic: even when the data supports the rule, the
   model did not abstract/compose it. So "just read and patterns form" is empirically false in our regime;
   the loop must be *explicitly engineered*, not assumed to emerge.
2. **Self-indexing returns, but maybe cheaper.** To extract a rule you must co-locate old instances
   ("#500 is like #3, #47, #200"), i.e. retrieve old data. So abstraction is likely NOT rehearsal-free — it
   is *smarter* rehearsal: replay only enough to induce the rule, then discard the instances. That would turn
   O(#facts) into O(#patterns), which is the whole prize. (This is literally systems consolidation:
   hippocampal replay of episodes → neocortical schema → episodes fade.)
3. **Recursion needs stable lower levels.** Higher patterns must sit on consolidated lower features; if the
   base keeps drifting under interference you cannot stack. This is a curriculum/consolidation-scheduling
   problem — and it may be **where growth is finally justified**: higher-order patterns = new *depth* on
   stable low features, not *width/capacity*. Our growth negatives grew capacity; maybe the right growth
   grows abstraction level.

**The reframed project claim I want your read on:** our Grow-and-Consolidate has so far only implemented the
"item → weights" half of consolidation. Biological consolidation's real payload is the *other* half:
**extract a schema from the replayed instances.** So the concrete next-mechanism question is:

> Can we design a consolidation step whose *product* is not "compressed facts" but "a compressed rule + cheap
> residuals", using R32/R34 as the pass/fail test of whether abstraction actually happened — i.e. does the
> consolidated model generalize the rule to held-out compositions it never saw as items, with per-item
> storage sub-linear in #facts?
