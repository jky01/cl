## What I now think the negative means (attack this)

Two facts together are the real signal: input-only removes **94%** of the gradient and STILL forgets
(0.47 at 12 streams); and adding capacity isn't the issue (P2a: eff-grad stable, newest fine). So
forgetting persists even when almost all of the update is projected out. That points at something the
whole "protect-by-direction" family can't fix:

- The projection is first-order and per-layer (`grad ← grad(I−UUᵀ)` makes ΔW·x≈0 for old x to first
  order), but **nonlinearity (attention softmax, downstream layers, LM-head argmax) + accumulation over
  1000 steps × many streams** means the surviving 6% still drifts old answers. You cannot remove more
  without killing newest (already at the edge). So direction-protection has a **ceiling** here (~0.47).

If that's right, the lever is NOT a better direction basis. It's one of:

1. **Attack the drift magnitude directly (cheapest, run first).** Forgetting accumulates over the 1000
   steps/stream. newest learns fast (fresh 0.85–1.0), so maybe far fewer steps or a lower LR on the
   projected update writes the fact while drifting old knowledge much less. A quick nswrite sweep over
   `STEPS ∈ {1000, 300, 100}` and `LR ↓` tells us if the residual is "too much writing" (fixable, cheap)
   vs fundamental. I think this is worth one run before any new machinery.

2. **Change the primitive to closed-form FUNCTIONAL editing (your B).** Instead of constraining the
   gradient *direction*, solve for ΔW that installs the new fact while *exactly* preserving old key→value
   outputs (least-squares with equality constraints on stored old (key,value) low-rank summaries). This
   constrains the FUNCTION, not the update direction — strictly stronger. Open problem: sequential
   closed-form editing is known to degrade, and the "preserve old outputs" constraint needs old key/value
   summaries as training state (compressed, not prompts — still rehearsal-free at inference).

3. **Isolate-then-consolidate (reopens slots/adapters, but for a NEW reason).** Here's the inference I
   most want you to attack: the negative suggests **shared-parameter writing inherently interferes** —
   not because capacity is exhausted (it isn't), but because any update in a shared weight, however
   projected, perturbs old readouts through nonlinearity. If so, the fix is to **write new knowledge into
   ISOLATED parameters (per-stream adapter/slot, zero-init gated), then consolidate** into the dense
   model with a function-preservation step. This is your `slotwrite`, but motivated by
   interference-avoidance, not capacity — and it would mean P2a's "not a capacity wall" does NOT rule out
   added parameters; it only rules out *needing* them for writable gradient. Adapters here buy
   *interference isolation*, not capacity.
