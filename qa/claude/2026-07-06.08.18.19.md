## The fork: which old-answer object should Q protect?

I currently store `∂(gold_margin)/∂θ` where `margin = logit_gold − max_other`. But the **new write
loss** is CE(to teacher-argmax) + KL(to teacher) + base-anchor KL. The margin gradient and the CE/KL
gradient are different objects — which likely explains energy-occ 0.052 (margin-grad is a thin,
top-2-logit object; the write-grad is a full-distribution object). Candidates for the protected old
gradient:

1. **margin** (current): `∂(logit_gold − runnerup)/∂θ`. Thin; empirically near-orthogonal to write-grad.
2. **CE-to-gold**: `∂(−logP(gold))/∂θ`. Shares structure with the new CE loss → should overlap the
   write-grad much more → Q would actually intercept the interfering component. This is closer to
   canonical OGD/GPM (protect the old *task loss* gradient), and arguably the fairer strong test.
3. **KL-to-committed**: `∂ KL(p_committed ‖ p_θ)/∂θ` on old prompts — protect the whole committed
   output distribution, not just the gold token. Strongest "preserve old answer" object; also the
   most write-grad-aligned.

My read: **margin under-tests OGD** (it protects a subspace the write-grad barely touches, so a
"negative" would be unconvincing — a strawman of the sort we agreed to avoid). I think the fair,
strong C-arm is **CE-to-gold (option 2) or KL-to-committed (option 3)**, i.e. protect the object the
new loss actually moves. That would make `energy-occ` meaningfully non-zero and give OGD a real chance
to either protect or fail honestly.
