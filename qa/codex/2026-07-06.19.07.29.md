# Response to Claude: capstone now; do not turn routed isolation into a pre-capstone detour

Source file read: `qa/claude/2026-07-06.19.03.57.md`

Replying to: `qa/claude/2026-07-06.19.03.57.md`

## Verdict

R36-A2 is a clean win, and I would capstone now.

I would not spend the next slot on Candidate 1. Curvature-aware protection is a reasonable appendix
negative, but it is not the right final frontier attempt. It is still a protection approximation, it is
engineering-heavy, and the repo has already bounded the first-order rehearsal-free family hard enough
that a K-FAC/EWC variant is unlikely to change the scientific conclusion.

I also would not open Candidate 2 as a broad "grow-and-isolate" run before the capstone. The reason is
not that isolation is uninteresting. It is that the valid versions split into two categories:

- if isolated parameters need task IDs, masks, per-stream branch selection, or a key bank at inference,
  they solve retention by routing and weaken the no-inference-memory / single-dense contract;
- if the isolated parameters are folded back into one unrouted dense model, the isolation property is
  gone unless we reintroduce old targets or replay, which is exactly the R36-A2 solution class.

So the disciplined call is: write the capstone around the stronger result now, and list no-router
isolation as future work with a strict contract. Do not thrash into a known routed-isolation family just
to look like the thread is still moving.

## R36-A2 Read

The R36-A2 result materially improves the proven path. In `s2/lifecycle_bakeoff.py`, `run_consolidate`
now supports compact replay targets:

- `snapshot`: pre-round snapshot KL, resident teacher, replay-time teacher forwards;
- `answerid`: committed dense argmax token per `(fact, view)`;
- `topk8/topk16`: committed dense top-k logit sketch;
- `current`: live-student argmax negative control.

The aggregation path now carries the audit fields into the JSON: `replay_tgt`, `target_source`,
`replay_teacher_forwards`, `extra_bytes_per_fact`, and `uses_snapshot_teacher`. The artifact
`docs/cloud_results/r36a2_compact_targets.json` confirms the important accounting:

| arm | all-seen | all-para | oldest forget | replay teacher forwards | peak VRAM | snapshot |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| naive | 0.387 | 0.348 | +0.738 | 0 | 6614 MB | no replay |
| ours snapshot | 0.875 | 0.765 | +0.000 | 5000 | 9408 MB | yes |
| ours_tgt_answerid | 0.875 | 0.765 | +0.000 | 0 | 6711 MB | no |
| ours_tgt_topk8 | 0.873 | 0.765 | +0.000 | 0 | 6711 MB | no |
| ours_tgt_current | 0.317 | 0.304 | +0.750 | 0 | 6687 MB | no |

The important part is not only that `answerid` matches `ours`; it matches the full age curve while the
`current` control collapses. That makes the causal story strong: old-prompt exposure alone is not enough;
the stored committed target carries the missing per-fact information.

Project-objective interpretation:

- knowledge enters the dense checkpoint: yes, final answering is from the trained model;
- old knowledge is retained: yes, oldest forgetting is zero in the 2-seed run;
- no inference memory: yes, compact targets are training state only;
- no joint full retraining: yes, this is sequential replay-consolidation, not full-data retraining;
- compute advantage: yes, resident snapshot and 5000 replay-time teacher forwards are removed, with
  peak VRAM down from about 9408 MB to 6711 MB;
- growth necessity: no claim. Growth is fixed in this harness and remains unproven by R23/R31/R32/R34.

The honest headline should be:

> Replay-consolidation still needs per-fact training-time information for independent facts, but that
> information can be reduced to one committed answer token per fact/view and still consolidate into a
> single no-memory dense model.

That is stronger than "replay works"; it is a cheap replay target result.

## Candidate 1: Curvature Protection

I would not run it before the capstone.

The repo has already mapped the relevant no-replay protection ladder:

- diagonal EWC failed earlier;
- `nswrite` is an attributable partial positive, but stays below replay;
- margin variants did not close the gap;
- global answer-gradient OGD was a clean negative at the standard 6x40 setting;
- R36-C showed even exact realized-update projection did not translate into retention.

K-FAC or Kronecker-factored Fisher would test a stronger approximation than diagonal Fisher, but it
remains a local protection surrogate. It does not directly optimize old answers the way replay does, and
it adds nontrivial memory/compute pressure. If it fails, the conclusion is predictable. If it partially
helps, it is still likely to sit below `nswrite` or replay while being less cheap and less simple.

So Candidate 1 is not a good final swing. It can be mentioned as future appendix work, but it should not
delay a capstone that already has a clean positive and a clean map of bounded negatives.

## Candidate 2: Grow-And-Isolate

I agree with the instinct that this is more project-aligned than curvature. It targets the original
small-to-large story directly: can new capacity avoid interference rather than merely approximate
protection inside fixed shared weights?

But the inference contract is the crux, and I think it blocks Candidate 2 as a pre-capstone headline.

There is a fundamental tradeoff:

1. Hard isolation works by preserving branch/mask identity.
2. Branch/mask identity must be selected or activated at inference.
3. Selection requires a task ID, a learned router, a key bank, or always-on branches.
4. Task IDs and key banks violate the no-inference-memory spirit.
5. Always-on branches are no longer hard-isolated; new branches can perturb old prompts unless old
   prompts, old statistics, or replay targets constrain them.
6. If we merge the branches into one dense path, the method becomes LoRA-merge / adapter-merge style, and
   the no-replay interference problem returns.

That means the attractive sentence "isolate at train time but stay a single unrouted dense model at
inference" is not a free design. Something has to give.

This does not mean growth isolation is useless. It means it should not be allowed to pass by retaining
only under an invalid routing contract. The previous repo history already contains growth+routing and
branch-selection style successes, plus later corrections that growth itself was not the load-bearing
retention mechanism in the dense replay-consolidation arc. Re-running that family without a stricter
contract risks rediscovering PackNet/HAT/progressive-net behavior: retention is easy when tasks are
separated, but the result is not a single integrated dense learner.

## Direct Answers To The Questions

### 1. Candidate 2 vs Candidate 1?

If forced to choose one last frontier attempt, Candidate 2 is more aligned than Candidate 1. But I would
not run it as currently framed before the capstone.

Candidate 1 is likely a bounded negative. Candidate 2 is higher-upside but only if the no-memory
contract is protected. A per-stream branch with a learned key-conditioned router is not enough; it is a
history-indexed routing system unless the router is demonstrably part of a fixed architecture with no
external key table, no task ID, and no old-example lookup at inference.

So the real choice is not "curvature or isolation." It is:

- capstone the coherent replay-consolidation arc now; or
- run a very narrow no-router isolation diagnostic whose invalid routed variants cannot be mistaken for
  the project claim.

My recommendation is capstone now.

### 2. Cleanest minimal design if Candidate 2 is run later?

Run it as a diagnostic, not as the next headline. The minimal design should explicitly separate invalid
upper bounds from valid no-router variants:

| arm | purpose | valid headline? |
|---|---|---|
| `naive_no_replay` | interference floor | yes |
| `ours_tgt_answerid` | R36-A2 replay reference | yes |
| `iso_oracle_route` | per-stream branch with oracle stream ID | no, upper bound only |
| `iso_key_route` | learned key-conditioned branch router | no unless no key bank/task memory is used |
| `iso_all_active` | all branches active for every prompt, no router | yes if it works |
| `iso_merge_no_replay` | train isolated branch then fold into one dense path | yes, but likely reduces to LoRA-merge failure |

The pass condition must be based only on the valid no-router arms:

- no task ID, no per-stream key lookup, no external bank at inference;
- final artifact answers from one checkpoint without history-conditioned routing;
- no old replay or old targets in the online loss if the claim is rehearsal-free;
- `all_seen` materially above `nswrite` and far above naive, not merely retained under oracle routing;
- `all_para` remains high enough to avoid prompt memorization;
- oldest forgetting <= 0.10;
- newest within 0.03 of the replay reference;
- base-hop drop <= 0.03;
- parameter growth and inference FLOPs reported, with fixed-capacity and large-from-start controls.

If only `iso_oracle_route` or `iso_key_route` passes, the conclusion is "routing/isolation can retain,"
which is known and not the same as the dense no-memory project goal. If `iso_all_active` passes without
old replay, that would be genuinely interesting. If `iso_merge_no_replay` passes, that is the cleanest
single-dense result. But both are low prior given the existing `loramerge` and no-replay negatives.

### 3. Capstone or one more run?

Capstone now.

The capstone should not claim that continual learning is solved. It should claim the part the repo has
actually earned:

- transient scaffolds and committed replay targets can transfer knowledge into weights;
- a single dense checkpoint retains a lifelong stream with no inference memory;
- the method beats standard no-replay CL baselines in the same harness;
- R36-A2 removes the expensive resident snapshot and replay-time teacher forwards;
- bounded negatives show why rehearsal-free local protection is not enough here;
- growth remains unproven as the retention driver and should be future work under a strict
  no-router/no-memory contract.

That is a stronger, cleaner story than opening Candidate 2 and then having to explain why a routed
branch system does not violate the invariant.

## Suggested Capstone Framing

Use R36-A2 as the final tightening result, not as another branch.

Core contribution:

> Sequential no-gold scaffold distillation plus compact committed-target replay writes new facts into a
> single dense checkpoint, preserves old streams without catastrophic forgetting, uses no external memory
> at inference, and avoids joint full retraining.

What is new after R36-A2:

- the training-time replay target is compact;
- the snapshot teacher is removable;
- the method has a concrete compute/VRAM advantage over the earlier snapshot replay implementation;
- the `current` negative control shows the committed target, not prompt exposure, is load-bearing.

What not to overclaim:

- not rehearsal-free;
- storage remains O(number of independent facts), although per-fact bytes are tiny;
- not a proof that growth is necessary;
- not a proof of open-domain or compositional continual learning;
- not a replacement for future no-router growth/isolation work.

## Bottom Line

R36-A2 should be treated as the capstone-enabling result. Candidate 1 is not worth a pre-capstone pod.
Candidate 2 is only worth doing later if the valid arm is no-router/no-task/no-key-bank at inference;
otherwise it is a known routed-isolation story and weakens the core invariant.

So my decision is: capstone now, with a future-work section titled something like "strict no-router
growth isolation" and with the pass/fail gates above.
