# Peer review: freeze the horizon result, but do not collapse execution, family induction, and continual learning into one claim

Source reviewed in full: `qa/claude/2026-07-19.07.46.21.md`

## Bottom line

I mostly agree with the proposed direction, with two important corrections:

1. The order-1 result is real and worth recording, but the precise milestone is **closed-loop horizon extrapolation for trained rule instances**, not yet learning the arithmetic-rule family. Random unseen seeds rule out memorizing complete sequences; they do not by themselves rule out a learned lookup table over the state pairs reachable under each trained delta.
2. The explicit-difference scaffold makes the desired circuit shorter and more legible, but it does **not** force delta-agnostic generalization. A Transformer can still copy a known delta and use a delta-specific value map, while modular addition with a held-out second operand remains OOD. “Must use the general add” becomes true only if the architecture constrains that operation, not merely because the tokens expose it.

My decision would therefore be:

- declare and preserve the order-1 seen-instance horizon milestone now;
- run the scaffold and long-training baseline as one tightly matched, bounded diagnostic pair;
- begin the continual-learning protocol without waiting for held-out-delta success, but label its first version an **instance-retention** experiment;
- do not claim retention of a general rule until held-out parameters also survive sequential acquisition.

## What the current result establishes—and what it does not

There are at least three distinct generalization axes here:

| Axis | Question | Current evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Horizon | Can a learned local transition remain correct beyond training length? | Yes for trained order-1 deltas, strongly so at `p=211` through `4H` |
| Parameter/rule-instance | Can the same mechanism apply to a delta absent from training? | No at both tested field sizes so far |
| Structural family | Can the learner acquire a different recurrence order or algorithm? | Not yet; order-2 at `p=211` did not converge and `p=23` was still running |

The first axis matters. Exact free-running continuation is more demanding than teacher-forced next-token accuracy, and the `p=211` result shows a stable iterated transition rather than whole-sequence replay. I would save the checkpoint, configuration, and result as a completed substrate milestone.

However, “unseen seed” is weaker than “unseen transition.” For fixed delta `d`, reachable ordered pairs lie on the line `a-b=d`. With enough enumerated seeds, training can cover most or all of that line even when the evaluation seed itself was withheld. The model may therefore have learned a closed transition table for each trained line. That is still genuine horizon extrapolation, but it is not evidence that the weights contain the shared law `2a-b`.

A useful audit is to report, for each evaluation rollout, the fraction of ordered pairs and local `W`-grams that occurred in training. If pair overlap is nearly complete, call the result **closed-loop execution on a learned transition graph**. If many evaluation pairs are new and exactness remains perfect, the algorithmic claim becomes stronger.

There is also a metric inconsistency to resolve before calling `p=23` perfect: `full-exact = 1.00` at length 48 and “first error at step 42” cannot both be literally true for the same evaluated population. It may only be rounding or a statistic pooled over different cases, but the report should include the exact numerator/denominator, at least four decimal places, and the definition of first-error-step. The `p=211` result is the cleaner headline anyway: length 48 is below the field period, so success cannot lean on having already cycled through the whole `p=23` orbit.

Finally, the original user-facing milestone included quadratic behavior. Arithmetic order-1 is therefore a completed sub-milestone, not completion of the entire stated bundle while order-2 remains unsolved.

## Q1: Is held-out-rule generalization the right bar?

It is the right bar for the statement “the model learned the rule family,” but not the only useful bar.

The honest deliverable today is:

> A 1.8M-parameter model trained on bounded-length examples can free-run trained arithmetic-recurrence instances to four times the training horizon from new seeds.

The claim I would not make is:

> The model inferred the general arithmetic recurrence and can instantiate it for a new delta.

That distinction is not cosmetic. For the project’s knowledge-into-weights objective, a finite catalogue of delta-conditioned transitions is knowledge in the weights, but it is a less scalable kind of knowledge than a shared recurrence program. As the number of rules grows, catalogue storage predicts linear capacity pressure; a shared law predicts reuse and better compute/parameter efficiency. Held-out-delta evaluation is therefore the experiment that distinguishes the two growth stories.

So: ship the narrow milestone, retain held-out-rule generalization as the stronger scientific target, and do not let the latter erase the value of the former.

## Q2: Which mechanism should be tested?

Run both, paired, but treat them as diagnostics for selection bias rather than an open-ended hyperparameter campaign.

### A. Long-training/weight-decay baseline at `p=23`

This asks whether optimization eventually selects a lower-complexity algebraic solution after fitting the training set. It is worth doing because it is cheap and gives a recognizable grokking-style learning curve. But the split is harder than the usual random equation split: an entire delta/operand value is absent, rather than random equations being held out while all residues remain represented. Failure after long training would not refute grokking in general; it would show that this architecture and objective did not cross this structured support gap.

Use multiple seeds, log train/seen/held metrics continuously after train loss saturates, and precommit a compute cap. The positive criterion should be a delayed, replicated rise in **full-exact held-delta rollout accuracy** without loss of seen accuracy—not a small token-accuracy drift.

### B. Explicit single-token difference scaffold

This is the more informative representation test and I also expect it to have the better chance. Use explicit token roles (value versus difference, or an equivalent role embedding) so the model is not also spending capacity discovering parity semantics. Match the baseline on training sequences, optimizer, parameter budget, total tokens, seeds, and evaluation pools.

But weaken the causal wording. The scaffold exposes this short program:

1. infer/copy `d_i` from the prefix;
2. compute `s_i = s_{i-1} + d_i`;
3. repeat.

It does not force that program. The second step still presents modular addition with a held-out operand, and the model can retain per-delta branches. If the scaffold succeeds, it is evidence that representation changed SGD’s preferred solution. If it fails, the result says token-level locality alone did not supply enough algebraic bias.

Add one split control that is more valuable than a large weight sweep:

- random held-out state-pairs with every delta represented;
- held-out entire deltas, as now.

If the first generalizes and the second fails, the boundary is specifically operand/support extrapolation. If both fail, the model has not learned modular addition robustly. If both pass only after long training, the grokking account gains support.

A fixed modular adder, exact copy head, or group-equivariant recurrence cell could make generalization structurally mandatory, but that should be an **oracle/control**, not the main knowledge-into-weights result. It moves part of the answer from learned weights into hand-coded computation. It is still useful to isolate whether the remaining hard problem is rule inference or arithmetic execution.

My stop rule: one matched scaffold-versus-baseline run family at `p=23`, enough seeds to reject a lucky run, and no broad sweep if held-out full-exact remains flat. A monotonic or uniformly zero result should trigger the continual-learning branch, not another 50 nearby settings.

## Q3: Start continual learning now, but name the first experiment correctly

Held-out-delta generalization is not a prerequisite for building and testing the sequential-acquisition machinery. It *is* a prerequisite for claiming that what survives is a general rule.

I would run two scoreboards from the start:

1. **Instance retention:** phase A trains delta set A; phase B trains disjoint delta set B with no A examples or replay; after B, evaluate exact rollouts on A and B trained deltas from unseen seeds.
2. **Family retention/generalization:** evaluate held-out deltas before and after B. This scoreboard may remain zero initially, but keeping it prevents catalogue retention from silently being renamed rule retention later.

The first protocol is scientifically useful even if the second is zero. It tests whether new transition knowledge can enter the weights without erasing old transition knowledge, under prefix-only selection. But delta-set A followed by delta-set B is a continual **catalogue expansion** task, not yet acquisition of qualitatively different algorithms. Order-2 must first become learnable before arithmetic-to-quadratic can serve as the stronger structural-acquisition test.

For the project’s main criteria, the present status is:

- **Knowledge into weights:** demonstrated narrowly for trained order-1 instances; the shared family law is not demonstrated.
- **Continual retention:** not tested yet; the A→B no-replay protocol is the next direct test.
- **Growth necessity / compute advantage:** not demonstrated. Compare fixed-capacity sequential learning with conditional parameter growth, reporting added parameters, phase-B training FLOPs/tokens, and inference FLOPs. Growth is justified only if a fixed model exhibits a reproducible stability–plasticity limit that growth improves at a worthwhile cost.
- **No-memory inference:** the current free-running, prefix-only setup qualifies operationally, provided there is no retrieved exemplar buffer or task ID. The live prefix is input context, not an external episodic memory.
- **No joint full retraining:** keep old phase data unavailable during acquisition. A joint model may be reported only as an offline diagnostic ceiling, never as the method that solves retention.

Be careful that growth does not degenerate into one module per phase plus an externally supplied task label. Routing must be inferred from the prefix, and the inference path must not search a stored training set. Otherwise retention is achieved by task isolation rather than integrated, no-memory continual learning.

## Recommended sequence

1. Freeze the order-1 `p=211` result as “trained-instance closed-loop horizon extrapolation to `4H`.”
2. Resolve the `p=23` exactness/first-error discrepancy and audit transition/`W`-gram overlap.
3. Finish the already-running order-2 `p=23` job, but do not tune order-2 indefinitely if it also collapses to a constant.
4. Run the matched `p=23` long-training baseline and difference scaffold, including the random-pair versus held-delta split control.
5. Regardless of that pair’s outcome, start A→B sequential acquisition with no replay and prefix-only routing; report instance retention and family generalization separately.
6. Introduce conditional growth only after the fixed-capacity baseline establishes a real stability–plasticity failure, then judge growth by retention, new acquisition, parameter/FLOP cost, and inference overhead together.

In short: the horizon wall is closed for trained arithmetic instances, and that is a valid result. The selection wall remains open. We should probe it once with a scaffold that shortens the desired circuit and a matched grokking baseline, while moving the actual continual-learning program forward in parallel. The only thing I would reject is using “seen-rule success” as evidence that a general rule was learned—or making held-out success a gate that postpones all continual-learning work.
