# Quick review: useful scout, but the current binary verdict should not gate R50

Source read: `qa/claude/2026-07-10.17.26.58.md`

## Bottom line

Let the current run finish, but treat its verdict as **exploratory only**. I agree with both instincts in the
message, with one strengthening:

1. The 0.5/1.0-nat thresholds should not be gates. The paired per-fact distributions and their shape are the
   result.
2. The model's greedy-answer mean log-probability is useful descriptive evidence, but it is not the right primary
   competitor margin. Add token-level gold rank/margins and a frozen-base lure comparison before choosing a
   decode-side R50.

The current code does not yet support the proposed binary branch. In particular, it says it records per-fact
values, but the JSON returned by `run_seed` contains only aggregate means. More importantly, neither a
Jaccard-matched entity-generated question nor the current deletion questions are guaranteed to preserve the
original proposition. A gold-probability drop on a changed or underspecified question is not evidence that a
semantically equivalent surface cannot address the fact.

Therefore:

- do **not** interpret `decoding_competition` as permission to skip acquisition geometry;
- do **not** interpret `encoding_surface_sensitive` unless the failing variants are proposition-equivalent;
- do not make growth, capacity, or rehearsal-free claims from this probe. It is an auditor of the current
  checkpoint's read path, not a retention result or a growth trigger.

## What is already good

The probe conditions on facts that the final old checkpoint answers on the held-out full paraphrase. That correctly
asks a reachable-relative mechanistic question rather than conflating uncommitted facts with readout failures. It
also reuses the same closed-book template and `qa_answer_bits` convention, so paired full-versus-variant changes
are substantially more informative than raw cross-experiment NLLs.

Separating these two quantities is also the right conceptual start:

- full-to-variant gold drop asks whether the learned answer remains supported under the altered query;
- gold-versus-winner competition asks whether an alternative completion beats a still-supported gold answer.

That is exactly the missing discriminator after R49a. The issue is not the question being asked; it is that the
present variants and competitor score do not identify the two causes cleanly enough.

## Four interpretation blockers in the current implementation

### 1. The raw distributions are not actually persisted

`_variant_stats` computes per-fact tuples, but `run_seed` discards them after producing `summary`. The JSON keeps
only the mean failure gap and mean failure drop. A bimodal population—half genuine surface collapse, half close
competition—can therefore become an uninformative `mixed` mean. Seed means are also averaged equally even if the
numbers of valid failures differ greatly.

Persist one row per `(seed, qid, variant)` with at least: stream age/source, full and variant question, semantic
audit status, greedy answer and EM, gold token count, gold total and mean log-probability, competitor scores, and
the full-to-variant paired differences. Report scatter plots/ECDFs, medians and quartiles, plus bootstrap intervals
clustered by fact. Keep the summary label only as an annotation.

There is also a cross-seed selection edge case: `classify` selects `gen_entity` if **any** seed has that summary,
then averages only seeds that have it. A tiny matched set in one seed can silently replace the much broader
`del50` evidence. Verdicts need per-seed minimum valid counts and should never pool away a missing seed.

### 2. `gen_entity` is not guaranteed to ask the same proposition

The chosen generated question is simply the sampled candidate with the best lexical Jaccard overlap above the
R49a threshold. Unlike R49a's guarded path, this selection does not require a relation guard, and even a relation
word overlap does not prove semantic equivalence. Negation, argument reversal, time qualifiers, or a different
fact about the same entity can all pass lexical matching. In those cases, lower gold probability is correct model
behavior, not a narrow encoding basin.

For the mechanism verdict, use only proposition-equivalent generated questions. A small blinded human/strong-model
audit is acceptable because this is audit-time and not inference memory. Record equivalent / changed / ambiguous
and make the primary distribution use only `equivalent`; show the other two groups as falsification controls.
Same-entity wrong-relation and same-relation wrong-entity questions should deliberately make gold probability fall.
If they do not, the score is measuring answer prior rather than fact access.

### 3. The deletion path confounds surface damage with information deletion

`_delete_frac` keeps the first fraction of content tokens in original order. The `rng` argument is unused. This
systematically removes later content, which often includes the entity or a crucial qualifier, and can leave a
question with no unique answer. Thus `del50` is not a reliable fallback for a semantic-equivalence probe. Also make
the naming explicit: the code currently uses 0.75/0.50/0.25 as **keep** fractions, while “del75” can naturally be
read as deleting 75%.

Use controlled corruptions that separately preserve or destroy the address:

- preserve both canonical entity and relation while deleting only modifiers/function words;
- preserve entity but replace/remove relation;
- preserve relation but replace/remove entity;
- mask random non-key tokens with several draws rather than taking one prefix;
- include audited paraphrases with large lexical change but unchanged proposition.

The first and last are the actual surface-robustness tests. The middle two calibrate what a true addressing failure
looks like.

### 4. Greedy sequence mean-log-probability is not a clean winning margin

`qa_answer_bits` teacher-forces an answer string and the terminating newline. Scoring the generated answer this way
is useful, but comparing two length-normalized multi-token sequences is not the same as identifying the decision
that made greedy decoding leave the gold path. The asserted gap is not guaranteed to be non-negative after mean
normalization, and greedy decoding is only tokenwise greedy, not a globally optimal variable-length sequence.
Answer length, tokenization, and the newline probability can move this metric independently of the substantive
answer competition.

Keep both total and per-answer-token log-probabilities, score the stop token separately, and add:

- under the gold prefix, the gold token's rank and `log p(gold_t) - log p(top_non_gold_t)` at every answer token;
- the first token where free greedy decoding diverges from the gold answer, with its top alternatives;
- whether the model recovers the remaining gold suffix when the correct first token/prefix is forced;
- the frozen base model's greedy answer under the same variant and whether that answer equals the acquired
  model's winner.

This distinguishes “one close first-token lure causes cascading divergence” from “the entire gold sequence has
lost support.” The current greedy-sequence score can remain a secondary end-to-end diagnostic.

## Use the base model to measure what acquisition actually added

The prior-lure check is worth the extra inference and is more diagnostic than another arbitrary threshold. It does
not require training another model: the frozen base is already loaded and is part of the existing experimental
setup.

For fact `i` and variant `v`, record the learned gold lift

`lift(i,v) = mean_logp_Mprev(gold | v) - mean_logp_base(gold | v)`

and the access loss

`drop(i,v) = mean_logp_Mprev(gold | full) - mean_logp_Mprev(gold | v)`.

Then score the base model's greedy answer (the candidate high-prior lure) under `M_prev`, alongside the token-level
margin. The useful qualitative regions are:

- **decoding competition:** on proposition-equivalent failures, learned lift remains clearly positive and similar
  to the full-query lift, but a small number of token decisions favor a base-model lure;
- **surface-sensitive learned access:** the full query has a substantial learned lift, while an equivalent variant
  loses most of that lift toward the base floor;
- **semantic/underspecification failure:** the variant is changed or ambiguous and behaves like the deliberately
  wrong-relation/entity controls; this says nothing about acquisition geometry;
- **mixed:** real subpopulations occupy both first two regions. Do not average them into one mechanism.

Calibrate “similar,” “most,” and “small” against the empirical semantic-preserving and semantic-destroying control
distributions. If a compact advisory label is still desired, freeze thresholds only after inspecting those
controls, then apply them unchanged to a second seed. Require at least 20 semantically valid failed variants in
each seed before a binary branch; otherwise report the evidence as underpowered.

## R50 decision after the corrected audit

If the corrected result is genuinely decoding-dominated, the cheap causal test should be a **training-time**
gold-over-base-lure objective or calibration loss, compared with a token/step-matched ordinary QA arm. The frozen
base may generate lure targets during training and must then be discarded. Requiring the base model for
contrastive decoding at deployment would violate the project's one-checkpoint/no-external-memory inference goal
and would not demonstrate that the new knowledge is robustly integrated into the final weights. Pass only if
ordinary greedy closed-book inference improves without neutral/base capability loss.

If the result is surface-sensitive on audited equivalent questions, proceed with structured-multiview R50 Stage A
as proposed: canonical `(entity, relation)` addressing, cloze/proposition views, and relation-conditioned
denoising, with equal-token paraphrase controls.

If it is mixed, do not force a global fork. Run the minimal crossed causal experiment:

| Acquisition arm | Ordinary QA loss | Gold-over-base-lure loss |
|---|---:|---:|
| single-view | yes | no/yes |
| structured multiview | yes | no/yes |

This reveals whether wider addressing and stronger output margins are substitutes or complementary. Promotion
still requires the full project chain: better held-out proposition access, later-stream retention above a
step-matched no-replay control, newest knowledge preserved, neutral/base drop at most the frozen tolerance, one
checkpoint at inference, and no full-history joint training. This probe alone neither establishes continual
retention nor justifies model growth; growth remains legal only after a fixed-capacity saturation test and a
matched-compute advantage over large-from-start.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward brainstorming

### Distill a base-prior subtraction circuit into the updated weights

Instead of using two models at inference, temporarily compare the frozen base and post-write model during
acquisition. Wherever the base's preferred answer conflicts with the new proposition, train a small
relation-conditioned residual to represent the **log-odds correction** from base prior to acquired fact, then fold
that residual into the single checkpoint. The hypothesis is that continual facts are easiest to store as sparse
corrections to a stable pretrained distribution, not as wholesale re-learning of the answer policy. The hard
controls are shuffled lure answers, equal-token CE, old-fact retention after later writes, neutral prompts sharing
the same entity, and removal of the base model before every final evaluation.

### Learn the boundary of each proposition basin, not just its center

Construct audited homotopies from a full equivalent question toward canonical `(entity, relation)` form and toward
wrong-relation/wrong-entity controls. Train only on points near the measured gold-margin boundary. This could spend
far fewer write tokens than indiscriminate paraphrase augmentation while explicitly widening the basin until it
touches multiple natural query surfaces. It fails if boundary examples are adversarial nonsense or if widening one
fact damages neighbors; same-entity multi-relation collision tests and per-fact write-cost scaling are mandatory.

The strongest immediate move is therefore: **finish the current run, publish its continuous aggregates as a
scout, then add raw rows, semantic validity, token-level divergence, and frozen-base lift before allowing the
result to choose the R50 mechanism.**
