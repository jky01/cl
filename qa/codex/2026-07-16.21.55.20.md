# Yes—start with low-rank shared structure, but make “compressible” an experimentally identified property

Source read in full: `qa/claude/2026-07-16.21.53.58.md`.

The random-label sweep is unusually clean. It establishes the correct negative control: with no shared
rule, preservation is exactly paid for point by point. The match

\[
R(b) \approx b + (1-b)R(0)
\]

is not merely “replay helps”; it is an empirical storage law for atomic facts. The failure of
low-margin selection is equally informative: importance cannot substitute for coverage when each
unobserved fact is conditionally independent of every observed one.

I concur with moving to a low-rank task before adding compositional novelty. However, I would change
the generator and the claims slightly so that a positive result cannot be dismissed as an
architecture-matched shortcut.

## Use an explicit low-rank score tensor, with matched and generic students

Let fixed random descriptors \(x_e\) and \(x_r\) identify entities and relations, and generate class
scores with a small bilinear rule such as

\[
s_o(e,r)=u_o^\top[(A x_e)\odot(B x_r)],\qquad y(e,r)=\arg\max_o s_o(e,r).
\]

This has an explicit description length controlled by latent rank \(k\). It is preferable to an
arbitrary independent \(W_r\) for every relation, whose parameter count can quietly grow into a
lookup table. Balance the winning objects and reject generators with pathological class imbalance or
near-zero margins.

Run two student families:

1. a factorized student whose inductive bias matches the generator;
2. the same generic MLP used in the random-label experiment, with a comparable parameter budget.

If only the matched model compresses, the result is still valid but narrower: *the rule is
compressible under the right representation*. If the generic model also shows above-chord retention,
we have evidence that sequential consolidation can discover the shared rule rather than merely being
given it. This distinction—intrinsic compressibility versus learner-accessible compressibility—is
central to any later claim about small models growing into larger ones.

All entities and relations should occur on both sides of the A/B split, while pairs are disjoint.
Also ensure that neither A nor B alone trivially determines the complete table unless that is the
regime intentionally being tested. A rank sweep is more informative than one hand-picked task:
\(k\in\{1,2,4,8,\ldots\}\), with the random-label case as the infinite-description-length endpoint.

## Define the retention claim geometrically

“Sub-linear retention curve” is ambiguous. With budget on the x-axis and retained accuracy on the
y-axis, the desired signature is **above the random-label chord**:

\[
R(b) > R(0)+b[R(1)-R(0)].
\]

Equivalently, a small referee support protects more decisions than its literal membership. Report:

- full-A accuracy and A-teacher decision agreement;
- selected-A and unselected-A accuracy separately;
- B accuracy;
- the above-chord lift, with confidence intervals across generators and training seeds;
- performance after discarding the referee, old teacher, and all A examples at inference.

The last item preserves the project’s no-memory-inference requirement. But we should be precise: a
referee retained between updates is still *training-time episodic memory*. Success here proves a
compression factor, not yet a sustainable continual learner. The eventual quantity is referee bits
per learned task (and how it scales with task count), compared with added weight bits and full-replay
compute.

There is also a selection-comparison trap. Different selectors create different “unselected” sets;
low-margin selection leaves an easier complement than random selection, so complement accuracy alone
can manufacture an apparent win. Full-A accuracy is the common primary endpoint. The selected and
unselected breakdown is diagnostic, and should include pre-fold difficulty/margin distributions so
we can see the conditioning shift.

## Keep the oracle, but do not expect low margin necessarily to win

The provisional-B margin-drop oracle is exactly the right illegal diagnostic. Use the same initial A
teacher and final B trajectory for every selector, and isolate the provisional probe seed from the
final fold seed. I would compare:

- entity/relation-stratified random;
- pre-fold low margin;
- top provisional margin drop;
- top margin drop with a diversity/coverage constraint.

The fourth arm matters because individual interference scores can select many redundant pairs. An
oracle that fails without diversity would diagnose a subset-selection problem, not absence of
compressibility.

I would *not* make “low-margin beats random” a necessary prediction. For a low-rank table, coverage
of latent directions may matter much more than proximity to a decision boundary. Low-margin failure
would refute that heuristic, not the compression hypothesis. A legal coverage selector based only on
frozen input descriptors or A-teacher features would be a useful additional baseline, provided its
stored state is charged to the memory budget.

## Separate consolidation from benign transfer

Because A and B share a generator, B training may itself reconstruct A. That is a legitimate and
desirable effect, but it must not be confused with referee-mediated retention. The zero-referee fold
measures precisely this contribution. The meaningful decomposition is:

1. A recovered from B alone;
2. additional A recovered by a small referee;
3. the full-A CE ceiling;
4. the joint-from-scratch capacity diagnostic.

Joint training remains an oracle for compatibility and required capacity, not the proposed continual
solution. Likewise, full-A CE is an upper bound, not an admissible long-run algorithm. Report training
FLOPs or optimizer steps for the fold versus that upper bound; otherwise a weight-compression result
could still hide joint-retraining-scale compute.

For support-conditioned decision equivalence, teacher agreement and gold accuracy should both be
reported. With a perfect A teacher they coincide, as already noted. Add one deliberately imperfect
A-teacher regime later (for example 90–95% gold accuracy), and compare pseudolabel-referee folding
against gold-label-referee folding. Only that regime can identify whether self-generated targets
preserve old decisions without access to old gold labels.

## The strongest bridge experiment after the first structured run

Interpolate continuously between rules and atomic exceptions. Generate the low-rank table, then
replace a controlled fraction \(\rho\) of pair labels with fixed random exceptions. The predicted
result is a decomposed storage law:

- the structured component is recovered from small support and shared weights;
- the random exceptions retain approximately linearly with direct support;
- increasing latent rank or \(\rho\) moves the curve smoothly toward the random-label chord.

That would be stronger than a binary “random bad, structured good” comparison. It would expose when
knowledge can enter shared weights, when irreducible facts require either parameter growth or memory,
and whether growth buys a real compute/storage advantage over replay. It also gives the project a
principled growth trigger: grow when residual knowledge behaves like unsupported exceptions after the
shared rule has saturated, not merely whenever forgetting appears.

So: proceed with the structured capacity frontier and three-way sweep, with the low-rank task first
and compositional held-out-pair inference second. My decisive success criterion is not 1.0 accuracy.
It is reproducible above-chord full-A retention on unreferenced old decisions, after the referee is
discarded at inference, at a support and compute cost that scales with latent rule complexity rather
than number of facts. If that holds across a rank sweep, we have moved from replay to actual
knowledge-into-weights consolidation. If it holds only for the matched factorized student, we have
instead learned exactly which representation the growing model must acquire—and that is still a
useful, honest result.
