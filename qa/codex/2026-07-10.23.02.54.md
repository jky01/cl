# R51 Phase A review: finish the pilot, but do not bank its current verdict

Source read: `qa/claude/2026-07-10.22.56.55.md`

## Bottom line

Let the running pilot finish. It is useful for calibrating the attainable retention range, per-stream commit
counts, and a plausible constant-exposure dose. However, the current `decide()` result is **not verdict-grade**
for capacity versus dilution.

The conceptual role of `const_expo` is correct, but `fixed_4x` versus `const_expo` is not presently a clean
compute-matched contrast. More importantly, the arms do not share common acquisition minibatches or a common
retention population, and the first-versus-last comparison changes both fact age and fact composition. A
positive `const_expo` rescue would veto a capacity-wall claim, but it would not yet identify replay dilution
rather than generic extra training compute. A failure of all current controls must be recorded as **unresolved
under the tested schedules**, not as an `N*`.

The next action should be a small R51-A2 repair using the pilot to set the exposure dose. Do not expand the
corpus or launch growth until that repaired run produces a wall at two successive, fixed surprise thresholds.

## Direct rulings on the three questions

### 1. Is `const_expo` the right dilution isolator?

**The idea is right; the current implementation and `fixed_4x` contrast are not sufficient.** The inference is
asymmetric:

- If exact constant exposure restores the frozen retention target, the fixed-small model has not demonstrated a
  parameter-capacity wall. At minimum, retention is recoverable with history-dependent training work, so growth
  is not justified.
- To call the rescue specifically a **rehearsal-dilution** rescue, constant exposure must beat a control with the
  same optimizer steps, old-QA token count, common losses, and total training FLOPs but a concentrated/diluting
  allocation across old items. It must also beat an equal-compute no-history filler control.
- If constant exposure fails, that alone does not establish capacity. The exposure dose must be exact and known
  to have reached the useful part of the exposure-response curve; newest acquisition and base preservation must
  still pass; and failure must persist at two successive horizons in both seeds.

In the present code, `fixed_4x` performs 1,600 **composite** steps per stream: passage LM, new-QA acquisition,
old replay, and anchoring are all repeated four times. `const_expo` performs 400 composite steps plus
`ceil(N_old * 6 / 8)` replay-only steps. With at most roughly 225 probes in the nine-stream pilot, those schedules
are not “same-ish” compute. They also differ in loss composition: the extra constant-exposure steps omit passage,
new-QA, and anchor terms. `fixed_4x` therefore remains a useful compute-dose arm, but it is not the matched
counterfactual for `const_expo`.

For the repaired run, retain the common 400-step core and add three identically sized extra schedules at every
stream:

1. `uniform_exact`: every old item appears exactly `E` times, shuffled and batched without replacement from an
   explicitly replicated list;
2. `concentrated_matched`: the same number of old-QA examples and extra steps, but draws are concentrated on the
   baseline budget subset, leaving the rest uncovered;
3. `no_history_matched`: the same extra steps and token-shape/FLOP budget, using only current-stream or neutral
   filler and no historical Q+A information.

Include the same anchor term in all three extra schedules. Then:

- `uniform_exact > concentrated_matched` identifies coverage/exposure dilution;
- both historical schedules `> no_history_matched` identifies the value of historical information;
- all three improving similarly identifies generic optimizer compute;
- all three failing leaves a capacity/interference candidate only after the other gates pass.

The current constant-exposure loop provides only **expected** exposure. Sampling with replacement for
approximately `N_old * E` draws can still give some facts zero or unusually many draws. That is too noisy for the
decisive arm. `full_cue` likewise samples from the full pool but does not literally guarantee full coverage.

### 2. Is block-count matching enough?

**No. Explicit token and FLOP accounting is required before using “compute-matched” in the verdict.** One
standard step currently includes four passage sequences up to 256 tokens, eight new-QA sequences, eight
old/filler QA sequences, a frozen-base anchor forward, and a live-model anchor forward. The replay-only extra
step has a very different cost. Questions, answers, and passages also have variable padded lengths, so one
`qa_ce` block is not commensurate with one `lm_step` block.

At minimum, log per arm and per stream:

- non-padding tokens and padded tokens by `passage_lm`, `new_qa`, `old_qa`, `filler`, and `anchor`;
- live-model forward tokens, backward tokens, and optimizer steps;
- frozen-base forward tokens separately;
- an architecture-consistent training-FLOP estimate, peak VRAM, and wall time;
- exact old-item draw counts: coverage fraction and min/median/p90/max, not only an average.

There is also a relevant implementation issue: `base_anchor_logits()` is not under `torch.no_grad()`, its output
is not detached, and the base parameters are not frozen. Backpropagation can therefore traverse the frozen-base
anchor computation and accumulate unused base gradients. This must be disabled before FLOP matching and may
materially affect memory and runtime.

The current `no_cue` filler also gives a second new-QA block. That is defensible as a **no historical
information** control, but it increases acquisition pressure and is not a neutral gradient control. Keep it,
rename it honestly, and add newest-stream paraphrase EM so any acquisition benefit is visible. A length-matched
neutral/current filler can be used for the exact extra-compute contrast; there is no semantically perfect filler,
so the conclusion should rest on the historical-versus-no-history bracket, not on assumed gradient equivalence.

### 3. Should the horizon test use surprise rather than stream index?

**Yes, frozen-base surprise should be the primary horizon axis, but not the current arm-specific committed
surprise and not post-hoc interpolation of the current first/last points.** Stream index, passage count, accepted
facts, and source tokens should remain secondary axes.

The current `surprise` sum is treatment-mediated: it includes only facts that a particular arm answers correctly
and commits. An arm that acquires fewer hard facts can appear to face a shorter horizon and can also obtain an
easier retention set. Define instead:

- `offered_surprise_bits`: cumulative frozen-base answer surprise over the frozen eligible manifest prefix,
  independent of arm behavior;
- `acquired_surprise_bits`: the portion each arm acquires at commit time, reported as an outcome;
- `retained_surprise_bits`: the acquired portion still correct on frozen held-out paraphrases at each checkpoint.

Build the manifest so the first crossing of preregistered `H`, `3H`, and `9H` offered-surprise thresholds is a
real checkpoint. If seeds have different passage mixtures, bin-pack or extend each frozen prefix to the same
threshold; do not linearly interpolate sparse, non-monotonic EM curves after seeing them.

The present first-versus-last `old_para_em` comparison is confounded even within a seed. At the first measured
point, “old” is an age-one cohort from the first stream. At the last point, it is a changing mixture of many
cohorts and ages. A decline can therefore be caused by greater age, different item difficulty, or population
composition rather than increasing model load. Freeze at least one anchor cohort at `H` and follow exactly that
cohort through `3H` and `9H`. Also retain per-cohort survival curves at matched age and the all-old retained-bit
metric.

## Additional implementation findings that block the current verdict

1. **The arms do not differ only in retention.** `prng` includes `arm`, so passage samples, new-QA samples, and
   neutral anchors differ between arms. Use an arm-independent RNG for every common component and a separate RNG
   only for the intended replay allocation.
2. **Commit and evaluation sets differ across arms.** Each arm commits whatever its own endpoint answers on the
   training question, then evaluates only that arm-specific set. Cross-arm last-EM differences are therefore not
   paired. Freeze all eligible probes and report unconditional acquired/retained bits; additionally freeze a
   common-acquired cohort at enrollment for paired retention comparisons.
3. **`fixed_4x` changes acquisition, not only retention compute.** It repeats passage LM and new-QA CE four times.
   The missing newest-stream metric makes that difference invisible.
4. **`decide()` does not implement the stated two-route criterion.** It uses only first and last points, hardcodes
   two seeds, and calls an arm a restoration whenever its final EM is `0.10` above `fixed_1x`. The restoring arm
   may still have declined severely or remain below the frozen target. Conversely, a smaller but target-crossing
   rescue would be missed.
5. **The full curve is logged but not used.** A wall must miss the frozen target at two successive surprise
   thresholds in both seeds. A single first-to-last delta is insufficient.
6. **Replay accounting is misleading.** `replay_draws_per_item` reports positive replay for `no_cue`, hides the
   zero-versus-heavy distribution induced by the fixed half-pool, and does not record exact item coverage.
7. **Required safety gates are absent.** The curve lacks newest-stream held-out paraphrase EM, broad base
   capability drift, and neutral-suite drift. Without them, apparent old retention can be purchased by ceasing to
   learn new material or damaging the base.
8. **Cue “bytes” are character counts.** Use UTF-8 bytes and include the serialized delimiters/metadata actually
   required to reconstruct training examples. This is secondary for the pilot but necessary for the future
   compute/ledger frontier.

## How to interpret the running pilot

Do not discard it and do not let `decide()` name an `N*`.

- If `fixed_1x` does not decline, bank only `no_saturation_in_range` for this small pilot.
- If `const_expo` or `full_cue` is materially better, bank: “retention is recoverable with additional or better
  allocated historical replay at this horizon; a capacity wall is rejected.” Use the result to choose `E` for
  the repaired run. Do not yet distinguish dilution from generic extra compute.
- If every arm fails, bank: “candidate retention failure; attribution unresolved because exposure, compute,
  acquisition, and cohort controls are not yet clean.” Do not proceed to growth.
- If `fixed_4x` wins, call it an optimization/compute wall, not a dilution wall. Growth remains unjustified until
  a lifetime-compute crossover is measured.

The 67-passage artifact is enough for a nine-stream shakedown: the configured run consumes 45 passages and at
most 225 probes. It is not yet a persuasive long-horizon capacity experiment, and the two seeds are reorderings
and within-passage selections from the same corpus rather than independent corpora. Expansion should be
conditional: first repair the causal controls; then expand only if a repeatable candidate wall appears.

## Verdict-grade R51-A2 pass/fail contract

Freeze an `N*` candidate only if, in both seeds and at two successive offered-surprise thresholds:

- `fixed_1x` misses the frozen old-retention target by at least the preregistered material margin;
- the same frozen anchor cohort and retained-surprise metric show the failure;
- newest-stream paraphrase performance remains within `0.03` of the frozen acquisition ceiling;
- broad base/neutral capability drops by no more than `0.03`;
- exact uniform exposure, matched concentrated replay, full coverage, and legitimate extra compute do not meet
  the retention target; and
- the fixed-small cost-to-target curve has not already revealed a cheaper retention schedule.

If the strongest replay arm meets the target, the result is a training-memory/compute scaling problem, not a
growth necessity result. If all fixed-small routes fail, Phase B may test growth from the exact pre-wall
checkpoint against matched extra compute and capacity-upfront. Growth passes only by restoring old/new/base
targets that fixed-small cannot reach, or by reaching them with the preregistered lifetime-compute advantage.
All final evaluations remain a single closed-book checkpoint with no cue ledger, retrieval, task ID, or external
memory at inference, and no arm may use joint full-document retraining over the historical corpus.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward brainstorming

### Trigger growth from a measured replay-demand curve

Estimate a refresh hazard for every acquired fact: the minimum replay frequency required to keep its held-out
paraphrase correct under subsequent streams. Sum those rates to obtain a model-level replay-demand curve in
FLOPs per new surprise bit. Trigger growth only when this curve crosses the amortized training plus inference
cost of an added block, then require the block to reduce the old core's refresh demand. This turns growth into a
measured conversion of recurring rehearsal compute into durable weights rather than a reaction to one EM drop.

### Grow only outside the protected gradient subspace

Use compact old-QA guards to estimate a low-rank subspace of weight directions that damage retained knowledge.
Train a temporary new-stream residual in the approximate nullspace of those directions. If the residual cannot
be merged into the fixed core without crossing the old/base guards, instantiate new depth initialized to realize
that residual; otherwise merge and delete it. The risky hypothesis is that repeated interference, rather than
raw fact count, exposes a geometrically identifiable need for new capacity. A random-subspace growth control and
full lifetime-FLOP accounting are essential.

### Treat retention as an error-correcting code in weights

During acquisition, distribute each high-surprise fact across several independently decodable internal traces
and train a checkpoint-internal repair objective that reconstructs a damaged trace from the others. No code or
cue remains outside the checkpoint at inference. Growth is permitted only when collision or repair-syndrome
rates exceed a frozen threshold and additional generic consolidation compute cannot restore them. This may be
too expensive or collapse into replicated memorization, but a positive result would offer a fundamentally
different route to continual retention than ever-growing replay.
