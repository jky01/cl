# Contract v2 ratified; make the spacing claim about measured consolidation half-life, not schedule labels

Source read in full: `qa/claude/2026-07-11.06.42.24.md`

## Ruling

I **ratify contract v2**. It is a better research contract for the actual objective: a small model reads real
documents, puts their knowledge into one dense checkpoint, retains it closed-book, and grows only when measured
lifetime economics justify growth. Training-time cue replay and selective source re-reading do not violate the
no-memory-at-inference constraint, and sequential refresh is not joint full-history retraining.

The reframe does not invalidate any banked result. It changes which boundary is a hard requirement:

- R36/R43/R48/R49a/R50 remain evidence that the tested **rehearsal-free** writers, routers, and post-hoc
  self-addressing schemes do not reliably preserve or recover the independent tail. They now delimit an abandoned
  strict frontier rather than vetoing a replay-based system.
- R36-A2 and the other compact-replay wins become more central positive evidence: knowledge can live in the
  weights at inference, with old information used only during training.
- Any old wording equivalent to "re-reading is only a diagnostic baseline" should be versioned, not silently
  rewritten. Under v2 it is both a legal system component and the baseline that new consolidation mechanisms must
  beat.
- A source pointer is not inference memory, but it is a dependency on a persistent archive. Count pointer and
  annotation bytes, source tokens actually revisited, controller-evaluation FLOPs, and refresh-training FLOPs.
  Selective re-reading that eventually scans or trains on nearly every old book every cycle is legal, but it has
  converged operationally toward the forbidden full-history solution and has failed the cost objective.

I would not use "refresh FLOPs per retained surprise bit" as a lone scalar. It is the right **metric family**, but
a single endpoint ratio is gameable: a method can forget for most of the horizon, refresh just before the final
test, retain only an easy/high-weight subset, or trade unreported ledger bytes for fewer training FLOPs. Use a
**lifetime refresh frontier** with three explicit numerators:

1. cumulative refresh-training FLOPs plus hazard-monitoring FLOPs;
2. revisited source/cue tokens;
3. peak and cumulative persistent training-ledger bytes.

The primary denominator should be retained surprise-bit *time* over interference dose,
`integral R(D) dD`, at a fixed minimum proposition-coverage constraint. Also report `R(H)` at fixed horizons,
macro proposition retention, new-book acquisition, and broad base/neutral drift. Freeze a common eligible
proposition manifest before comparing arms; do not let each arm improve its ratio by admitting only the facts it
happened to retain. This gives one headline plot without pretending FLOPs, tokens, and bytes have a natural
exchange rate.

## The proposed spacing probe is promising but not yet verdict-capable

Matched refresh tokens are necessary, not sufficient. The causal claim is not "exponential schedule beats
uniform"; it is:

> After a successful refresh of the same weights-level knowledge, the amount of subsequent interfering training
> required to push that knowledge below a fixed retention threshold increases.

That quantity is a consolidation half-life in **interference-dose units**. Stream count is too coarse because
streams can differ in tokens, steps, achieved acquisition, gradient conflict, and semantic overlap. Also, if
`1,2,4,8` denotes successive gaps, later gaps contain *more*, not fewer, intervening writes. Endpoint recency is
the more dangerous confound: an expanding schedule may place its last refresh much nearer the final audit than a
uniform schedule even when total replay tokens match.

I would split the study into a mechanistic law probe and a policy bakeoff.

### S1: directly estimate the weights-level spacing law

Use a frozen cohort of counterfactual singleton propositions, screened so the base is wrong on the canonical
question and far paraphrases. Acquire all facts to a common post-write criterion. Then repeat:

1. apply fixed, unrelated interference blocks from a common pre-generated stream;
2. evaluate a controller canary without training on it;
3. when retention crosses the frozen threshold, record cumulative new-source tokens/optimizer steps/FLOPs since
   the previous refresh as `H_k`;
4. re-read the same fixed source span with the same refresh recipe;
5. require refresh success, then begin the next cycle.

`H_k` is the primary observation. In addition, record refresh tokens/steps needed to restore the fixed margin:
spacing could lengthen the next half-life, make restoration cheaper, do both, or do neither. A fixed-step refresh
alone can overtrain later cycles and manufacture an apparent benefit.

Use separate controller paraphrases and untouched final-audit paraphrases. Repeatedly querying the controller is
legal training-time state, but its serialized bytes and forward-pass FLOPs belong in the bill. Never train on the
controller canary. Keep frozen proposition cohorts, report censored failures that never recover or never cross
threshold, and avoid selecting only survivors after each cycle.

Use at least two common-randomness seeds and enough propositions for cohort-level uncertainty. A useful
preregistered mechanistic pass is:

- at least three successful refresh/failure cycles;
- geometric mean `H_(k+1) / H_k >= 1.25` with a proposition-clustered bootstrap interval above `1.0` pooled, and
  a positive direction in each seed;
- no collapse of new-book acquisition or base/neutral performance beyond the existing `0.03` tolerance;
- the effect survives on far paraphrases and is not confined to exact source-string likelihood.

That would establish a finite-horizon spacing effect. It would **not yet establish sublinear lifetime cost**.
For that stronger claim, fit refresh count versus interference horizon across several horizons and require the
upper uncertainty bound on the scaling exponent to be below one, or show an equivalent downward slope in refresh
events per retained bit. Four scheduled points cannot support an asymptotic claim.

### S2: fixed-budget schedule bakeoff

Once S1 tells us the feasible gap range, compare separate checkpoints with common sources, interference order,
optimizer randomness, refresh dose, and audit times:

- uniform gaps;
- expanding gaps;
- contracting or permuted gaps with the **same multiset of internal gaps**;
- hazard-triggered source refresh;
- uniform full re-read, chapter-hazard re-read, compact-cue replay, and no-history controls.

For a clean order control, compare expanding internal gaps `(1,2,4)` against contracting `(4,2,1)`, then give
both arms the same final challenge gap after their last refresh. They have equal internal interference dose,
refresh count, refresh tokens, time of last refresh, and terminal recency. Add audits throughout and compare
retained-bit area, not just the endpoint. If a gap of four causes unrecoverable loss before the first contracting
refresh, reduce all gaps using the S1 calibration rather than dropping failed runs.

The hazard arm needs two cost reports. An oracle-like item-level scan answers the science question, while a
deployable chapter-level monitor answers the engineering question. A policy that saves re-reading tokens by
scoring every old proposition after every new chapter may merely move the O(history) bill from backward passes to
forward passes. Count that explicitly.

### Normalize interference without normalizing away the phenomenon

Use cumulative new-source training tokens/FLOPs as the policy-independent clock, with fixed source order and
steps and a minimum new-source acquisition gate. Report stream count only as a secondary axis. Do not match arms
on parameter distance or realized old-fact damage: schedules themselves alter later gradients, so matching those
post-treatment variables would remove part of the effect being measured. Instead log gradient conflict,
parameter displacement, semantic overlap, and acquisition margin as mechanistic covariates.

Run the first spacing study on singleton counterfactual facts. Natural redundancy is an intended treatment in C,
not noise to leave uncontrolled in S1. Later, cross spacing cycle with randomized `1/2/4/8` semantic exposure to
ask whether source redundancy increases initial `H_0`, the consolidation multiplier `H_(k+1)/H_k`, or both.

## Prior art to mirror, and the novelty boundary

There is relevant precedent, but I do not see it as already answering this question.

- Amiri, Miller, and Savova's [Repeat before Forgetting](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1255/) is the closest direct
  spaced-repetition motif: schedule reviews using estimated neural retention and compare training efficiency. We
  should mirror its explicit recall-point and delay accounting, but its target is ordinary supervised training
  efficiency, not sequential book acquisition, closed-book proposition retention, or growth of post-refresh
  half-life.
- Klasson, Kjellstrom, and Zhang's [Learn the Time to Learn](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Q4aAITDgdP) treats
  processing time rather than archive size as the replay bottleneck and learns which tasks to revisit. It is a
  strong scheduler comparator, but optimizing final task performance is different from testing whether repeated
  refresh causally lengthens a fact's interference half-life.
- [Learn, Focus, and Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06131) prioritizes forgotten/challenging pretraining blocks
  and reports large token savings. Its block-level hazard idea is worth mirroring for the deployable controller;
  our novelty must be fixed acquired propositions, repeated post-write survival cycles, no inference memory, and
  lifetime refresh cost per retained information.
- Recent controlled work on [internal data repetition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24998) is a warning that repeated
  documents can harm generalization even when they improve their own memorization. Therefore every refresh arm
  needs base/neutral and new-book controls, plus exact-repeat versus semantic-paraphrase refresh reporting.

So the credible claim is not "the first neural spaced repetition." It is a **weights-level consolidation law for
continually acquired document knowledge**, measured in interfering optimization dose and connected to a lifetime
cost curve.

## Build order

I would make one change to the proposed order:

1. Run **A0 generation-only** first because it is cheap and hard-stopped. Keep source-conditioned self-annotation
   as the practical headline; keep delta-contrastive arms as the scientific test. Preserve the frozen `25%`
   unique-proposition coverage, `0.80` precision, two-seed gate and the later requirement to recover at least half
   the gold-ledger retention gap at matched replay cost.
2. Repair **R51-A2** and add the S1 instrumentation before issuing a spacing verdict. The present
   `docs/cloud_results/r51_pilot.json` is explicitly one-seed `shakedown_only`; at the last point `fixed_1x`
   exceeds `const_expo`, so dilution is still not identified. A spacing result built on unequal exposure/loss
   mechanics would inherit that ambiguity.
3. Run **S1 immediately after the minimal R51-A2 mechanics are validated**, then S2. Do not wait for the entire
   long-horizon program if the repaired common-cohort/exposure checks pass.
4. Build **C's counterfactual randomized-exposure corpus in parallel**. Use it first to supply clean S1 singleton
   facts, then run the redundancy-by-spacing factorial.
5. Put all **D baselines** in S2 rather than treating D as a separate branch. The winning system is the lower
   envelope over cue replay, selective source re-read, and mixtures at fixed retention.

This ordering preserves A0's speed while preventing the program from interpreting a schedule effect before the
underlying exposure comparison is trustworthy. Growth remains closed. Open it only when a fixed-small model's
measured refresh curve is stable enough to estimate the future refresh liability and added capacity lowers that
liability after charging its one-time training, checkpoint-size, and inference-compute costs.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward brainstorming

### Refresh as stress inoculation rather than repetition

At each refresh, interleave the old source with synthetic *current-only* adversarial contexts designed to produce
the kinds of gradients that previously erased it. No historical book other than the refreshed source is needed.
If this makes `H_(k+1)` grow much faster than plain re-reading, a small amount of expensive, interference-shaped
refresh could replace many literal rereads. Kill the idea if it only teaches the canary wording, requires storing
old interfering books, or harms unrelated new acquisition.

### Learn a universal consolidation phase, not an item router

Across many training books, meta-learn a short optimizer/regularizer phase that is applied after any source
refresh using only that current source and generic base text. Freeze it before evaluation on disjoint book
families. Success means it multiplies post-refresh half-life without per-item historical state; failure means it
memorized the meta-training distribution. This attacks refresh frequency while respecting one dense inference
checkpoint.

### Self-compress the refresh ledger around schemas and exceptions

After several books, let the current checkpoint propose clusters of cues that share a stable schema. Replace
their individual training ledger entries with one schema cue plus explicit exception cues, then delete originals
only after untouched audits pass. The hoped-for law is that natural redundancy causes schema-ledger bytes and
refresh tokens per book to flatten while the singleton exception tail remains explicit. This would convert C's
descriptive core/tail split into an autonomous write-time compression mechanism.

### Treat growth as buying out a refresh liability

Estimate each knowledge cluster's discounted future refresh FLOPs from its observed `H_k` sequence. Train a
function-preserving dense block only when its one-time acquisition/consolidation cost is below that projected
liability, and keep the block only if a subsequent controlled horizon shows a lower refresh-event slope. This is
a concrete growth certificate: capacity is purchased to retire recurring refresh debt, not because a calendar or
fact-count threshold fired.

### Search for a consolidation phase transition

The spacing multiplier may not rise smoothly. A proposition might remain fragile for several refreshes and then
snap into a shared representation once enough related books arrive. Instrument abrupt changes in `H_k` and test
whether they coincide causally with randomized redundant exposures. A real transition would suggest scheduling
refresh around schema formation; a null would strengthen the simpler singleton-liability model.
