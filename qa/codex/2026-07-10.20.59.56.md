## Bottom line

Your concern in (1) is correct. The implemented

```text
log p_M(q_i | q_<i) + lambda [log p_M(q_i | q_<i) - log p_S(q_i | q_<i)]
```

is a valid expert/anti-expert decoder for finding **question continuations whose surface distribution changes**
between `M_prev` and the shadow. It is not an estimator of damage to the answer conditional
`p(answer | question)`. A future write can alter question wording while preserving the answer, or leave the
question distribution stable while destroying the answer. Top-p masking improves fluency but does not repair
that causal mismatch.

Let the current run finish and preserve its raw rows, because it can still reveal parse-rate, diversity, and
surface-divergence behavior. However, label `shadow_contrastive` a **question-surface pilot** and do not allow
`decide()` to issue `tomography_promising`, `all_history_free_null`, or a branch decision from it. A positive is
not evidence that answer damage located an old fact; a null does not reject answer-damage tomography.

The frozen instruct proposer is a useful policy baseline, but the present run is not yet verdict-grade for that
arm either: attempts are matched while generated tokens/FLOPs are not; duplicate/template concentration is not
gated; there is no frozen-instruct answerer contamination readout; and the policy gate does not explicitly enforce
the previously specified minimum of four diverse unique propositions per seed.

## 1. Correct the search target: propose questions, then score their answers

The clean Stage-A mechanism is a two-stage, history-free search:

1. Generate a **shared candidate question pool** from generic sources: passive `M_prev`, frozen instruct, and
   optionally generic mutations. No proposer sees an old question, answer, entity, relation, title, qid, passage,
   stream label, or hidden-gold signal.
2. For each candidate `q`, have `M_prev` greedily produce a short self-answer `a_M(q)`.
3. Teacher-force that same answer under both checkpoints and compute

   ```text
   D_real(q) = mean_logp_Mprev(a_M | q) - mean_logp_Sreal(a_M | q).
   ```

   Also record answer flip, pre-write answer confidence, answer length, and the corresponding scores under every
   control shadow.
4. Rank the shared pool by `D_real`, admit only fluent natural questions with confident and stable pre-write
   answers, and use historical signatures, equivalence judgments, gold answers, and threatened labels **only in
   the offline audit after ranking**.

This directly asks whether the impending write damages the answer behavior elicited by a candidate. It remains
history-free at discovery time and uses the checkpoint's own answer as the search target, so it does not leak the
historical answer. In the offline audit, a counted knowledge hit still needs proposition equivalence, acquired
holder correctness, base failure/acquired lift, and uniqueness.

Use the **same candidate pool for `S_real`, `S_wrong`, a different-stream shadow, and `S_identity = M_prev`**.
Otherwise each shadow changes both which questions are sampled and how they are scored, so a coverage difference
cannot be attributed to damage ranking. First test `coverage@K` and threatened enrichment at fixed `K`; only if
reranking works should you spend compute on an adaptive generator that mutates questions using `D_real` as a
black-box reward.

Two stability filters are worth adding before an answer becomes a replay target:

- require `M_prev` to give the same normalized answer under at least two generic paraphrases of the candidate;
- require the real-shadow score to remain positive across those views rather than being driven by one exact
  prompt surface.

The paraphraser may see the generated candidate, because that is transient search state, but not hidden history.
Save all rejected and admitted candidates so this filter can be audited for selection bias.

### The decision statistic must actually measure tomography

The current `decide()` calls a run `tomography_promising` from **correct answerable coverage**, even though the
mechanism is supposed to target threatened facts. That statistic can establish history-free address discovery,
but not threat localization. Keep two distinct outcomes:

- `address_discovery_signal`: unique proposition-equivalent, acquired-specific correct facts found at a matched
  budget;
- `tomography_signal`: the top-`K` real-shadow ranking is enriched for realized-damaged facts relative to random,
  passive score, identity shadow, shuffled-answer shadow, and matched different-stream shadow in every seed.

If either seed has fewer than 20 threatened facts, report exact counts and intervals and call the tomography gate
underpowered. Do not replace it with answerable coverage and retain the same mechanism label. A larger audit
universe or more transitions is preferable to drawing a threat-targeting conclusion from roughly nine facts.

Stage A is still only discovery. Promotion requires an end-to-end consolidation experiment: replay selected
`M_prev` answers into `M_t`, delete candidates and shadows, and beat compute-matched no-replay, random-question,
passive, and oracle-upper-bound controls on old held-out paraphrase retention while preserving new-stream learning
and neutral/base capability. That is the step that advances durable knowledge-into-weights with checkpoint-only
inference; no full-history joint retraining is needed.

## 2. Acquired lift: keep the original base, but strengthen the gate

There are three different reference comparisons and they should not be called by the same name:

| Question | Correct comparison |
|---|---|
| Was this behavior acquired after initialization? | acquired `M_prev` versus original frozen base `B0` |
| Is this behavior threatened by the impending write? | `M_prev` versus `S_real` |
| Was it actually forgotten after the full write? | `M_prev` versus `M_real` |

Therefore the acquiredness reference should remain `B0`. The “frozen pre-write holder” for the impending future
write is `M_prev` itself; comparing `M_prev` with that checkpoint gives zero and cannot distinguish acquired
knowledge from a generic prior. `M_prev` is instead the correct reference for shadow damage.

One factual correction to the run note: in `audit()`, the lift is **not** computed on `M_prev`'s greedy answer.
Lines 138--147 construct `qas` with the matched historical `g["answers"]`, and `_meanlogp()` teacher-forces that
gold answer. The greedy `pred` is used for EM. This is acceptable as an offline acquiredness audit, but it must
not enter the discovery/ranking path.

`lift > 0` alone is too permissive. It admits an item for which `B0` already answers correctly or nearly
correctly and `M_prev` merely raises confidence slightly. For a verdict-driving acquired hit, require all of:

- `M_prev` answers the generated question correctly;
- `B0` answers that **same generated question** incorrectly, not only the stored original/eval wording;
- gold-answer mean log probability improves by a preregistered nontrivial margin, with the raw delta reported;
- the proposition-equivalence judgment passes and the result is not a duplicate/template cluster;
- for the instruct-policy claim, separately report whether the frozen instruct proposer answers the target
  correctly when directly queried.

Because `build_census()` deliberately has no per-item base-hard filter, the direct `B0` answer check is especially
important here. Preserve a looser `lift > 0` column for sensitivity analysis, but do not use it as the sole
knowledge-into-weights gate.

For the history-free search score, use `a_M(q)` and compare its likelihood under `M_prev`, `S`, and optionally
`B0`; only after selection may the hidden audit replace this self-answer with the historical gold answer for the
scientific truth label.

## 3. Wrong-shadow control: useful secondary adversary, not the primary null

The current shuffled-answer shadow is not a clean “same write with the true structure removed” null.
`bare_write()` independently trains on the unchanged real passages and on QA answer CE. Shuffling only
`q["answers"]` therefore leaves the true passage write intact while making the QA objective contradict both the
question and often the passage. It preserves answer-token marginals, which is useful, but it may change loss,
gradient norm, parameter-delta geometry, and general damage far more than semantic alignment alone.

Keep it, but rename it `shuffled_answer_adversary` and use it as a secondary control. The preferred primary
specificity null is a **matched different-stream shadow**: same source/domain mixture, passage and QA token
budgets, answer-length distribution, update steps, optimizer, and neutral anchoring, but disjoint future content.
Also include `S_identity = M_prev` as the no-damage control. If practical, a layerwise norm-matched random or
permuted parameter delta is a third control for generic movement through weight space.

Before comparing any shadow ranking, report for each shadow:

- update norm by layer and cosine to the real update;
- current-stream QA learning and passage loss;
- neutral/base drift;
- distribution of old-answer damage over the full answerable audit set;
- generated-question parse rate and diversity if generation remains a diagnostic arm.

Do not require the shuffled shadow to “lose half the coverage” without these checks. On a shared candidate pool,
the stronger causal test is that `D_real` ranks facts damaged by `M_real` substantially better than each null
score at matched `K`, while the nulls do not show the same enrichment. This separates semantic specificity from
mere update magnitude and from generic question-generation drift.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward brainstorming

### Differentiate through answer damage into a soft question

Optimize a short soft prompt against `D_real(q)` while freezing both checkpoints, then decode it into multiple
natural questions and keep only paraphrase-consistent candidates. This attacks the actual answer channel instead
of relying on a fixed generic candidate pool. The main danger is adversarial, nonlinguistic prompts; naturalness,
multi-view agreement, identity-shadow, and different-stream controls must all pass before any candidate is used.

### Intersect acquisition salience with future damage

For every generated candidate, compute two answer-channel deltas:

```text
acquisition salience = logp_Mprev(a_M | q) - logp_B0(a_M | q)
future damage        = logp_Mprev(a_M | q) - logp_Sreal(a_M | q).
```

Search for the Pareto intersection rather than high future damage alone. This asks for behavior that was installed
after initialization and is now at risk, which is much closer to autonomous continual retention. It could still
select hallucinated self-consistent answers, so hidden proposition audits and cross-paraphrase stability remain
hard gates.

### Turn the write delta into a disposable interrogator

Compress the layerwise parameter delta from `B0 -> M_prev` into a transient low-rank sketch and train a tiny
decoder to propose questions whose answers have high acquisition salience. Delete both sketch and decoder after
consolidation. Wrong-delta and cross-seed controls would test whether it reads knowledge-bearing update geometry
rather than optimizer noise. Repeated success could provide a scalable in-weights self-index without inference
memory; failure would show that ordinary dense updates do not expose a decodable episodic address.

### Let retention saturation, not chronology, trigger growth

Track retained acquired bits per training FLOP and marginal new-stream acquisition at a fixed replay/search
budget. Grow the holder only after both measures hit a preregistered plateau across several transitions, then use
a function-preserving expansion and test whether the larger checkpoint moves the Pareto frontier. This makes
small-to-large growth an experimentally necessary compute/capacity response rather than an architectural slogan.
