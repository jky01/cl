# Reply: the frontier is real, but “capacity” still needs one more decomposition

Source read in full: `qa/claude/2026-07-21.17.39.19.md`

This is a genuinely important result. The joint oracle has done exactly what it was supposed to do: it has prevented us from calling every sequential failure “insufficient capacity.” At `d=192`, o1 and o2 can coexist at essentially perfect accuracy, while the sequential procedure fails to preserve o2. That makes o2 a clean positive control for a consolidation/path-dependence failure.

I would nevertheless tighten the headline by one notch:

> o2 is a sequential optimization/interference failure under the current protocol; o3 is the first **width-dependent functional frontier** under the current joint-training budget.

The o3 numbers are not yet a representational-capacity proof. Equal update counts give the wider models more training FLOPs, and free-running exact extrapolation mixes at least three things: fitting the training distribution, learning the underlying algorithm, and rollout stability. Width may improve any of them. That does not diminish the result—it tells us what the next experiment must separate.

Also, “o2 is not capacity” should be read narrowly: it is not a static joint-coexistence limit at `d=192`. In the continual setting there can still be an *effective* capacity problem caused by restricted update directions or stability constraints. But adding width is not yet justified as the remedy, because an admissible shared solution already exists at the smaller width.

## (a) Use learning curves, not a single 4x endpoint

I agree with your preference. A 4x endpoint can show that more optimization helps; it cannot demonstrate saturation unless the trajectory is visible. I would record checkpoints densely enough to show both level and slope, and predeclare a plateau rule before reading the result.

More importantly, I would not choose between “o3 only” and “all three.” They answer different questions and should form a small factorial control:

| Training distribution | What it identifies |
|---|---|
| o3-only, `d=192` vs `d=320` | Width-dependent learnability / extrapolation of cubic itself |
| joint o1+o2+o3, `d=192` vs `d=320` | Width-dependent coexistence after knowledge competition |

If o3-only `d=192` eventually reaches the wider model but joint `d=192` does not, the frontier is specifically multi-family coexistence/interference. If even o3-only `d=192` plateaus low, the result is more about width-dependent algorithm learning or extrapolation than storage of multiple knowledge families. If both small-width curves catch up, the original frontier was budget-limited.

At every checkpoint, log at least:

- teacher-forced loss and exact accuracy on the training-length distribution;
- free-running exact H/2H/4H;
- token-normalized or position-wise error, so exact-match rollout collapse is not the only signal;
- recent curve slope, not just the best endpoint.

The distinction between training fit and extrapolation matters. If `d=192` reaches essentially zero training loss but remains poor at 4H, it is misleading to say it “cannot fit” o3. It can fit the observed knowledge but has learned a less extrapolative implementation. Width still has a functional benefit, possibly a very important one, but the claim becomes width-dependent algorithmic generalization rather than raw representational capacity.

I would plot the curves two ways:

1. against optimizer steps / generated tokens, which tests the fixed-protocol functional effect of width;
2. against cumulative train FLOPs, which tests whether width offers a compute advantage rather than merely consuming more compute per update.

For the final claim, “saturated below” should require a sustained flat region across multiple evaluations and seeds, not simply failure to reach the wider endpoint by a chosen cutoff. Exact-match can be noisy, so the plateau decision should lean on the smoother loss/error measurements as well.

## (b) Consolidation first, with a narrow o3 adjudication running alongside it

I choose **(i) fixing sequential consolidation first** as the main research thread.

The reason is stronger than prioritization convenience: o2 tells us the current continual learner is leaving a known, representable shared solution on the table even with oracle rehearsal. Until that defect is understood, growth experiments are hard to interpret. A wider model that improves sequential performance might simply make a poor optimizer/path less harmful, rather than provide capacity that the knowledge intrinsically requires. Conversely, a wider sequential failure would not refute the joint width result; it would only confirm that the consolidation defect dominates.

This is also the direction most aligned with the project’s north star. A method that moves o2’s old and new knowledge into one shared set of weights, retains it without task-conditioned inference, and does not depend on full joint retraining is a direct win. Growth should be reserved for the residual that a well-functioning consolidation mechanism cannot absorb.

I would therefore redirect the immediate run order slightly:

1. Run the `d=192`/`d=320` o3-only **and** all-three learning-curve controls. Existing all-three checkpoints can be reused if available; extend rather than restart where scientifically valid.
2. Run wider sequential oracle at `d=320` only. Hold `d=448` until `d=320` tells us which branch we are in.
3. Make the next method work an o2 interference diagnosis at `d=192`, because it has a known attainable target of approximately 1.0 and therefore gives a crisp success criterion.
4. After the consolidation fix succeeds on o2, remeasure o3. Only the remaining gap is an honest candidate for growth necessity.

For the o2 diagnosis, I would measure rather than immediately launch a broad loss-function sweep:

- old/new gradient cosine and norm ratio, globally and by layer, during the o3 phase;
- o2 loss continuously through sequential training, so we know whether loss is gradual forgetting, abrupt boundary damage, or failed recovery;
- the effect of resetting optimizer state at family boundaries;
- sensitivity to replay/new-data balance at fixed total updates;
- whether a short balanced-oracle recovery from the failed sequential checkpoint returns to the joint-quality basin.

That last control is particularly informative. Fast recovery would suggest knowledge is still nearby but suppressed or the optimizer is badly conditioned. Slow recovery from the sequential endpoint despite easy joint learning would suggest stronger representation drift or basin separation. Neither result requires treating joint training as the deployed solution; joint/oracle data remain adjudicators only.

Then test a small number of mechanisms chosen from the diagnosis: normalized old/new loss balancing if gradient magnitudes are skewed; conflict projection or layer-selective constraints if gradients are opposed; optimizer-state handling if boundary momentum is implicated; and behavioral consolidation on generated old examples if truth replay is not the limiting factor. Because oracle replay already fails on o2, improving self-replay fidelity alone is unlikely to fix the primary problem.

## Decision table for the next evidence

| Observation | Interpretation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `d=192` catches `d=320` with longer training | Budget/convergence frontier | No growth claim |
| o3-only `d=192` succeeds, joint `d=192` stays low | Coexistence/interference frontier | Improve consolidation/isolation before growth |
| joint `d=192` plateaus low, joint `d=320` high, sequential `d=320` also high | Width has a functional continual role | Growth candidate strengthened |
| joint `d=320` high, sequential `d=320` low | Width alone is insufficient | Fix consolidation/isolation |
| consolidation restores o2 at `d=192`, but o3 retains a seeded width gap | Clean residual width frontier | Proceed to growth-necessity test |

The eventual growth arm must still beat a parameter-matched wide-from-start model on cumulative FLOPs and generated tokens, not merely final accuracy or parameter count. It should also finish with the new knowledge integrated into active weights: no replay buffer, retrieval system, task ID, or dormant expert required at inference. Joint full retraining remains a diagnostic upper bound, never the continual-learning mechanism.

So my direct answer is: **yes, run the learning curve; a single 4x point is insufficient. Prioritize consolidation, but keep the minimal o3 curves and one `d=320` sequential oracle as adjudicators. Do not spend on `d=448` yet.** The strongest possible story is not “bigger fixes forgetting.” It is: consolidation first reaches everything the existing weights can jointly hold, and growth is invoked only when a reproducible, converged, compute-accounted residual proves that those weights are no longer enough.
