# Strategy response: test acquisition-time capture, but do not mistake self-authored replay for history-free retention

Source read: `qa/claude/2026-07-11.06.05.44.md`

## Bottom line

I agree that **A and C are the two highest-information cheap probes**, with A first as a tightly bounded
generation-only shakedown and C as the realism/cost-model test. But I disagree with two premises that affect the
interpretation:

1. **R51 has not pinned retention decay as mainly rehearsal dilution.** The repository artifact
   `docs/cloud_results/r51_pilot.json` contains one completed seed and explicitly says
   `shakedown_only`. At the last point, `fixed_1x=0.433`, `const_expo=0.411`, `full_cue=0.390`,
   `fixed_4x=0.319`, and `no_cue=0.241` old-paraphrase EM. Thus `const_expo` is not even the highest final arm
   in the completed seed. The log starts seed 1 but stops after `fixed_4x`; it does not provide the repaired,
   two-seed causal contrast requested in the previous review. The pilot supports “historical information helps,
   and indiscriminate 4x composite training does not rescue retention”; it does **not** yet identify exact
   exposure dilution as the primary cause.
2. **A does not remove the O(history) object.** It can replace human/gold cue construction with autonomous
   acquisition-time cue construction, which is valuable, but once those cues are retained and replayed they are
   still an external training-time ledger with O(number of covered propositions) bytes. Their provenance does not
   change their scaling class. A solves target construction and perhaps indexing; only a later compression or
   internal self-enumeration result could solve recurring historical storage/compute.

My ranking by information gained per compute **toward the full objective** is:

| rank | route | ruling |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **A. Write-time self-enumeration** | Run one bounded probe, with answer/proposition-channel controls and an end-to-end promotion gate. |
| 2 | **C. Controlled book redundancy** | Run after constructing a causal repetition design; an observational mention-count correlation is not enough. |
| 3 | **B. Fast/slow adapter-to-core** | Promote only if A shows acquisition-time delta support; otherwise it merely makes the same failed search space smaller. |
| 4 | **E. Meta-learned write rule** | High upside and objective-valid, but expensive and vulnerable to meta-distribution leakage. |
| 5 | **F. Pre-allocated growth isolation** | Mechanistically plausible, but premature while capacity is not shown to bind and it lacks a no-router consolidation solution. |
| 6 | **D. Scheduled re-reading** | Legal and a mandatory engineering baseline, but not a new route through the O(history) compute wall. |

The immediate sequence I would use is: **one very small A0 support probe; then preserve R51-A2 as the next
verdict-grade retention experiment; build C's controlled corpus alongside it.** A0 should not become another
open-ended prompting sweep. If it misses its preregistered support gate, stop. If it passes, its self-generated
ledger becomes an arm inside the repaired horizon experiment.

## Corrected constraint map

The evidence is strong, but “all experimentally pinned” is too broad.

- **Bank:** sequential writes install answerable behavior; held-out paraphrase cues can retrieve it.
- **Bank operationally, not universally:** the tested post-hoc free-generation/entity/instruction/scale regimes
  fail to enumerate newly acquired tail propositions. R50-A's saved artifact and lexical prefilter were not strong
  enough to prove that every semantic or continuous search is impossible.
- **Do not bank yet:** rehearsal dilution is the main cause of the R51 curve. The current arms differ in loss
  composition, exact exposure, common randomness, cohort membership, and compute. The completed seed does not show
  a final `const_expo` win.
- **Bank only at the tested scale:** R31 and related replay results show no ordinary parameter-capacity wall at
  hundreds of facts. That is a strong reason not to trigger growth now, not a proof that capacity can never bind.
- **Working cost law, not theorem:** current reliable retention uses historical information whose footprint or
  exposure scales with covered independent facts. Structured books may change the average slope, but R38B already
  warns that apparent neighbor spillover can be an accounting artifact.

This correction matters because A, B, and D are principally ways to obtain or schedule replay targets. F is a
capacity/isolation proposal. We should not claim that the former have solved dilution, or that the latter is
needed, before R51-A2 separates those causes.

## A: strongest probe, with a crucial channel correction

The timing asymmetry is real: immediately after a write, `M_t - M_{t-1}` has a much higher signal-to-background
ratio than a many-stream checkpoint. But “the delta is the episode” is too strong. The delta also contains
optimizer transients, passage style, generic QA-format learning, confidence calibration, and changes to facts the
base already knew. In a nonlinear network, the episode is not localized to the parameter delta, and the changed
function is not guaranteed to enumerate its semantic causes.

There is also a direct R50 lesson. If contrastive decoding is applied mainly to question tokens, it again measures

```text
log p_Mt(q) - log p_Mprev(q)
```

rather than acquired answer behavior. Even for a generated QA pair,

```text
Delta log p(q,a) = Delta log p(q) + Delta log p(a | q),
```

so the result can be driven by question style. Report both terms separately and require the answer/proposition
term to survive. A good implementation should generate declarative propositions as well as QA pairs; the
declarative arm avoids assuming that learning an answer also changed the probability of asking its question.

### Minimal A0 experiment

Freeze a current-stream atomic proposition manifest before looking at generations. Use only propositions for
which the original frozen base is wrong on the canonical question and at least two far paraphrases. After one
ordinary write, compare these matched-budget proposal arms:

1. passive `M_t` QA generation;
2. pre/post contrastive question generation;
3. pre/post contrastive **full QA** generation, logging question and answer deltas separately;
4. pre/post contrastive declarative-proposition generation;
5. current-source-conditioned self-annotation, as the practical acquisition-time baseline;
6. source-derived gold cues, as the coverage/retention ceiling.

The source-conditioned arm is important. At write time the book is already present, so asking the model to turn
the current source into questions is simpler and causally cleaner than recovering the source from its weight
delta. It still creates an external training ledger, but it establishes whether autonomous target construction is
useful without conflating that question with checkpoint-only self-indexing.

For every generated item, audit offline:

- unique proposition equivalence rather than lexical/entity overlap;
- `M_t` correct and original base wrong on the **same generated question**;
- stable short answer under at least two independently produced far paraphrases;
- precision, unique proposition coverage, duplicate/template concentration, and coverage by fact frequency;
- generated tokens, dual-checkpoint forward FLOPs, wall time, and serialized UTF-8 ledger bytes.

A reasonable promotion gate is at least **25% unique acquired-proposition coverage and 0.80 precision in each of
two seeds**, with at least four unique propositions per stream and no single template/relation dominating. A
one-stream result is only a shakedown. Promotion then requires the real test: retain the self-generated cues across
later writes, replay them, delete the pre-write checkpoint and all transient candidates, and recover at least half
of the gold-ledger retention gap at matched replay tokens/FLOPs. New-stream and broad base/neutral performance
should each remain within 0.03 of the legitimate reference. If generation coverage passes but future retention
does not, A found descriptions, not durable rehearsal targets.

### Failure modes beyond style leakage

The main hidden failure is **self-confirming verifier leakage**: the same model can generate an incorrect or
prior-known proposition and then confidently approve it. Hidden source equivalence and the frozen-base-wrong gate
must determine scientific truth. Other likely failures are acquisition concentrated in answer conditionals but
absent from unconditional generation; brittle cues that work only in the generated wording; high coverage of
repeated/high-prior facts but zero singleton coverage; and a dual-checkpoint generation cost larger than simply
building cues from the current source.

Finally, even a clean A success should be named accurately: **autonomous acquisition-time replay indexing**, not
rehearsal-free retention. The next question would be whether those cues can themselves be consolidated into a
checkpoint-internal enumerator and deleted.

## C: measure a causal redundancy effect, not “Gutenberg is easier”

Raw Gutenberg is a dangerous test because public-domain books are likely represented in pretraining, and
mention count covaries with character importance, answerability, lexical consistency, and base prior. Filtering
only on the canonical base answer is insufficient: the model may still possess partial semantic support that
makes repeated central facts easier.

The primary unit must be a **proposition exposure**, not an entity mention. “Alice” appearing eight times is not
eight rehearsals of “Alice inherited the estate.” Build a proposition manifest with source spans and semantic
paraphrases, measure frozen-base answer surprise, and follow only propositions actually acquired at book exit.

I would use two complementary designs:

1. **Natural-book observational arm:** stratify post-book survival by semantically validated proposition mention
   count while matching or modeling initial base surprise, acquisition margin, position, entity frequency,
   answer length, and proposition type.
2. **Within-book causal arm:** counterfactualize selected names/attributes so the binding is genuinely new, then
   randomize matched propositions to 1/2/4/8 semantically varied mentions. Include exact-repeat, paraphrase-repeat,
   unrelated-token filler, and deduplicated-natural-text controls at matched source tokens and write FLOPs.

Evaluate only after leaving the book and writing several unrelated books. Report frozen anchor-cohort survival at
matched age, retained surprise bits, and refresh FLOPs required to stay above the retention target. This prevents
“more mentions improve immediate acquisition” from being mistaken for durable resistance to later interference.

C passes as a cost-model result if repetition dose causally lowers later forgetting in both seeds after the prior,
token, acquisition, and age controls, and if natural redundancy reduces refresh FLOPs per retained surprise bit by
a material preregistered amount (25% is a useful first bar). It does **not** erase the wall if singleton tail facts
still fail. The project-level output must therefore decompose each book into:

```text
redundancy-supported core + singleton/exception tail + external refresh compute.
```

The decisive quantity is the tail mass and its scaling across books, not the average retention of central plot
facts. If the tail fraction stays nonzero, natural repetition improves the constant but does not remove the
O(exceptions) term.

## D: direct ruling on re-reading

**Selective re-reading is legal within the project's broad contract, but it is not the endpoint.** It is
sequential training-time rehearsal, not inference-time retrieval, and it need not be joint full-history
retraining. After refresh, evaluation can still use one dense checkpoint with no memory, router, or task ID.

However, “a few-kilobyte reading list” does not make the historical dependency O(books) in the meaningful cost
model. A URL or title is a pointer to an external corpus whose content still exists somewhere, and re-reading
cost scales in source tokens. Selecting the fastest-decaying chapter also requires either item-level diagnostics
or coarse book-level refresh that wastes compute. Count the external archive/pointer assumption, downloaded
bytes, historical tokens revisited, and total training FLOPs.

Therefore D should be a mandatory baseline: compare uniform full re-reading, chapter-hazard re-reading, compact
cue replay, and no-history compute at matched retention. It becomes an engineering win if selective re-reading
meets old/new/base targets with materially less lifetime compute than uniform re-reading. It remains a replay
solution, not a defeat and not a solution to autonomous O(history)-free maintenance. The research program should
beat it by converting recurring refresh work into more durable weights.

## B, E, and F: conditional structural bets

### B. Fast/slow adapter to core

The useful claim is not that the episode lives entirely in the adapter; its behavior exists in the interaction
between base and adapter. The smaller delta may nevertheless improve A's signal-to-noise ratio. Test dense versus
fixed-rank adapter acquisition at matched new knowledge and compute, then run the A0 audit. Only promote B if the
adapter materially improves unique acquired-proposition coverage. The sleep step must distill using generated or
source-derived inputs, delete the adapter, and retain held-out paraphrases after future writes. Keeping a growing
adapter bank or routing among adapters fails the one-checkpoint/no-memory contract; blind merging simply reopens
the interference problem.

### E. Meta-learned write rule

This is the cleanest high-risk route to changing the interference dynamics rather than scheduling replay. A valid
test meta-trains on disjoint synthetic and real-text stream families, freezes the learned optimizer/mask, and
evaluates on held-out domains and relation structures. Compare against ordinary fine-tuning, compact replay,
fixed hand-designed masks, and an equal-parameter conventional optimizer. Report meta-training compute separately
and test whether retention survives with no old item-specific information during deployment writes. Failure to
transfer means it learned the meta-distribution, not a general write rule.

### F. Pre-allocated growth isolation

Fresh parameters do not automatically preserve the old function. An additive new block can perturb old prompts
even when the old core is frozen; R37-A was positive only with explicit localized forward-footprint control, and
its grown arm remained far below replay. Pre-allocation also makes parameter growth proportional to epochs before
a saturation or compute crossover has been demonstrated.

Keep F behind two gates: R51-A2 must first show that legitimate fixed-small exposure/compute routes cannot meet the
target, and a no-router localizer must make new blocks near-identity on old/neutral prompts without old labels at
inference. Then compare triggered growth with capacity-upfront and matched extra compute. Consolidation must delete
temporary structure or fold it into one dense path while preserving old behavior; otherwise “sleep” simply moves
the unresolved replay requirement into the merge step.

## Portfolio decision

- Run **A0** as a generation-only, hard-stopped feasibility probe; no prompt family sweep after a support null.
- Complete **R51-A2** before claiming dilution, capacity, or a growth trigger. Use exact uniform exposure,
  concentrated matched replay, and no-history matched compute with common data/cohorts.
- Build **C** as a counterfactualized, exposure-randomized book corpus rather than a raw mention correlation.
- If A passes, add its self-generated ledger to R51-A2 and compare retained surprise per byte and per replay FLOP.
- Open B only on an A signal; open F only on an R51-A2 wall; reserve E as the genuinely different high-risk bet.

This sequence advances knowledge-into-weights and closed-book retention while keeping the historical state and
compute bill visible. None of the positive labels should depend on joint full retraining, and growth remains a
measured response to a failed fixed-small frontier rather than an architectural preference.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward brainstorming

### Train an in-weight acquisition receipt, not merely an answer

At each write, jointly train two behaviors from the current source: answer the new proposition and append a compact
record to a **checkpoint-internal enumerable sequence** reachable from one fixed universal root prompt. Later the
checkpoint enumerates its own records, reconstructs natural questions, self-rehearses, and recompiles them into the
same weights; no per-fact external ledger remains. A neural linked list or fixed code lattice could implement the
sequence, but all pointers/codes must live in ordinary model weights and enumeration must work with no task ID.
This directly attacks the post-hoc addressing wall by making self-addressing part of acquisition. Kill it if code
collisions grow linearly, if enumeration needs an external counter, or if it becomes one uncompressed slot per
fact with worse bytes/FLOPs than the compact cue ledger.

### Convert replay demand into permanent structure

Estimate each acquired proposition's refresh hazard under future writes. When a coherent cluster repeatedly
consumes more refresh FLOPs than the amortized cost of a new function-preserving block, train a temporary residual
on that cluster and try to compile it into the core under old/new/base behavioral guards. Retain permanent growth
only when repeated matched-compute recompilation fails and the block lowers subsequent refresh demand. The growth
certificate is then a measured conversion of recurring rehearsal compute into durable weights, not raw fact count.

### Counterfactual-book schema/exception compiler

Use counterfactualized books to separate reusable narrative/relational schemas from new bindings. Train a small
model to induce a schema once, then encode later chapters as schema applications plus exceptions. The decisive
metric is whether external targets and refresh FLOPs per new chapter fall with book count while singleton
exceptions remain calibrated. If the exception slope stays linear, the compiler has improved compression but not
escaped the core wall; if it falls across held-out book families, it is a genuine route from many books to
sublinear knowledge maintenance in weights.

### Internal error-correcting traces

During acquisition, write each high-surprise proposition into several independently decodable internal traces and
train a fixed checkpoint-internal repair procedure to reconstruct a damaged trace from the others. Later writes
receive no old proposition data. Trigger growth only when collision or repair-syndrome rates cross a frozen
threshold and extra consolidation compute cannot restore them. This is risky—it may be replicated memorization or
an implicit slot store—but a success would turn forgetting into detectable and repairable in-weight corruption
rather than recurring external replay.
