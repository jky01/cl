There are also two small but scientifically important qualifications.

Thus the experiment establishes **classification-loss versus exact-logit-regression geometry** very strongly, but it does not empirically distinguish pseudo-label replay from gold-label replay here. “Label-free for old data” is operationally true—the algorithm need not read stored labels—but it is not yet a separate causal finding. ## What this proves against the project criteria

- **Knowledge into weights:** yes. After deletion of the private slots, both A and B behavior resides in the shared checkpoint.
- **Continual retention:** yes on the fully replayed A support; general retention under bounded support remains open.
- **No-memory inference:** yes after folding. There are no task slots or old examples needed at inference.
- **Growth necessity / compute advantage:** not established. At `h=128`, fixed shared capacity already contains a union witness, so growth has not earned a role in this regime.
- **Avoiding joint full retraining:** not yet. Full-A CE revisits the entire old input set jointly with B; removing gold labels does not remove the storage and revisit cost. This is a mechanism proof and upper bound, not yet the target continual-learning algorithm.

That is still a major advance: it replaces the false question “why can the shared weights not hold both tasks?” with the real one, “what is the smallest legal support that identifies the compatible decision basin?”

## Referee-budget sweep: use both random and margin selection

I would not choose between them. Random is the necessary baseline; low-margin selection is the first deployable hypothesis. Run both at every budget, class-stratified, with repeated seeds.

Suggested budgets are full A, 50%, 10%, 2%, one example per A class, and zero. Percentages alone can hide the relevant sample complexity, so report absolute counts and counts per class as well.

Use three selection rules:

1. **Stratified random** — the unbiased reference curve.
2. **Lowest pre-fold teacher margin** — a legal rule at the end of A, because it needs no knowledge of B. This tests whether boundary anchors dominate retention.
3. **Largest B-induced interference** — rank A examples by margin drop, loss increase, or gradient conflict after a short provisional B update. This is the closest match to evidence-induced residual selection, but it must be labeled a **diagnostic oracle** if computed by rescanning all A after B arrives. It is not deployable under a bounded-memory protocol because the unretained A inputs would already be gone.

That legality distinction is essential. If the interference oracle wins strongly, it tells us what a generator, sketch, or online residual detector must approximate. It does not itself solve the memory problem. A future legal version could update priorities only among the already retained buffer or select replay queries from self-generated samples.

For each subset, separate two effects that otherwise get confounded:

- **Support coverage:** train with the same old/new loss mass at every budget, resampling the retained subset as needed. Then failure means the subset does not constrain enough of A, not merely that A received fewer gradient steps.
- **Fixed-compute deployment:** also run a protocol where compute really falls with the referee budget. This is the curve relevant to compute advantage.

Evaluate retained-A and unretained-A examples separately. A small buffer can achieve perfect apparent retention by memorizing its anchors while the rest of A collapses. The primary quantities should therefore be teacher agreement and gold accuracy on **unretained A**, plus B accuracy, minimum accuracy, and the margin distribution—not only its mean. Keep full-A CE as the upper bound, zero-A as the forgetting floor, and one representative MSE setting as the negative control.

I would postpone the growth control until this curve is known. Growth is justified only where a parameter-matched wider shared model reduces the amount of old support needed, improves retention at fixed support and compute, or makes previously incompatible tasks compatible. Merely succeeding after adding parameters would not establish growth necessity. The clean test is a surface over `(shared capacity, referee budget)`, with a fixed total parameter and optimization budget control. The interesting signal is a leftward shift: growth buys the same retention with fewer old anchors.

Finally, the zero-input random-key result should be treated as a designed impossibility control, not a disappointing endpoint. Since the old keys contain no recoverable structure, no input-free method can infer which unseen points must keep which decisions. The toy can locate the replay-support law and falsify bad preservation losses; it cannot validate self-generated replay. The LM harness must test that separate bridge: whether the model can generate old-support queries with enough coverage, use its pre-update predictions as the decision referee, fold the update into shared weights, discard all temporary memory, and retain performance without periodic joint full retraining.

So my answer is: **yes, elevate the invariant choice as the finding; sweep random and low-margin together; add post-B interference only as an explicitly illegal oracle.** The decisive next result is not another 1.0/1.0 at full replay. It is the retention-versus-support curve, with legality, coverage, and compute disentangled.
