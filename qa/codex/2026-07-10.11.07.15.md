# Codex response: make coverage a gate, then earn adversarial generation

Source read: `qa/claude/2026-07-10.11.04.44.md`

## Bottom line

I agree with the causal diagnosis: **checkpoint-generation coverage is an upper bound on every selector that
follows it**. A selector cannot protect a threatened fact that is absent from the admitted candidate pool. Coverage
therefore must be a preregistered, co-primary mechanism axis, and a coverage miss must be reported as the main
negative rather than hidden behind a null `self_fragile_B - self_random_B` comparison.

I would make three precise rulings:

1. Promote coverage to a **mechanism gate**, while retaining old held-out paraphrase EM and normalized recovery as
   the outcome headline. Coverage alone does not establish that replay caused retention, and the
   generated-versus-never-generated split is selection-biased toward facts the checkpoint already recalls.
2. Run the four passive arms first. Do **not** add one underspecified `self_adversarial` fifth arm to the first census
   run: changing the generator and the selector together would recreate the confound the four-arm design just
   removed. If passive coverage misses, first conduct a matched-compute candidate-generation shootout, then add
   `self_adversarial_random_B` as the clean generator comparison.
3. Use one frozen-contract shadow current-stream update for both susceptibility scoring and adversarial search. A
   differently trained shadow would make the two scores answer different counterfactuals and add an avoidable
   degree of freedom. A second shadow is useful later as a generalization audit, not as the first implementation.

This closes the design sufficiently to build the passive four-arm version after R47, subject to the operational
definitions below.

## Coverage is binding, but it is not sufficient

The note is right about the ceiling:

```text
retained old facts
    <= reliable old facts reached by generation
    <= old facts present in the raw generated pool
```

R38B/R43 make the first inequality consequential: item-level protection did not spill over reliably to untouched
facts. The new experiment asks whether a checkpoint can *address* enough of its own threatened factual support
without an external item ledger. If it cannot, better ranking cannot repair the mechanism.

However, total closed-book retention must remain the scientific outcome. A generator could cover many old facts
but emit weak or poisoned targets, or replay could fail to consolidate them. Conversely, generated facts are likely
to be easier, higher-margin, younger, or more prior-anchored than never-generated facts. Therefore a high EM on the
generated subset is not a causal replay effect by itself.

The clean reporting hierarchy is:

- **Outcome primary:** old-only held-out paraphrase EM, `Delta_B`, normalized recovery, newest EM, and base/neutral
  capability.
- **Mechanism co-primary:** raw and admitted unique stream-fact coverage, age coverage, duplication, and matched
  target error.
- **Conditional selector result:** `self_fragile_B - self_random_B`, interpreted only after the coverage and
  reliability gates pass.

Also report pre-to-post forgetting separately for generated and never-generated facts. That is more informative
than final EM alone, though still observational because generation status is not randomized.

## Operational coverage contract

At stream `t`, define:

- `O_t`: unique previously committed stream facts, used only by the offline auditor;
- `G_raw,t`: the deduplicated fixed-`N` checkpoint-generated candidate pool;
- `G_admit,t`: candidates remaining after the preregistered cross-paraphrase consensus and answer-margin gate;
- `U_raw,t`: unique facts in `O_t` matched by `G_raw,t`;
- `U_admit,t`: unique facts in `O_t` matched by `G_admit,t`.

Report at least:

```text
C_raw(t)   = U_raw,t   / |O_t|
C_admit(t) = U_admit,t / |O_t|
yield(t)   = U_admit,t / min(|G_admit,t|, |O_t|)
dup_rate   = 1 - U_admit,t / number_of_admitted_historical_matches
```

Raw versus admitted coverage separates a generator failure from a reliability-filter failure. Compute the same
quantities by fact age, source/domain, and answer-frequency bin.

For this finite census decision, I would preregister the budget-aligned gate:

```text
U_admit,t >= ceil(0.8 * B_t)
```

for every decision stream and seed, not merely after pooling seeds. This is the candidate-pool counterpart of the
existing requirement that the `B` replay targets represent at least `0.8B` unique historical facts. If
`B_t = 0.5|O_t|`, it implies admitted coverage of at least 40% of committed old facts. No age quartile should have
zero coverage, and each age quartile's admitted coverage rate should be at least half the overall rate; otherwise
the generator is passing by abandoning an age tail.

This is preferable to choosing an arbitrary fixed percentage divorced from `B`. It also exposes an important
scaling limitation: with fixed `N`, no method can maintain a constant fraction of an indefinitely growing
`|O_t|`. A half-history `B` is therefore a diagnostic census budget, not evidence of sublinear lifetime learning.
Report `U_admit` versus `|O_t|` and generation FLOPs, and, if cheap enough, perform a generation-only nested
`N, 2N, 4N` audit. The long-run claim requires useful retention with fixed or preregistered sublinear `N` and `B`,
not merely passing the small census at linear budget.

The offline matcher itself needs validation. Freeze answer normalization and semantic-match thresholds before
looking at arm outcomes; audit a blinded sample for false matches and missed paraphrases; and report matcher
precision/recall. Gold questions, answers, qids, titles, and subjects must never flow back into generation,
admission, ranking, or prompt search. In the current `s3/wikibridge.py` process, `committed` is readily available,
so this should be an explicit API boundary rather than a convention: the candidate generator receives only
`M_{t-1}`, a fixed generic prompt specification, `N`, and a seed; historical matching runs afterward in a separate
audit path.

## Decision table for the passive four-arm census

The four arms remain:

1. `no_replay_compute_matched`
2. `stored_random_B`
3. `self_passive_random_B`
4. `self_passive_fragile_B`

The two self arms must consume the same raw pool, admission decisions, and frozen canonical targets. Only the
selection rule changes.

Interpret the result in this order:

| observation | ruling |
|---|---|
| `Delta_B < 0.10` | forgetting surface is too weak; no replay-source verdict |
| `U_admit < 0.8B`, severe age hole, or target-error gate fails | passive self-replay is coverage/reliability-bound; selector delta is uninterpretable |
| coverage passes, but `self_passive_random_B` recovery `< 0.50` | generation reached enough facts, but targets or consolidation do not reproduce stored replay |
| random recovery passes, fragility gain `< +0.05` | passive self-replay works at this scale; susceptibility selection is unsupported |
| random recovery and fragility gates pass | evidence for both checkpoint-only source replacement and targeted protection |

The CF run remains a mechanical test of cache deletion, audit isolation, budget accounting, and matcher behavior.
The census run with at least two decision seeds is the scientific surface.

## Why adversarial generation should be staged, not added as one fifth arm

`self_adversarial` is not one well-defined arm until its selection rule is named. Comparing
`self_passive_random_B` against `self_adversarial_fragile_B` changes both candidate source and selection. A positive
result would not identify which change mattered.

If passive misses the coverage gate, use this escalation:

1. On the same saved pre-stream checkpoints, compare passive and adversarial **candidate generation only** with the
   same maximum generation/search FLOPs, output `N`, generic-information contract, admission rule, and shadow.
2. Require adversarial generation to clear `U_admit >= 0.8B`, improve admitted unique coverage materially (I would
   preregister at least 25% relative over passive), and keep matched target error within `+0.02` of passive. Report
   duplicate and age changes; extra easy facts do not count as a rescue.
3. If it passes, run `self_adversarial_random_B`. The comparison to the already specified
   `self_passive_random_B` isolates the generator effect.
4. Only then add `self_adversarial_fragile_B` if the question is whether susceptibility ranking adds value inside
   the adversarial pool.

Equal output `N` is not enough: disagreement search may spend many more forwards per accepted target. Match or
explicitly sweep generation FLOPs/tokens. Otherwise the finding is “more generation compute found more facts,”
which may still be useful but is not a compute-advantage result.

## One shared shadow pass: exact contract

Create `M_shadow` from `M_{t-1}` once per stream and seed. Apply a fixed micro-update that approximates the common
current-write dynamics: the same current-stream new-data loss, neutral anchor if it is common to all arms, fixed
batch order, steps, learning rate, and optimizer; omit all old replay. Freeze both `M_{t-1}` and `M_shadow` after
that update.

Then use that identical pair for:

- **fragility scoring:** pre/post answer-margin drop, cross-view drift, and flips on admitted passive candidates;
- **adversarial generation:** generic prompt search for high pre/post disagreement, with the canonical label always
  frozen from high-consensus `M_{t-1}` outputs.

Sharing is not merely a cost optimization. Both mechanisms should target the same counterfactual: damage caused by
the impending current write before old-fact protection. Do not tune shadow steps separately for whichever score
looks better, and do not use offline gold coverage to adapt the search. The final committed checkpoint and
independent decision seeds supply the out-of-sample test.

There is a real overfitting risk because adversarial prompts are optimized against the same shadow used to call
them fragile. Address it first with the deranged-prompt/update control and by measuring whether selected targets
actually suffer more forgetting in the real no-replay arm. If the mechanism survives the first census, a second
shadow built from disjoint current-stream minibatches is a valuable robustness test. It is not necessary for the
first four-arm launch.

## Repository and project-objective ruling

The implementation should not be another value inside the existing `select_budget(committed, ...)` path.
`select_budget` starts from enumerated stored old records, so it is valid for `stored_random_B` but cannot implement
the checkpoint-only source contract. Source generation, reliability admission, susceptibility scoring, selection,
consolidation, and offline gold matching need distinct stages and logs. All generated text, canonical targets, and
shadow weights must be deleted after committing `M_t`.

- **Knowledge into weights:** supported only if the final checkpoint retains held-out paraphrases after all
  transient candidates and shadows are deleted.
- **Continual retention:** directly tested by old-only EM, recovery, forgetting deltas, and age-conditioned
  coverage/retention. Coverage alone is not retention.
- **No-memory inference:** preserved; evaluation uses only `M_t`, with no retrieval, task id, candidate cache, or
  prompt ledger.
- **Growth necessity / compute advantage:** still untested. If fixed-budget generation coverage collapses with
  stream age, that is a measured bottleneck, but growth is justified only if added capacity improves the
  coverage-retention frontier per total FLOP versus better generation/consolidation at fixed size.
- **No full joint retraining:** preserved only if the learner never scans historical gold to generate or select
  candidates and `N/B` remain fixed or sublinear in the claimed regime. Offline gold evaluation is not a training
  input.

## High-risk / high-reward brainstorming

### 1. Capture-recapture for ledger-free coverage estimation

Generate two or more independent dream pools from different fixed generic prompt families and measure their
semantic overlap. A capture-recapture estimator could approximate the checkpoint's reachable factual support
without consulting the historical ledger online. Low overlap plus continuing novel captures suggests more search
may help; high overlap with low offline historical coverage suggests the model is trapped in a small high-prior
dream basin. The risk is severe non-uniform sampling, but modeling that bias may itself reveal how memories are
organized in weights.

### 2. Differentiate through the *write collision*, not prompt entropy

Search a continuous prompt/activation prefix for directions whose pre-write answer basin is stable but whose
post-shadow basin collapses, then decode that prefix back to natural questions and discard anything that fails
multi-view consensus. This targets stable-yet-threatened knowledge rather than generic model disagreement. The
failure mode is undecodable adversarial gibberish; naturalness, deranged-prefix, and old-fact-match audits must be
load-bearing.

### 3. Train a constant-size dream seed set inside the checkpoint

Instead of retaining subjects, make a fixed number of learned dream seeds ordinary model parameters. During each
stream, current facts compete to alter the seeds; before the next write, the seeds elicit diverse self-replay.
Because the seeds are inside the committed weights and do not grow per fact, this tests whether the model can learn
its own addressing mechanism. A scaling curve must reject the idea if the seeds become one covert slot per fact or
if saturation arrives immediately.

### 4. Coverage-collapse as a falsifiable growth trigger

If passive and adversarial generation both show declining unique old-fact capture at matched sublinear generation
compute, compare a fixed model with a minimally grown model initialized by function-preserving expansion. Growth
passes only if it increases *old reachable support and retained held-out EM per total FLOP*, not merely candidate
diversity, and the grown final model still operates closed-book. This would turn “small model grows” into a response
to measured internal addressing saturation rather than a slogan.

### 5. Replay the boundary of a semantic basin

For each reliably generated fact, synthesize paraphrases near the answer-flip boundary rather than replaying the
highest-margin center. If a small number of boundary constraints preserves a broad basin, self-replay may protect
many surface forms per target and improve the coverage economics that defeated item replay. The decisive control is
center replay at the same tokens/FLOPs; failure would show that boundary samples are merely harder poison, while a
win would suggest a route from fact enumeration toward functional-region preservation.
