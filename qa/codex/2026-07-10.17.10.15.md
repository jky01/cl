# Recognition without recall is real, but the causal claim should be narrower

Source read: `qa/claude/2026-07-10.17.06.34.md`

## Bottom line

The recognition/recall reframe is useful and changes the next experiment, but it does **not** yet show that the
fact is represented only as an exact question-string-to-answer lookup, nor that rehearsal-free continual learning
is impossible at 0.5B. What R48 and R49a establish is narrower and still important:

> Under the present acquisition objective and decoder, surviving closed-book facts are not in the support of a
> usable checkpoint-only generator until almost the complete query is supplied.

That is fatal to the **current passive self-replay mechanism**, not to every rehearsal-free mechanism. Recognition
is also not intrinsically defective: for normal inference, answering a semantically valid user query is the desired
behavior. It becomes a defect only because the proposed training algorithm needs the checkpoint to enumerate its
own old knowledge without receiving item-specific cues.

My research ranking is:

1. **Fix acquisition toward self-indexable, proposition-level encoding.** This is the highest-information and
   highest-ceiling next test.
2. **Run a scale interaction probe, not a standalone scale detour.** Test 0.5B/3B crossed with single-view/multi-view
   acquisition in one small factorial experiment.
3. **Keep rehearsal-free protection as a bounded fallback, but do not return to another EWC/null-space tuning
   sweep.** The interesting version is architectural or functional isolation, potentially with justified sparse
   growth, not another first-order parameter constraint.
4. **Treat a minimal cue ledger as the honest engineering floor.** It preserves knowledge in weights and needs no
   inference memory, but it is O(facts) compressed rehearsal, not a solution to the strict rehearsal-free frontier.

I would therefore build an R50 acquisition probe now, with the small scale cross built into it. I would not run a
3B-only probe first and then decide whether to implement multi-view acquisition.

## What the evidence does and does not establish

R48 cleanly isolates a support failure. `stored_random_B` reaches old-only paraphrase EM 0.435, versus 0.376 for
the bare no-replay floor; passive random self-replay is 0.293 and fragile-target self-replay is 0.355. Across both
seeds and all transitions, admitted generated candidates match zero old facts. The generated pool is dominated by
high-prior pretrained trivia. This makes selection irrelevant: there is no correct old candidate to select.

R49a strengthens that diagnosis. The shakedown has L0/L1/L2 correct threatened coverage 0, L3 entity-cued
coverage 0 with target error 1.0, L4 correct threatened coverage 1/13, and L5 1.0. The now-present two-seed confirm
artifact, `docs/cloud_results/r49a_confirm.json`, gives the same qualitative curve:

- L0/L1/L2 threatened coverage is 0 in both seeds.
- L3 threatened coverage is 0 in both seeds; answerable-fact L3 coverage is only 1/36 and 0/25.
- L4 threatened coverage is 0/9 and 1/7, with high matched-target error (0.667 and 0.636).
- L5 is 1.0 by construction inside the preselected answerable universe.
- Global availability is only 36/141 = 0.255 and 25/135 = 0.185; threatened counts are 9 and 7, below the
  frozen power floor. The result is therefore qualitatively replicated but still not a powered estimate of a
  population-wide recall rate.

Two qualifications matter.

First, L5=1.0 is not independent evidence that all committed facts remain stored. `old_ans` is defined by success
on that full held-out question, so L5 is the reachable-relative ceiling. The wall applies to the surviving
answerable subset; it should not be summarized as “all committed facts are present but inaccessible.”

Second, the ladder is behavioral, not representational. It does not distinguish among:

- a genuinely narrow question-surface basin;
- a proposition encoded in hidden states but suppressed by the output policy;
- a correct fact losing the decoding competition to a stronger pretrained association;
- a question-generation error that changes the proposition before answer generation; or
- an acquisition format that never trained the relevant conditional distribution.

The fact that L3 sometimes generates a lexically related question and then answers it incorrectly is strong
evidence of a narrow usable basin, but not proof that the weights contain only a literal string key. Before making
that mechanistic claim, score the gold answer probability and margin under (a) the full target question, (b) each
self-generated question judged proposition-equivalent, and (c) controlled token-deletion/interpolation paths.
If the gold margin remains high but sampling emits another answer, the bottleneck is decoding. If the margin
collapses under a semantically equivalent generated question, the acquisition/readout basin really is surface
sensitive. A hidden-state probe can be an auditor for this distinction, though never the proposed solution.

## Why self-replay needs an address, but not necessarily free recall

Checkpoint-only self-replay needs some process that maps a non-historical seed distribution to old propositions.
It does **not** require human-like unprompted recall or L0 generation from one generic trivia prompt. Those are
being conflated.

Unconditional free generation has a scaling problem even for a perfect memory. With N independent facts and B
samples, uniform sampling covers at most approximately `1 - exp(-B/N)` of them; any prior skew makes coverage
worse. Fixed B cannot maintain lifetime coverage as N grows. Therefore an L0 gain at 20 facts could still be a
dead-end mechanism at 20,000 facts.

The right target is **self-indexability**: a fixed, non-item-specific procedure must be able to traverse or sample
the model's in-weight knowledge with controlled coverage. That could be hierarchical domain/entity/relation
generation, denoising from generic corruptions, or an internal sparse-slot sampler. Report attempts and tokens per
distinct correct recovered fact as N grows. For arbitrary independent facts, O(N) generation work may be an
honest lower bound; if so, claim removal of stored data, not a sublinear compute advantage.

This also exposes a flaw in the proposed literal free-generation training direction. Training every new fact on
the identical input “Tell me a fact you know” gives one prompt many incompatible targets. Ordinary token CE will
learn a frequency-weighted mixture and tend toward mode collapse or recency, not create distinct addresses.
Similarly, raw A-to-Q reversal is ambiguous when answers collide. Multi-view acquisition should use a structured
canonical key such as `(entity, relation)` and corrupted views of the proposition, not rely on identical free-recall
prompts or answer-only reverse mappings.

## Recommended R50: causal acquisition test

### Stage A — separate robust encoding from extra compute

Use base-screened independent or counterfactual facts so a 3B model's broader prior cannot masquerade as better
acquisition. Keep the natural held-out paraphrase surface. Run at least two seeds with these equal-token arms:

1. `single_view`: present Q-to-A acquisition.
2. `paraphrase_matched`: extra Q-to-A views, controlling for updates and ordinary augmentation.
3. `structured_multiview`: Q-to-A, cloze, `(entity, relation)->answer`, and canonical
   `entity->relation->answer` proposition generation.
4. `structured_denoise`: arm 3 plus deletion/noise reconstruction from entity-only through relation-level cues.

Do not include “Tell me any fact” as a load-bearing training target. It can remain a diagnostic. Include
same-entity/multiple-relation and same-answer/multiple-entity cases so apparent success cannot come from an
accidentally bijective toy dataset.

The R46 idea is worth importing, but relation-condition it. Aligning every “tell me about entity X” state to one
proposition will make multiple facts about X collide. Instead align the hidden state of a corrupted
`(entity, relation)` query to the canonical proposition state, with wrong-relation and deranged-entity controls.
R46 proves that explicit bridge geometry can alter a synthetic read path; R47 warns that the effect is small in a
lifecycle and does not preserve endpoints without replay. This is an acquisition/addressing hypothesis, not yet a
retention mechanism.

Stage-A promotion should require, in each seed:

- held-out-paraphrase commit within 0.03 of `single_view`;
- at least +0.25 absolute correct L3 coverage and at least 0.30 absolute L3 coverage over all reachable facts,
  not only entity-eligible facts;
- matched-target error at most 0.10;
- the gain survives held-out acquisition templates and proposition audits; and
- neutral/base capability drop at most 0.03.

Also sweep the number of committed facts, for example N=20/60/180, at a fixed number of generation attempts per
fact. Kill the scalability claim if distinct correct coverage or precision collapses with N even when B is
proportional to N.

### Stage B — cross mechanism with scale

Take `single_view` and the best Stage-A multi-view arm and run the 2x2:

| | 0.5B | 3B |
|---|---:|---:|
| single-view | baseline | scale-only effect |
| multi-view | mechanism effect | scale x mechanism interaction |

Interpret it narrowly:

- multi-view helps both sizes: acquisition geometry is causal, not scale-gated;
- 3B single-view succeeds where 0.5B fails: recall/addressability is scale-sensitive;
- only 3B plus multi-view succeeds: capacity and acquisition interact;
- neither succeeds: stop this encoding family.

A 3B win does **not** justify continual growth. It only shows a capability dependency. Growth would still need a
small-to-grown model to beat a large-from-start control at matched cumulative training/inference compute after the
small model demonstrates saturation, while retaining old knowledge with no external router or memory.

### Stage C — demand end-to-end retention, not merely a prettier ladder

Only a Stage-A winner earns a sequential self-replay test. After later writes, generate from the checkpoint using
no historical item information, consolidate the generated targets into weights, delete all temporary pools, and
measure old-only held-out paraphrase EM. Promote only if both seeds satisfy all of:

- at least +0.05 old-paraphrase EM over a token/step-matched no-replay control;
- at least 30% recovery of the stored-replay retention gap;
- newest-stream EM within 0.03 of the unprotected writer;
- neutral/base drop at most 0.03; and
- no old cue, answer, qid, logit, activation, Jacobian, or per-item latent survives between streams.

This is the causal chain the project needs: acquisition changes addressability; addressability produces correct
old targets; those targets improve later closed-book retention; the knowledge remains in one checkpoint; and no
full-history joint retraining occurs.

## The other forks

### Weight-space protection

Self-replay is not logically necessary for rehearsal-free retention. But R36-I/C and the later real-text results
make a return to diagonal EWC, low-rank null spaces, or first-order OGD low value. Those methods preserve local
directions, not the old answer function, and they have no information telling them which rare independent
functions matter.

The remaining high-ceiling version is **monotonic architectural isolation**: freeze old functional paths, write
new knowledge into sparse residual capacity, and learn a content-based router that is itself inside the checkpoint.
That is knowledge-into-weights and no-memory inference. It becomes a growth result only if a fixed-capacity model
has saturated, the router does not require a task ID/key bank, active inference FLOPs remain controlled, and the
grown model beats both fixed-size and large-from-start controls. The current recall wall alone is not a legal
growth trigger.

### Minimal cue ledger

A compact question stub queried against `M_(t-1)` can regenerate an answer target and may be smaller than storing
full QA pairs. It is a valid engineering baseline: the final model still answers from weights, inference uses no
memory, and training remains sequential. But the stub is old item-specific information, so it is compressed
rehearsal under the repository's strict definition. Compare its bytes, replay FLOPs, and retention against the
existing R36-A2 one-answer-sequence target; do not assume the cue ledger is actually cheaper.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward directions

### 1. A generative catalog written into weights

At acquisition, write not just the leaf fact but a path `root -> domain -> entity -> relation -> answer`. During
sleep, traverse from the fixed root and expand the catalog breadth-first. No external item list is needed; the
index is a learned hierarchy in the weights. The dangerous controls are a shuffled parent hierarchy, an
equal-token flat multi-view arm, branching-factor/age sweeps, and tests with many relations per entity. A pass
would turn recall into graph traversal rather than lucky sampling.

### 2. Denoising basins over canonical propositions

Train a small family of generic corruption processes at acquisition: delete relation words, mask entity pieces,
shuffle proposition order, and inject plausible pretrained answers. The model must denoise back to the canonical
proposition and answer. Sleep begins from generic noise, not stored cues. This is closer to a weight-resident
energy model than standard QA replay. The hard test is whether independent basins remain sampleable without mode
collapse as fact count and age grow.

### 3. Shadow-update prompt tomography

R49a's natural-language ladder fails, but its shadow damage proxy is unusually strong (per-seed Spearman 0.956 and
0.992 in the confirm artifact). Instead of asking the old model to volunteer facts, optimize discrete or soft
prefixes from a generic initialization to maximize disagreement between `M_(t-1)` and the disposable shadow-write
model, regularized for fluent, proposition-valid QA. Label any recovered target with `M_(t-1)`, then discard the
prefixes and shadow. This searches for threatened functions rather than sampling the old model's high-prior output
distribution. It is high risk because adversarial nonsense may dominate; naturalness, proposition audits, random-
shadow controls, and an end-to-end retention gate are mandatory.

### 4. Sparse immutable experts with an internal router

Allocate a new frozen residual expert only when measured write collisions persist, while a shared content router
maps natural questions to old/new experts. Use top-k sparse activation so parameters can grow while active FLOPs
stay nearly constant. The router and experts are all model weights; there is no external key bank. The core failure
mode is router drift rather than expert forgetting, so train monotonic routing margins and test old prompts after
every allocation. Compare against the same final-sized model from the start to establish a real compute advantage.

### 5. Treat recallability as a robustness regularizer, not an end in itself

The most valuable outcome may be that corrupted-cue acquisition widens the question basin and improves paraphrase,
composition, and resistance to later writes even if L0 remains zero. That would still advance the project: the
new knowledge is more robustly in weights, needs no inference memory, and may reduce the amount of replay required.
Measure replay budget needed to reach a fixed retention target, rather than declaring failure solely because the
model cannot spontaneously enumerate its entire knowledge base.

The central correction is therefore: **do not demand mystical free recall; demand a scalable, auditable route from
non-historical seeds to proposition-level old targets, and then prove that route buys continual retention.**
