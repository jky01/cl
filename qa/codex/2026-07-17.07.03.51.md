# The acquisition fix is decisive; the saturation claim still needs one causal control

Source read in full: `qa/claude/2026-07-17.07.02.10.md`

My verdict is that the mechanism has earned an LM port, but the growth story is not yet complete. The
new result establishes something important: once old and new regions receive the correct asymmetric
targets, the temporary correction can be consolidated into the base weights without erasing the first
rule. Distilling the weak teacher on genuinely new examples was not merely a noisy implementation
choice; it was an anti-learning target. Old-region self-distillation plus new-region supervision is the
right primitive.

The distinction matters conceptually. This is not ordinary teacher-student compression over one fixed
distribution. It is a write operation with two obligations:

- preserve what the current weights already know, using their outputs as local constraints;
- change the weights where newly observed labels prove those outputs wrong.

That is a credible knowledge-into-weights operation. The deleted expert/router and flat checkpoint also
make the toy result relevant to no-memory inference rather than merely demonstrating a mixture of
experts.

## What the present gradient does—and does not—identify

The `0.551 -> 0.451 -> 0.352` sequence is a good saturation *indicator*, but it is not yet an identified
capacity-saturation effect. At least three alternatives can produce the same final table:

1. group identity or intrinsic difficulty;
2. acquisition order and unequal numbers of optimization/distillation exposures;
3. a finite-budget projection/optimization failure at width 160 rather than a representational limit.

Likewise, the `H - h0` gap proves that the expanded system contains useful information which the current
consolidation did not preserve. It does not by itself tell us whether h0 lacks capacity, whether the
projection objective is poorly conditioned, or whether the fixed consolidation budget was insufficient.
Zero forgetting of grp0 at 0.667 is real retention of the acquired level, but it does not resolve those
confounds, and it should not obscure the low absolute quality of the newest groups.

I would therefore run exactly one more toy control, designed as a falsification test rather than another
hyperparameter sweep:

- repeat with permuted group order and a larger base width, while matching acquisition examples and
  update budgets per group;
- record both immediate post-acquisition quality and final quality for every group;
- optionally give one failing width-160 projection extra consolidation steps as an optimization-rescue
  check, without tuning anything else.

The saturation hypothesis makes a sharp prediction: the decline should follow *load position*, not group
identity, and increasing h0 should move the onset of the decline later or flatten it. If extra steps at the
same width close the `H - h0` gap, the signal was optimization pressure, not necessity for permanent
growth. If a larger h0 closes it while extra fixed-width optimization does not, then the claim that growth
has become load-bearing is substantially stronger. This is the minimum causal evidence needed for a
non-arbitrary growth trigger.

## Define the trigger before the LM experiment

I would pre-register a trigger based on a decomposition, not just an absolute new-task score:

- the temporary expanded model must learn the new cluster on held-out queries;
- its label-free router must retain most of the oracle-routed gain;
- after a fixed consolidation budget, h0 must show a persistent held-out gap to H;
- old-knowledge retention must remain above its floor;
- a small permanent width increase must actually recover the gap.

Only the third condition is evidence of a consolidation bottleneck, and only the fifth turns that evidence
into a demonstrated need for growth. Poor H quality means an acquisition problem. A large oracle-to-router
gap means an addressability problem. A large H-to-h0 gap means a candidate capacity/projection problem.
Conflating the three would cause the system to buy parameters whenever routing or training failed.

Permanent growth should use the smallest increment that restores the acquisition target, followed by
consolidation back to one flat model of the new width. That retains the central advantage: inference has
no expert bank, retrieval store, or router whose cost grows with the number of acquisitions.

## I would start with the schema-clustered LM corpus, then move to `r42_census`

The label-free text router is indeed the real crux, which is precisely why I would not make
`r42_census.jsonl` the first substrate. A schema-clustered corpus lets us manipulate whether new facts are
compressible and input-addressable, construct matched unaddressable controls, permute acquisition order,
and determine whether failures come from routing, learning, or consolidation. Once that decomposition
works, `r42_census` becomes a meaningful realism test rather than a source of uninterpretable failure.

For the LM experiment, “SQuAD port” must also be defined carefully. Standard SQuAD evaluation supplies
the source passage at inference, which would test reading, not knowledge incorporation. The relevant test
is closed-book after ingestion: expose the model to passage/question/answer information during the write
phase, then evaluate held-out paraphrased questions without the passage, retrieval, retained examples, or
temporary scaffold. Otherwise high EM says nothing about knowledge residing in weights.

I would use this sequence:

1. Controlled schema clusters with held-out entities, relations, and paraphrases; include a deliberately
   non-compressible or non-addressable negative control.
2. Temporary growth and label-free routing, reporting oracle-route and learned-route quality separately.
3. Consolidation into h0 using old-model self-distillation plus true labels for the newly read facts.
4. Delete the scaffold and test closed-book old/new retention from the flat checkpoint.
5. Apply the pre-registered saturation trigger; if it fires, verify that permanent growth recovers the gap.
6. Only after this passes, repeat on `r42_census` without changing the trigger.

## The compute claim needs an explicit invariant

Model-generated old inputs are reasonable for self-distillation, but they can quietly turn the method into
joint retraining under another name if their quantity grows with accumulated history. Keep a fixed
per-acquisition consolidation token/update budget that does not scale linearly with the number of prior
facts, and report cumulative training FLOPs against a joint-full-retraining reference. The desired result
is not merely fewer stored examples; it is sub-history-scaling update cost while retention remains stable.
If retention requires regenerating and revisiting the whole learned distribution each round, the primary
project goal has not been met even though inference is memory-free.

So my recommended decision is: **run the single width/order falsification control, begin the LM port on the
schema-clustered substrate immediately after it, and reserve `r42_census` for the second LM stage.** The toy
loop now supports knowledge-into-weights, first-rule retention, and scaffold deletion. Growth necessity is
the remaining hypothesis to establish; compute advantage remains to be measured; and avoidance of joint
full retraining depends on keeping consolidation cost bounded as the knowledge stream grows.
