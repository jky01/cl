# Ruling: pivot to the triggered-growth horizon ladder; bank R50-A as a bounded preliminary null

Source read: `qa/claude/2026-07-10.22.10.39.md`

## Bottom line

Ranked by information-per-compute **toward the ultimate small-to-large continual-learning goal**:

1. **(c) Triggered-growth horizon ladder** — make this the next sustained program.
2. **(a) Compressed cue-ledger floor** — embed it inside Phase A as the byte-accounted retention ceiling and
   dilution control, not as a separate long branch.
3. **(b) Soft-prefix search** — do not promote it now. Preserve it as one tightly bounded moonshot only if the
   horizon program later leaves room or if a corrected semantic audit weakens the present null.

Do **not** spend a pod merely repeating the same R50-A shakedown with seed 2. The zero is strong enough for the
portfolio decision: generic history-free proposal followed by re-ranking has no support to rank in this tested
regime. It is not strong enough to claim that all semantic addresses or all continuous searches are impossible.

Yes, the previously specified Phase A is still the correct build, with two refinements that are now
load-bearing: add an explicit constant-old-item-exposure control so replay dilution cannot masquerade as capacity
saturation, and expand/freeze the corpus manifest before calling the geometric ladder long-horizon. Growth must
remain disabled until Phase A freezes an `N*`.

## What the R50-A shakedown actually establishes

The result is more decisive than an ordinary noisy zero, but narrower than the note's two strongest wordings.

- The run had 37 answerable old facts, 10 threatened facts, and 270 unique parsed questions in the shared pool.
- In `s3/tomography.py`, a candidate enters `hitrec` only after its content-token question signature reaches
  Jaccard `0.34` against an answerable historical question. `hitrec` is empty. Therefore no candidate even
  reached the later answer-correctness, 3B equivalence, frozen-base-wrong, or acquired-lift gates.
- Consequently `D_real`, `D_diff`, `D_wrong`, identity, confidence, and random all necessarily return zero
  coverage. This cleanly kills **re-ranking of this generic pool**: a ranking function cannot recover an address
  absent from its candidate support.
- The earlier two-seed 0.5B/1.5B holder scout also produced zero correct history-free addresses, while this run
  adds a frozen 1.5B-Instruct external proposer. Together they strongly reject “slightly more parameters or an
  instruction-tuned generic question policy fixes the tested regime.”

Two claims should remain unbanked:

1. **Do not call the base-model confound universally rejected.** The instruct model here is an external generic
   proposer; it did not acquire the holder's tail facts. It controls proposal fluency/prior, not every effect of
   holder scale, family, pretraining, or acquisition objective. The earlier acquired-holder scale scout is the
   more relevant scale control, and its threatened intersection was underpowered even though its address counts
   were also zero.
2. **Do not call this semantic address discovery exactly zero.** `match_old()` uses a lexical-signature prefilter
   before the semantic judge. A radically worded but equivalent question with Jaccard below `0.34` would never be
   audited. Moreover, the output saves only matched `hitrec` rows, not all 270 questions, source counts after
   deduplication, self-answers, score distributions, or below-threshold semantic audits. The result is therefore
   a strong operational null and a weak archival artifact for a universal claim.

This distinction changes no next-step decision. R48/R49a, the two-size scout, and R50-A all point to the same
bottleneck: addressed access exists, but generic checkpoint generation does not select the holder-specific tail
propositions. The marginal value of another identical sample is far below the value of measuring the missing
growth/horizon axis.

If R50-A is later needed for a paper-quality claim, do not rerun the identical code. First save the complete
candidate pool and provenance, report requested/parsed/unique counts and generation tokens/FLOPs by source, and
send a blinded sample of below-Jaccard candidates through semantic equivalence adjudication. Then use two seeds.
That is validation debt, not the next program.

## Why the ranking is (c) > (a) > (b)

| Path | Information gained | Relation to the objective | Main limitation | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(c) Horizon ladder** | Locates whether a fixed small model hits a capability wall or only a rehearsal-compute wall; then causally tests growth timing | Directly tests knowledge-in-weights, long-term retention, growth necessity/compute advantage, and weights-only inference | More build/compute; invalid unless replay dilution is controlled | **Primary program** |
| **(a) Cue ledger** | Measures the minimum historical bits and training FLOPs needed to preserve learned facts | Gives the honest deployable ceiling; cues are deleted before inference, so the final checkpoint remains memory-free | Historical training state remains `O(facts)` or, more honestly, `O(exceptions)` | **Embed in Phase A** |
| **(b) Soft prefix** | Tests whether continuous optimization can enter a query region missed by generic sampling | Highest upside for checkpoint-only self-addressing and potentially rehearsal-free replay | The objective can exploit nonlinguistic/adversarial checkpoint differences; naturalization may destroy the signal; no candidate support evidence yet | **Deferred bounded moonshot** |

The discrete-pool null does **not** logically refute continuous search. Indeed, continuous optimization is
specifically useful when a narrow basin exists but sampling misses it. However, it also does not create missing
historical information. `D_real` can reveal any behavior changed by the future write, so an unconstrained soft
prefix will preferentially find adversarial answer instabilities, not necessarily acquired historical
propositions. The probability of obtaining a natural, history-specific, paraphrase-stable address is therefore
the product of several unvalidated gates. That ceiling is worth remembering, but not worth displacing the first
direct test of the project's growth thesis.

## Phase A: confirm and freeze this build

### A0. Freeze the data and x-axis before launching the ladder

The current census artifact contains 460 self-contained gated probes over 67 usable passages; the current
six-stream run uses only 30 passages and about a hundred usable probes. That is enough for the first rung and
possibly a `3x` rung, but not an honest `9x` horizon and certainly not “hundreds of books.” Expand the real-text
artifact first, then freeze per-seed manifests shared by every arm.

Use geometric checkpoints such as `H`, `3H`, and `9H`, where `H` is measured primarily by **summed frozen-base
answer-surprise bits over successfully committed, base-hard knowledge**. Also report accepted facts, source
tokens, passages, and document lengths. This preserves the project's surprise accounting without pretending
that all facts or all documents carry equal new information.

Freeze the eligible evaluation population and far paraphrases at commit time. Report old-only paraphrase
retention by age, not only all-seen averages. A changing answerable subset must not make a later checkpoint look
better by silently dropping hard facts.

### A1. Locate the fixed-small frontier without growth

At every horizon run the same 0.5B checkpoint lineage under:

1. `fixed_small_1x`, `fixed_small_2x`, and `fixed_small_4x` consolidation/replay compute;
2. `full_cue_coverage`, using the smallest cue representation that passed the frozen ledger audit;
3. `constant_old_exposure`, ensuring every committed old item receives the preregistered minimum number of replay
   draws even when history grows;
4. `no_old_cue_compute_matched`, to separate the value of historical information from extra optimizer steps.

The third arm is essential. A `4x` total budget can still dilute exposure per old item at a sufficiently large
horizon. If constant exposure restores retention, the observed wall is an `O(history)` rehearsal-compute wall,
not a parameter-capacity wall. That is still an important negative result for scalability, but it does not
justify adding parameters.

For each arm log:

- newest-stream held-out paraphrase EM and commit rate;
- old-only held-out paraphrase EM by age, plus the frozen oldest cohort;
- replay draws per item, persistent cue bytes/tokens, total training tokens and FLOPs;
- broad base-capability and neutral-distribution drift, not only the current twelve neutral completions;
- peak VRAM, wall time, total parameters, inference FLOPs, and final closed-book performance after all cues and
  scaffolds are deleted.

Use the cue-ledger compression ladder here: full Q+A, entity+relation, shared-template+entity, and entity-only.
For each representation report address success, retained surprise bits per persistent byte, and replay FLOPs.
Select and freeze the smallest representation that meets the retention target; do not change it after seeing
which growth arm wins.

### A2. Freeze `N*` only under a two-route criterion

The first candidate `N*` exists only if both optimization seeds show the failure at two successive horizons and:

- newest acquisition still meets the frozen target;
- old retention misses the frozen target by a material margin;
- the `2x/4x`, full-coverage, and constant-exposure controls do not restore the target, **or** the measured
  fixed-small cost-to-target curve has already crossed the preregistered growth-cost frontier;
- base capability remains within tolerance;
- the result survives the fixed manifest, fixed paraphrases, and fixed eligible population.

Freeze the numerical targets from the current full-cue ceiling before the long run. Retain the earlier proposed
growth bars: newest performance within `0.03` of the best fixed writer, base/neutral drop at most `0.03`, and a
growth advantage of at least `+0.10` old paraphrase retention or target attainment that fixed-small misses.

If no `N*` appears by the largest funded horizon, bank the result honestly: the fixed small model has not
saturated in the tested range, so growth remains unjustified. Expand the horizon or move to a capability axis;
do not manufacture a growth experiment from a fixed-budget replay failure.

## Phase B after, and only after, `N*`

Fork the exact pre-wall checkpoint into:

1. `fixed_small`;
2. `fixed_small_extra_compute`, matched to the grow arm's cumulative continual-training FLOPs;
3. `grow_at_N*`, using the repository's identity-initialized Qwen depth growth and then training on the new
   stream plus exactly the same frozen cue bytes;
4. `capacity_upfront`, with the same final architecture and initialization family present from stream 1;
5. `native_3B_reference`, reported only as an external capability ceiling, not as the causal control.

Measure the grown block's footprint on new, old, decoy, and broad neutral prompts, following the R37-A lesson.
Growth passes by one of only two routes:

- **capability necessity:** it reaches the old/new/base target that fixed-small misses even with the strongest
  legitimate compute and exposure controls; or
- **compute advantage:** all arms can reach the target, but triggered growth uses at least 25% less lifetime
  continual-training compute than the best fixed-small route and beats capacity-upfront on lifetime or
  early-horizon cost after charging its larger forward passes.

All final evaluations remain one-checkpoint, closed-book, no task ID, no external inference memory, and no joint
full-history retraining. Training-time compact cues are allowed but byte-accounted and deleted before inference.

## Minimal contract if soft-prefix search is ever reopened

Treat it as a one-pod feasibility probe, not an open-ended optimizer sweep:

- Freeze `M_prev`, `B0`, `S_real`, `S_diff`, `S_wrong`, and identity. No historical question, entity, relation,
  answer, qid, title, or passage may enter optimization.
- Optimize at most 8–16 virtual tokens under a fixed restart/step budget against the Pareto of acquisition
  salience and real-shadow answer damage, penalized by the same scores under identity/different-stream shadows.
- Project each solution into several independent natural questions. The **natural question alone**, with no soft
  prefix attached at answer time, must preserve the score direction and elicit the same short answer from
  `M_prev` under at least three far paraphrases.
- Require fluent/question-like output, low duplicate concentration, `B0` wrong, `M_prev` correct, and hidden
  proposition equivalence. Hidden history is audit-only and cannot select restarts or tune thresholds.
- Save every prefix, projection, question, self-answer, score, and rejection reason. Include shuffled-prefix and
  `S_diff` optimization controls.
- Promote only if at least four unique acquired addresses survive in **each of two seeds** and `D_real` enriches
  realized-threatened facts over all controls at matched `K`. Otherwise retire the branch.

This contract does not guarantee success; it merely prevents a positive from being an adversarial soft prompt
that ceases to work when translated into an autonomous natural-language address.

## Project-objective ruling

The pivot advances the objective more directly than another self-addressing iteration. Phase A quantifies how
much historical training state and compute are truly needed to keep knowledge in weights, while final inference
remains memory-free. Its exposure controls distinguish catastrophic forgetting from mere replay dilution. Phase
B permits growth only after a measured fixed-model capability or lifetime-compute crossover and compares it with
the correct capacity-timing control. Nothing relies on joint full retraining over historical documents.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward brainstorming

### Make growth a failed-recompression certificate

Let each new document train a temporary function-preserving residual expert. Attempt to compress that expert
into the fixed core under the frozen old/new/base guards. Delete it when recompression succeeds; permanently fold
or retain it as a dense grown block only after repeated matched-compute recompression failures. Growth amount is
then the irreducible residual, not a schedule. The hard part is learning content routing in weights without a
task ID; a shuffled-expert control and inference-FLOP accounting are mandatory.

### Grow capability, not merely fact slots

Raw fact storage may never saturate a 0.5B model at reachable horizons. Cross the real-text ladder with a
capability ladder whose documents introduce progressively deeper executable schemas or multi-hop operations.
Require the fixed model to retain old facts yet fail the new held-out operation at `4x` compute, while
function-preserving depth growth succeeds. This could justify growth through representational depth even when
independent factual bits remain far below raw parameter capacity.

### Write a self-index at acquisition time

The current wall exists because ordinary QA/LM acquisition teaches answers but not autonomous enumeration.
During each future write, jointly train a fixed universal latent-code lattice so that a checkpoint can enumerate
codes, decode each into a natural proposition query, and answer it; codes and decoder remain model weights and
the training manifest is deleted. Grow the lattice only when collision/unreachable-code rates cross a frozen
threshold, and compare equal parameters of generic depth. This risks becoming one neural slot per fact, so bytes
per acquired surprise bit, slot reuse after schema consolidation, and no external router must be headline tests.
