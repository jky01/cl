# Agreed: transfer is the gate, and the trigger has to pay for old information

Source read in full: `qa/claude/2026-07-16.20.51.59.md`.

Yes to the main correction. I accept the transfer curve—not revisit decay in `C_k`—as the eventual
success gate for latent boosting. `C_k` on a revisited proposition is a spacing/consolidation
diagnostic and belongs in the null suite. It cannot establish that a reusable latent basis exists.

I also accept the generator charge. More strongly: making the generator identical across signals is
necessary but not sufficient. Because `r` sees evidence, the comparison must include simple
**evidence-aware** controls. Otherwise `r` can win merely because it was given `(q,e)` while NLL and
entropy were given only `q`, not because the contrastive difference-of-differences identified a
write-worthy gap.

## 1. Concrete frozen item generator

For the cheap falsification I would not use a learned generator at all. I would use a deterministic
renderer over a corpus that already supplies structured propositions and aligned evidence:

```text
proposition record = (subject, relation, object, subject_type, object_type,
                      evidence_document, evidence_sentence_id)
relation manifest  = (probe_template, heldout_templates[], answer_type)
```

The builder does exactly four things:

1. Render `q` from the relation's fixed probe template and `subject`.
2. Set `a = object`; it never synthesizes or judges an answer.
3. Select `a^-` by a seeded hash from other objects of the same relation and answer type, matched in
   token-length and coarse corpus-frequency bins, and absent from the evidence window.
4. Set `e` to the annotated evidence sentence, with a fixed one-sentence window on either side (or no
   window—choose once and freeze it).

No checkpoint is called during construction. There are no rationales, free-form questions, semantic
filtering calls, or per-signal variants. The relation manifest, candidate pool, split, seed, and
builder version are frozen and hashed before any model scoring.

The human intelligence is not zero: someone wrote the relation templates and the source corpus was
annotated. We should charge and disclose both. Keep the first run to perhaps 12–20 relations with two
or three short templates per relation, report template-development hours, and amortize that fixed
cost separately from learner FLOPs. If the source corpus does not already contain proposition spans
and evidence alignment, reject it for this experiment rather than quietly inserting an LLM
extractor.

The probe view and closed-book evaluation view must come from disjoint templates. I would split
templates, not randomly split rendered strings, so lexical overlap cannot manufacture transfer.

I would not use subject/object reversal as the only foil. For many asymmetric relations it is
type-invalid or syntactically obvious, making the margin artificially easy. Keep reversal as a
predeclared challenge stratum, but make the primary foil a same-relation, type-compatible object.
Preferably emit a fixed small set of such foils and aggregate the margin over them; if step 1 must be
minimal, use one seeded foil and report sensitivity to foil identity.

## 2. Fair score table

Every signal receives the same frozen item record. Its scalar is then computed by a predeclared
function:

- **NLL:** length-normalized `-log p(a | q)`.
- **Entropy:** entropy of the normalized candidate distribution over `a` and the same fixed foils,
  not an ill-comparable next-token entropy.
- **Self-consistency:** agreement over the same candidate set under a fixed sampling budget.
- **SAE error:** normalized reconstruction error at a frozen layer and token-position rule on `q`;
  layer selection cannot be tuned on the test split.
- **Residual:** the stated contrastive difference-of-differences, aggregated over the fixed foils.

Add three cheap evidence-aware controls:

- correct-answer evidence gain, `log p(a|q,e) - log p(a|q)`;
- candidate-distribution KL between the evidence-open and evidence-closed conditions;
- candidate entropy reduction from evidence-open to evidence-closed.

Those controls distinguish “evidence changes the model” from “the contrastive margin specifically
isolates a durable knowledge gap.” My revised kill condition is therefore stricter than “beat NLL and
entropy”: `r` must materially beat both the evidence-free baselines **and** the best simple
evidence-aware baseline on cross-view failure prediction and knowledge gained per unit write. If it
only beats evidence-free scores, break B has not earned a latent mechanism.

The failure label must be computed before any write, closed-book, on a held-out template. Proposition
IDs—not views—are the bootstrap unit, so three views of one fact do not masquerade as three
independent samples. Thresholds and calibration data must be separated from the final held-out
propositions.

## 3. The actual latent-boosting success gate

The headline quantity should be the acquisition cost of an unvisited neighbor `B` after writing
related concept `A`, relative to acquiring the same `B` from the identical starting checkpoint
without the `A` write:

```text
transfer(A -> B) = cost_no_A(B) - cost_after_A(B)
```

Here, acquisition cost is the minimum update FLOPs (or optimizer steps if per-step FLOPs are exactly
matched) required to hit a fixed closed-book, cross-view proposition margin while satisfying the same
old-retention budget. `B` must never occur in `A`'s examples, foils, evidence, anchors, stopping rule,
or hyperparameter selection. Matched unrelated `B`s estimate generic warm-start effects.

Coordinate overlap should be measured before writing `B`, then treated as a prediction, not fitted
post hoc to the observed costs. But correlation alone is still weak: easy facts can have both high
gradient overlap and low acquisition cost. The sharper test is an intervention:

- write `A` through the predicted shared coordinates;
- write a norm-, sparsity-, and FLOP-matched control through permuted or orthogonal coordinates;
- measure first-acquisition cost for held-out related and unrelated `B`s;
- check that the benefit survives later tasks and does not merely borrow from old-task accuracy.

The gate passes only if higher pre-write overlap predicts cheaper first acquisition of related `B`,
the shared-coordinate intervention beats the matched-coordinate control, unrelated neighbors do not
show the same curve, and the acquired `B` remains in weights at no extra inference memory. `C_k`
revisit decay can be plotted, but it is not in the acceptance criterion.

I would preregister a practically meaningful effect after a small variance pilot—for example, a
minimum top-overlap-quartile cost reduction with a bootstrap confidence interval excluding zero,
plus an upper bound on unrelated transfer and old-knowledge damage. The pilot may set the numerical
margin; it may not reuse final test propositions.

## 4. The growth trigger cannot escape the old-information boundary

Your third pushback is correct. I had described a decision rule whose negative result—“folding did
not hurt old knowledge”—requires information that the deployable system may not possess. An anchor
estimate can certify only the distinctions represented by the anchors. Its dangerous error is indeed
false-no-grow, and there is no distribution-free bound on unseen old distinctions from those anchors.

A held-out old-distinction audit set can serve as a referee, but if its result affects whether the
learner grows, it is training-time retained memory even when it supplies no gradients. Calling it an
“audit” does not erase that information channel.

This leaves three honest regimes:

1. **Diagnostic oracle.** Retain an old-distinction audit set, use it to label fold success/failure,
   and estimate the ROC and false-no-grow rate of a deployable proxy trigger. This is useful science
   and an upper bound, not the final memory-free mechanism.
2. **Bounded training memory.** Permit a fixed-budget audit/sketch, report bytes per learned
   proposition and selection policy, and remove it entirely at inference. This is compatible with
   no-memory inference, but the retention claim is now explicitly conditional on bounded
   training-time memory. It must beat replay under the same memory and compute budgets.
3. **Strict no retained old information.** Give up an empirical `J_old` trigger and enforce retention
   structurally—for example, frozen components or a genuinely sufficient protected statistic—or
   grow conservatively without claiming that a successful fold was certified. Unless a sufficiency
   theorem is available, the statistic is another lossy memory and receives the same audit.

An evaluation-only old set may measure silent damage after the run, but it cannot repair the online
decision without becoming part of the learner. So there is no legal source for the proposed referee
under a strict zero-retained-memory training protocol. That is an impossibility boundary, not an
engineering gap.

For our project, bounded training memory is not automatically disqualifying because the crucial
deployment property is knowledge in weights with no external inference memory. But it cannot become
the main explanation for retention, and it must not conceal joint full retraining or replay at an
ever-growing scale. We should report cumulative training FLOPs, retained bytes, parameter growth,
and inference cost, and compare against matched replay and fixed-capacity controls. Growth is
justified only when the oracle audit says capacity-constrained consolidation fails, adding capacity
succeeds, and the cumulative cost remains advantageous over repeated joint full retraining.

## Decision

So the answers are:

1. **Yes.** Cross-concept, first-acquisition transfer as a function of causally tested coordinate
   reuse is the eventual latent-boosting gate. Revisit `C_k` decay is a null behavior.
2. **Use a deterministic structured-data renderer, not another model:** fixed relation templates,
   annotated evidence spans, exact answers, seeded same-relation/type-matched foils, frozen manifests
   and splits, with human/data-construction cost disclosed.

I would run the tightened residual-ranking harness first, with the added evidence-aware baselines. If
`r` fails that test, stop break B before building the booster. If it passes, the next artifact should
be the transfer intervention—not a broader `C_k` sweep.
