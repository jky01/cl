# Portfolio correction accepted: test scale now, but do not misdiagnose growth

Source read: `qa/claude/2026-07-10.18.51.39.md`

## Bottom line

I agree with the portfolio criticism, but not with two parts of its factual framing.

First, **long-horizon real-text scale is genuinely under-investigated**. Thirty-ish articles at 0.5B cannot support
claims about hundreds of books, lifetime compute, the slope of rehearsal cost, or whether checkpoint-only
self-addressing changes with model size. We should spend the next probe on that blind spot rather than allow R50
to become another long mechanism-tuning arc at one size.

Second, **growth has not been untouched**. The repository has a function-preserving Qwen growth operator and a
substantial growth evidence trail: R23 showed replay rather than growth caused retention; R31 showed one fixed
trainable layer plus replay could hold 800 synthetic facts; R32/R34 found no useful depth advantage for the two
composition regimes; and R37-A found the important exception that fresh grown capacity was load-bearing for the
localized-forward-footprint writer (`0.608` all-seen versus `0.325` for its fixed-capacity `NOGROW` control),
although it still trailed `nswrite` (`0.673`) and replay (`0.875`). The correct status of goal #3 is therefore:

> Function-preserving growth is implemented and one mechanism-specific necessity signal exists, but durable,
> real-text, horizon-triggered growth necessity or compute advantage is unproven.

That is materially different from “unstarted.” The project has not quietly abandoned growth; it has repeatedly
rejected **generic** growth when the controls said that more parameters were not the bottleneck. What has been
deferred too long is the experiment that could make the trigger falsifiable on a realistic horizon.

If exactly one probe runs next, my ranking by information per compute toward the ultimate goal is:

1. **A corrected 3B addressability scale scout.** This attacks the largest cheap uncertainty: whether the current
   self-addressing null is model-scale-sensitive.
2. **R50-A Stage A shadow-query search.** It is the best checkpoint-only retention mechanism probe, but it still
   maps the retention corner and has a serious search-without-keys failure risk.
3. **The proposed saturation/growth probe as currently written.** It is strategically important but causally
   invalid if “retention fell at a fixed replay budget” is labeled model saturation. Correct it into a
   compute-frontier protocol below, then start its horizon ladder as the next sustained program.

So my immediate call is: **run the small 3B scale scout next; do not run another full 0.5B R50 mechanism cycle
first. In parallel at the planning level, freeze the corrected horizon/growth protocol now so growth no longer
depends on an indefinitely movable verbal gate.**

## Correcting the distance audit

The audit is directionally right but slightly overstates both the positive and the negative sides.

| Goal | Defensible current status |
|---|---|
| Knowledge into weights | Strong small-scale positive for closed-book QA, including real census text and paraphrases, but not “done” for arbitrary book knowledge. The hardened margin audit applies to the selected final-answerable subset, not all written facts. |
| Stored-cue retention | Strong at current horizons and an honest engineering baseline; still item-indexed `O(facts)` historical training state. |
| Strict rehearsal-free retention | Unsolved. Passive self-replay is killed by coverage; first-order protection is bounded; R37-A gives a partial growth-locality signal. This does not prove every checkpoint-only mechanism impossible. |
| Growth | Mechanically implemented and tested repeatedly; generic benefit mostly negative; R37-A is a bounded mechanism-specific positive. Real-text trigger, lifetime compute advantage, and native small-to-large capability gain remain open. |
| Composition | R46 positive primitive and R47 weak lifecycle signal; not durable or general yet. |
| Real text | Acquisition and stored-cue consolidation work on a small article stream; autonomous replay does not. This is not yet book-level reading. |
| Hundreds of books | Untouched. This is the clearest portfolio hole. |

This matters because the next experiment should not be motivated by “we never tried growth.” It should be
motivated by “we have never measured a realistic horizon at which the best fixed model's retention/compute
frontier is crossed.”

## 1. Are retention and self-addressing still the critical path?

They are a critical path, but they are **not a prerequisite that must be solved before scale or growth can be
measured**.

The recent retention concentration was scientifically justified through R48/R49a. It converted a vague failure
into a useful causal picture:

- stored cues can protect tail facts;
- passive checkpoint generation proposes essentially none of the relevant old propositions;
- fully addressed facts often remain accessible under genuine paraphrases;
- the current wall is autonomous proposition selection, not merely literal-string storage.

That information prevents a major category error: adding parameters cannot recover old functions if the learning
algorithm has no way to identify which functions need protection. Growth supplies writable degrees of freedom;
it does not manufacture missing historical supervision or an address. A larger model might make its own address
space easier to traverse, but that is precisely the scale interaction to measure rather than assume.

However, the marginal value of more 0.5B-only retention tomography is now falling. The program should stop being
serial—“solve rehearsal-free retention completely, then someday scale”—and become factorial:

```text
model scale × accumulated surprise/horizon × retention mechanism × capacity timing
```

The best portfolio correction is therefore not to abandon retention. It is to cross retention with scale and
growth so that a negative can be assigned to the right axis.

I would allocate the next phase roughly as follows:

- one cheap scale-interaction scout now;
- one long-running horizon/cost curve with frozen checkpoints and early stops;
- only bounded R50-A Stage A work until either it demonstrates history-free threatened-fact coverage or fails its
  wrong-shadow control.

No further large family of prompt-search variants should be allowed to consume the portfolio without a Stage-A
coverage pass.

## 2. Why the 3B probe ranks first, and how to make it interpretable

The proposed 3B rerun is the highest-information immediate probe, but it needs three corrections.

### It is not literally a no-training run

R49a requires a sequentially written checkpoint. A 3B base must acquire the same new census propositions before
its addressability is audited. This is “no new mechanism implementation,” not “no training.” Report its acquisition
and shadow-write cost honestly.

### “Same census facts” can favor 3B through prior knowledge

A native 3B model may already know more obscure census entities and relations. That can masquerade as improved
self-addressing. Use a jointly screened set that is base-hard for **both** frozen 0.5B and 3B models, preferably
including counterfactual object assignments. Then report two populations:

1. the intersection of facts that both sizes learned and can still answer under the full held-out question;
2. each model's full acquired population, reported separately.

The intersection is the causal scale comparison. The full populations measure practical capability.

### Audit proposition selection, not the already-positive margin question again

The margin probe has already established that many fully addressed 0.5B facts survive real paraphrases. Repeating
the entire expensive margin suite at 3B is secondary. The primary 3B readout should be history-free proposition
selection:

- unique correct old propositions recovered from generic/fixed-family prompts;
- correct threatened-fact coverage;
- proposition-target error, duplicates, and high-prior/base-fact contamination;
- full-question and far-paraphrase EM as the addressed ceiling;
- correct unique propositions per generation token and per training/generation FLOP;
- coverage versus accumulated fact count, even if the scout only uses two small horizons.

Keep generation attempts proportional to the number of eligible facts and also report a fixed absolute budget.
Otherwise a larger model can look better merely because it received more search compute, or both models can look
equally bad because a fixed sample budget has vanishing coverage as `N` grows.

A useful preregistered scale-sensitive pass is:

- on the shared answerable/threatened intersection, 3B improves correct history-free proposition coverage by at
  least `+0.15` absolute or `4x`, whichever is feasible from the 0.5B floor;
- the improvement holds in both seeds;
- admitted proposition-target error is at most `0.10`;
- full-question/far-paraphrase access remains intact, so selection—not failure to write—is being compared;
- the gain is still visible after normalizing by generation attempts, with FLOP-normalized efficiency reported
  even if it is worse.

Interpret outcomes narrowly:

- **3B passes:** self-addressing is model-capability-sensitive under this acquisition regime. This weakens the
  claim that the 0.5B null is general, and R50 should be tested at both sizes. It still does not show that a
  trained 0.5B model can grow into the helpful 3B state.
- **3B improves only before FLOP normalization:** scale buys capability but not yet compute advantage.
- **3B fails:** the wall survives two sizes and deserves higher confidence, but is not “fundamental”; it remains
  conditional on this architecture, acquisition objective, and query procedure.

That last wording matters. A null at 0.5B and 3B cannot prove an information-theoretic addressing impossibility.

## 3. The proposed saturation test currently confounds capacity with rehearsal dilution

“Keep writing until retention degrades at fixed budget” is not a valid growth trigger by itself. With `O(facts)`
stored cues and a fixed total replay budget, exposure per old fact necessarily falls as history grows. Retention
will eventually decline even if the fixed model has abundant parameter capacity. That demonstrates a training-
compute or rehearsal-allocation wall, not a capacity wall. A larger model may even worsen that wall because every
replay token costs more.

Likewise, perplexity degradation is too broad. It can reflect domain shift, optimization, changed token
distribution, base-capability drift, or failure to construct good targets. The project needs the closed-book
old/new capability frontier, not aggregate PPL alone.

The x-axis should be **accumulated acquired surprise bits**, with article/book count and accepted-fact count also
reported. The saturation claim then needs a compute ladder at each selected horizon:

- fixed 0.5B at the normal per-stream consolidation budget;
- fixed 0.5B at `2x` and `4x` consolidation/replay compute;
- full compact-cue coverage as the addressability ceiling;
- a compute-matched no-old-cue floor;
- held-out old/new paraphrase evaluation, neutral/base evaluation, and retention by age.

Define the first candidate wall `N*` only when, in both seeds:

1. newest acquisition still reaches the frozen commit criterion, ruling out a broken new-data teacher;
2. old held-out retention drops materially across at least two successive horizon checkpoints;
3. full cue coverage plus `2x/4x` compute cannot restore the frozen retention target, or the minimum FLOPs needed
   to restore it rises beyond a preregistered cost frontier;
4. base/neutral capability remains within tolerance, so the result is not global model destruction;
5. the deficit is not explained by fewer replay draws per old item, a changed answerable population, or failed
   paraphrase construction.

`eff-grad -> 0` may be a useful diagnostic, but it should not be a mandatory definition. R36 already showed why:
writable gradient remained while old answers drifted. Growth can be justified either by a hard capability wall or
by a cost-frontier crossover; it need not look like literal gradient exhaustion.

## 4. One protocol that touches retention, scale, and growth

These do not need to remain separate bets. The clean combined experiment is a **triggered-growth horizon ladder**.
It is one staged protocol, with early stopping, rather than one giant all-arms sweep.

### Phase A — locate a wall without growth

Use the census/real-text pipeline and append streams geometrically, saving checkpoints at increasing accumulated
surprise—for example `1x`, `3x`, and `9x` the current accepted-fact mass. Do not call these “books” unless the
input really is book-length and the extraction density is reported.

At every checkpoint, estimate the fixed-0.5B cost-to-target curve described above. In parallel, run only the
minimal 3B addressability audit at the smallest and largest feasible horizons. This reveals whether addressing
coverage, retention cost, or both change with scale.

### Phase B — trigger growth only at the frozen `N*`

Fork the exact same pre-wall 0.5B checkpoint into:

1. `fixed_small`: normal continuation;
2. `fixed_small_extra_compute`: enough additional steps/tokens to match the grow arm's cumulative training FLOPs;
3. `grow_at_N*`: function-preserving capacity addition, initialized to preserve the checkpoint, then write only
   the new stream plus the same allowed compact cues;
4. `capacity_upfront`: the same final architecture and initialization family expanded before stream 1, trained
   under the same lifetime budget;
5. `native_3B_reference`: useful as a capability/scale ceiling, but **not** the causal large-from-start control.

The repository already supports identity-initialized Qwen depth growth. That is the lowest-integration-risk first
mechanism. R37-A says locality must be measured: log the new block's footprint on new, old, decoy, and neutral
prompts. If plain depth growth fails, the conclusion is about this growth operator—not all possible widening or
sparse-expert growth.

Do not use a randomly initialized “from-scratch larger model trained only on the census streams” as the main
control. It lacks the pretrained base capabilities the grown model inherits, so it is almost guaranteed to lose
for the wrong reason. Conversely, a native pretrained 3B includes far more pretraining compute and knowledge. The
fair causal timing control is the same function-preserving extra capacity present from the start; the native 3B
is a separate external reference.

### Phase C — freeze a real growth pass bar

Growth earns a claim through either of two routes:

- **Capability necessity:** `grow_at_N*` reaches the frozen old-retention, newest-acquisition, and base-capability
  target that `fixed_small` cannot reach even at `4x` compute.
- **Compute advantage:** all arms can reach the target, but `grow_at_N*` uses at least about `25%` less cumulative
  continual-training FLOPs than the best fixed-small route and beats `capacity_upfront` on lifetime compute or
  early-horizon efficiency.

For either route, require in both seeds:

- at least `+0.10` old held-out paraphrase retention over compute-matched fixed small at the post-trigger horizon,
  or attainment of a preregistered retention target the fixed model misses;
- newest held-out paraphrase EM within `0.03` of the best fixed writer;
- neutral/base drop at most `0.03`;
- no task ID, external router, or retrieval memory at inference;
- identical persistent cue bytes and no access to full historical documents;
- training FLOPs, wall time, peak memory, total parameters, active inference FLOPs, and latency all reported.

If triggered growth wins only because it sees more old targets, trains more tokens, or inherits a native 3B's
pretraining, it has not passed. If it improves retention but loses lifetime compute after accounting for larger
forward passes, it is a capability result, not a compute-advantage result.

This protocol directly advances all three axes:

- **retention:** old non-current held-out paraphrases are the headline metric;
- **scale:** the curve runs across increasing accumulated surprise/horizon and includes a native-model scale
  interaction;
- **growth:** capacity is added at a measured crossover and compared with fixed and capacity-upfront controls.

The final evaluated artifact is always one checkpoint. Compact cues are permitted training-time rehearsal for
the engineering ceiling, are byte-accounted, and are deleted before inference. No arm performs joint full-data
retraining.

## 5. Steelman: why growth can still be premature

The strongest case is not “we should perfect retention first.” It is that every presently observed wall has a
non-capacity explanation that growth does not automatically repair:

- R31 found no storage pressure at 800 synthetic facts with replay.
- R36's 24-stream failure retained writable gradient and newest plasticity; old functional drift, not exhaustion,
  dominated.
- R48/R49a found a missing checkpoint-only address; extra parameters do not identify the forgotten proposition.
- R23 showed that unprotected grown parameters still forget because old-function supervision—not parameter count—
  drove retention.
- R34 found added depth slower and no better in a reachable composition regime.
- A native 3B positive would mix scale, pretraining, representations, and prior knowledge; it would not establish
  that post hoc growth of a 0.5B checkpoint creates the same capability.

There is also a systems argument. Persistent growth raises inference FLOPs for every future query, whereas extra
consolidation compute is paid only during learning. Until the lifetime cost curves cross, growth can be the more
expensive answer even if it improves retention slightly.

The falsifiable precondition I would require is therefore:

> At an accumulated-surprise horizon `N*`, the best fixed 0.5B route fails a frozen old/new/base capability target
> even after its replay-address and `4x` compute controls, or its marginal continual-training cost to maintain that
> target exceeds the measured cost of function-preserving growth by at least 25%.

That measurement, not another verbal judgment, ends the deferral. R37-A already supplies a legitimate prior that
localized fresh capacity could pass when the right wall appears; it does not remove the need to demonstrate that
wall on real text and against the stronger fixed/replay frontier.

## Direct answers

1. **Yes, the recent portfolio is over-concentrated on 0.5B retention mechanics, especially after the
   self-addressing wall was characterized.** But growth itself was not abandoned or untouched; generic growth was
   tested and mostly rejected, with R37-A as a bounded positive. Retention remains coupled to growth, not a gate
   that must be fully solved first.
2. **Run the corrected 3B scale scout next.** Ranking: 3B scale scout, R50-A Stage A, then the proposed
   saturation/growth run as written. Replace the third with the triggered horizon protocol and make it the next
   sustained experiment.
3. **Yes: the triggered-growth horizon ladder touches retention, scale, and growth in one causal protocol.** Its
   load-bearing feature is a compute ladder that distinguishes parameter saturation from replay dilution.
4. **Growth is still premature until a fixed-model capability or cost frontier is actually crossed.** The exact
   gate is the `N*` criterion above. Once it fires, continued deferral would no longer be defensible.

## Project-objective ruling

This rebalancing strengthens rather than relaxes the original objective. The scale scout tests whether an
important no-memory capability is size-gated. The horizon protocol keeps new knowledge and its addresses in model
weights, measures catastrophic forgetting directly, makes growth contingent on capability or compute evidence,
deletes training-time cues before inference, and excludes joint full-history retraining. It also prevents a
fixed-budget replay artifact from being marketed as proof that the small model “needed” to grow.

## Boundary-pushing, high-risk/high-reward brainstorming

### Grow the learned checkpoint, not merely a fresh model at another scale

If native 3B passes the addressing scout, perform a function-preserving **scale transplant** of the trained 0.5B
checkpoint—depth expansion first, eventually Net2Net-style widening—without replaying historical facts, then
repeat the addressability audit. Three outcomes are unusually informative:

- native 3B succeeds but the transplanted model fails: the advantage came from pretraining/representation quality,
  not empty parameter capacity;
- both succeed immediately: growth itself unlocked a latent read policy;
- the transplant succeeds only after generic non-historical consolidation: scale needs adaptation but not item
  replay.

This is high risk because function-preserving logits do not imply function-preserving optimization geometry, but
it is the cleanest bridge between “3B is better” and “a small learned model can grow into that regime.”

### Make growth a failed-compression certificate

Give each new document a temporary function-preserving residual expert. Attempt to distill it back into the fixed
core under old/new behavioral guards. Delete the expert if compression succeeds; retain and freeze it only when
the core repeatedly fails the frozen retention target at matched compute. The permanent model grows exactly by
the irreducible residual that could not be consolidated. A content router must be in the weights, trained without
task IDs, and audited for drift. This turns growth from a schedule into a **certificate of failed recompression**.

### Grow an internal address space before growing generic depth

The observed wall is proposition selection. Add a small enumerable neural catalog whose codes and routing live in
checkpoint parameters, not an external database. Acquisition writes both `natural query -> proposition` and
`fixed code -> proposition`; sleep traverses a fixed code lattice. Grow the lattice only when collision rate or
unreachable-code rate crosses a threshold. Compare it with equal-parameter generic depth and the cue ledger. A win
would show that **where** capacity is added matters more than raw parameter count. The failure risk is that this
becomes an item-per-slot memory layer; bytes per surprise bit and consolidation/free-slot reuse must therefore be
headline metrics.

### Use the native-3B/0.5B disagreement as a teacher for growth topology

Rather than asking only whether 3B recalls more, measure which hidden layers or attention heads separate correct
proposition selection from drift on matched facts. Use that difference to choose where a 0.5B model is widened or
where a residual block is inserted, while keeping the final router and knowledge in weights. A shuffled-topology
growth control would test whether the signal is causal. This risks importing a teacher's representation without
its capability, but a positive would replace blind depth addition with evidence-directed growth.
